How it was made

  1. Plates stay unaltered archival clips; they may be slowed, retimed or cropped, never painted.
  2. Every pose is keyed by hand in code: key poses, contacts, timing.
  3. Each review agent describes what it sees before it reads what was intended.

What this case teaches

  1. Blind first, then intent, separates two questions: does it read, and does it read as the sign? Round 4: 17 read fully, but only 13 matched intent.
  2. Round 2 produced a shared integration standard (contact shadow, cast shadow, tone mapping, grain) that every passage then carries.
  3. The record says what has not been reviewed. That list is as much a part of the work as the verdicts.

How it differs. The atlas asked a machine to place Bola. Here a person places her every time, and the critic process is the main tool.

What broke and what changed

  • Props too small to read: a footprint, crumbs, copies.Props drawn larger and held longer.
  • Shadows pointing the wrong way.Shadow direction taken from the plate's key light.
  • Cause after effect: stamps, a dive, a jump.Retimed so the action lands before the response.
  • Figure too small in the frame.Push-ins on seven passages.
