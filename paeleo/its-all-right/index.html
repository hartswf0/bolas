IT’S ALL RIGHT

come here · it’s all right · come in

A film by Watson Hartsoe · PAE-02 · Bolas · 2026

A person speaks to a dog in three sentences. The order, come here, fails: the dog backs away and turns its back. Then the person sits, opens the door, lowers a hand and says it’s all right, and the dog’s head comes round. Come in is said only once the dog has reached the sill. It looks in, and stays.

For programmers

“Come here” drives a dog away. “It’s all right” turns its head. “Come in” brings it to the door, and no further. A film about what words can do.

Dusk, a porch, a doorway. A person calls a dog: Come here. It backs away. Called again and reached for, it turns its back. Then the person stops asking: they sit, the door opens, a hand is offered low. It’s all right, softly, and the dog’s head comes round. It comes, lies across the sill, hears come in, looks inside, and stays.

What the dog hears

The dog’s decision machine reads no words. It hears a call, a soft voice or silence, and it sees what the body does: the reach, the sitting down, the open door, the low hand. The sentences say aloud what the body is already saying.

One score, many languages

Every telling keeps the same score, frame for frame, so every dog makes each choice at the same instant. Each was judged by independent critics from the picture and sound alone. Two are marked paused: their repairs stopped after five rounds, and they stay on the record.
