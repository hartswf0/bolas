Lineage: Un homme qui dort · Georges Perec and Bernard Queysanne · 1974 release.

## 2 · Creative thesis

Make a spectator feel personally addressed while an anonymous figure stays available to observation. The proof hinges on one interval in which the voice’s address and the image’s point of view become perceptibly separate.

State the defining beat and a comparison baseline before allocating large work. Specify what the viewer or reader should be able to point to when that beat succeeds.

## 4 · Invariants and concurrency

Keep narrative address in the second person in the constrained language. Quoted diegetic speech lives on its own track. Preserve the declared addressee until a marked shift. Keep a voice-free passage long enough for the spectator to notice the body or place on its own.

Image and narration teams fork from a common beat ledger. They can work in parallel while each time span keeps its event ID. Moving a voice onset across a key silence or an edit across an address boundary invalidates the other track’s review. At 100×, align to beat IDs and flag distinct relation patterns before screenings.

Work from immutable source IDs and a known revision. Each candidate records owner, base, read and write sets, changed fields, relevant tests, artifact, and acceptance condition. Integrate through an expected-head check or the available versioned equivalent. Validate combined effects before merging. Treat retries idempotently and preserve noncommuting alternatives as named branches. On a broken hard invariant, quarantine the affected candidate, resume from the last accepted state, and record the evidence.

## 5 · Swarm assignments

A voice writer produces clauses and referent tags. A camera or drawing team makes a quiet observational cut. A sound editor controls distance, breath, and withheld cues. A referent checker audits the transcript. An independent viewer reports when ‘you’ lands on character, audience, or both.

Use real agents when available. Combine coupled tasks under one owner and fork independent candidates against the same input revision. Each assignment has a specific playable, readable, or otherwise inspectable output and a bounded budget. If separate review is unavailable, execute the checks you can and record which judgments remain pending.

## 6 · Hard proof

Build 35 seconds: a person wakes, ignores a ringing telephone, and takes an identical staircase twice. Keep the camera outside their viewpoint. Record two original second-person tracks with identical words, one ahead of actions and one delayed. Leave a held shot with only room sound.

Produce the smallest working artifact that makes the formal claim testable. For screen work use actual playback, purposeful sound or a declared silence, and a saved assembly. For page work provide the complete readable sequence and any route, physical action, or viewing condition the proof needs.

## 7 · Verification fixture and independent critique

An address clause like ‘You hear it through the wall’ passes with an explicit addressee and interval. A narrator’s shift to ‘He hears it’ fails the grammar gate. The same words can validly produce anticipatory and retrospective timelines.

Have a fresh viewer watch once at normal speed and mark when the voice feels addressed to them. Ask what the body does while the voice is silent and where narration merely duplicates visible behavior.

Give a fresh critic the brief, real output, and stable acceptance bar. Review in the intended reading or viewing condition before diagnostics. Return verdict, exact evidence, largest gap, falsifiable acceptance condition, and what to protect. Label evidence measured, structurally verified, perceptually judged, or unknown.

## 8 · Repair and recursive inquiry

Compare a single onset shift while freezing voice delivery, then hold onsets and change camera distance. Use the contrast to locate whether referent instability comes from timing or visual address. Preserve unease that follows the declared relationship.

Transfer the grammar to a first-person game camera as a separate experiment and test whether camera and addressee collapse into the same role.

After two meaningfully different repairs fail, state two explanations and run the smallest comparison that can decide between them. Record the result against its exact revision and recheck the whole work. Retain the strongest accepted checkpoint. Stop when the quality bar is met, the operator stops, the budget is used, or remaining work cannot resolve the failure in scope.

## 9 · Deliverable and start

A playable study, picture timeline, two aligned voice takes, clause/referent ledger, timed silence map, and a review locating the strongest change in address.

Link the output and evidence to their revisions. Expose the current preview, decisive review, and remaining failure in a readable status view when a run warrants it. Start now with the creative thesis, theory, and hard proof. Report what was actually built and inspected.

## 10 · Optional common seed

A visitor arrives at an empty station carrying a sealed envelope. A bell rings. The visitor opens the envelope, finds a brass key, leaves it on the bench, and walks away. The service door stays closed.

Address the visitor as ‘you’ while observing them from across the concourse. Test placing ‘You hear the bell’ immediately before and after the bell. Keep the same observed actions.
