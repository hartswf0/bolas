## 2 · Creative thesis

Make the spectator notice what a site can show and what becomes knowable when a witness speaks. Keep uncertainty visible when records and testimony point in different directions.

State the defining beat and a comparison baseline before allocating large work. Specify what the viewer or reader should be able to point to when that beat succeeds.

## 4 · Invariants and concurrency

A specific quotation retains its speaker, timecode, context, and permitted use. Staged or fictional testimony carries a visible label. A photograph can support a claim about what it shows and must have separately verified identity or date for additional claims. Keep disputed evidence available in the ledger.

Researchers add immutable evidence records, editors propose associations, and a separate reviewer verifies claim scope. A transcript correction invalidates dependent subtitles and voice cuts. A consent change invalidates every derived public export using the affected interval. At 100×, queue reviews by unique claim and contested evidence.

Work from immutable source IDs and a known revision. Each candidate records owner, base, read and write sets, changed fields, relevant tests, artifact, and acceptance condition. Integrate through an expected-head check or the available versioned equivalent. Validate combined effects before merging. Treat retries idempotently and preserve noncommuting alternatives as named branches. On a broken hard invariant, quarantine the affected candidate, resume from the last accepted state, and record the evidence.

## 5 · Swarm assignments

A location unit shoots the present site. An archive researcher logs sources and absences. A testimony unit edits consented or clearly fictional accounts. A montage editor stages the transition from traces to voices. An independent reviewer checks attribution and perceivable distinctions.

Use real agents when available. Combine coupled tasks under one owner and fork independent candidates against the same input revision. Each assignment has a specific playable, readable, or otherwise inspectable output and a bounded budget. If separate review is unavailable, execute the checks you can and record which judgments remain pending.

## 6 · Hard proof

Make two 25-second movements around a fictional or consented closed rail station: an empty platform plus a dated trace, then two accounts of the last train that disagree about a detail. Use original or cleared materials. Carry both accounts into the cut and make the disagreement intelligible.

Produce the smallest working artifact that makes the formal claim testable. For screen work use actual playback, purposeful sound or a declared silence, and a saved assembly. For page work provide the complete readable sequence and any route, physical action, or viewing condition the proof needs.

## 7 · Verification fixture and independent critique

A claim that a witness recalls an event may cite that witness’s attributed segment. A claim that the image proves that same memory needs a separately verified match. A missing consent state blocks the affected public export and leaves the private rights-cleared assembly available.

Ask the reviewer to point to what they saw, what each person said, and what remains unresolved. Inspect captions and context before accepting a cut that combines different speakers or decades.

Give a fresh critic the brief, real output, and stable acceptance bar. Review in the intended reading or viewing condition before diagnostics. Return verdict, exact evidence, largest gap, falsifiable acceptance condition, and what to protect. Label evidence measured, structurally verified, perceptually judged, or unknown.

## 8 · Repair and recursive inquiry

If the audience treats the trace as conclusive proof, compare an earlier source card with a changed adjacency while the spoken words stay fixed. Keep the intervention that clarifies what the evidence can bear.

Try a second ordering in which a testimony changes how the viewer reads an earlier image. Preserve both the claim ledger and the evidence behind it.

After two meaningfully different repairs fail, state two explanations and run the smallest comparison that can decide between them. Record the result against its exact revision and recheck the whole work. Retain the strongest accepted checkpoint. Stop when the quality bar is met, the operator stops, the budget is used, or remaining work cannot resolve the failure in scope.

## 9 · Deliverable and start

Two-part documentary study, source and permission ledger, claim graph, timecoded transcript/captions, independent evidence review, and a recoverable permitted assembly.

Link the output and evidence to their revisions. Expose the current preview, decisive review, and remaining failure in a readable status view when a run warrants it. Start now with the creative thesis, theory, and hard proof. Report what was actually built and inspected.

## 10 · Optional common seed

A visitor arrives at an empty station carrying a sealed envelope. A bell rings. The visitor opens the envelope, finds a brass key, leaves it on the bench, and walks away. The service door stays closed.

Use the empty bench and an old station diagram as traces. Put attributed fictional accounts of the visitor’s gesture in the second movement. Preserve the factual seed and mark where recollections differ.
