OULIPO FILMS

Proof films made by swarmsforaged or handmade, never faked

Each film tests one constraint from the Oulipo, Oucipo or Oubapo.

Every film was made by its own swarm of agents from the work's SCION prompt. Each swarm had a director, units that forage archives or make the material by hand, an editor, blind readers, an independent critic and a rights reviewer. The films loop a silent excerpt as you scroll; press the button to watch the whole film with sound.

Not yet passedOucipo and Raymond Queneau · c. 1974

La Machine à tuer le temps

Fixed shot pool. Make different films from one locked inventory of shots.

How it was made · 12 foraged sources

A drawn red spiral notebook, animated frame by frame, holds eight photographs Jack Delano took at Chicago's Union Station in 1943, foraged from the Library of Congress (FSA/OWI, no known restrictions) and frozen as one hashed pool. Cut A plays them in pool order; the prints are lifted, re-slotted and re-numbered in red pencil, and cut B plays the same prints reordered and retimed. Room air (Wreckless, 1935) and a whistle (Valley Town, 1940) are public-domain Prelinger audio via movingimagearchive.com. Captions, story B, the station crowd, tick and foley are invented or synthesised; no station recording could be fetched, and no fresh viewer has screened it. Review: the rights reviewer passed it; the critic did not. No fresh viewer has tested whether the second order reads as an illicit handoff, the porter's frontal portrait reads as a portrait rather than a look-out, and the notebook's paper, cover and handwriting are generated in code rather than photographed.

Not yet passedGeorges Perec and Bernard Queysanne · 1974 release

Un homme qui dort

Second-person voice. Let a voice say “you” while the image observes someone else.

How it was made · 8 foraged sources

One 35-second picture is shown twice with the same five second-person captions, first ahead of each action, then behind it. Foraged: Library of Congress FSA/OWI photographs, 1935-43, public domain: Esther Bubley's boardinghouse man (probably the same sitter in two negatives) lifting his head off his hand and refusing a call, Russell Lee's wall telephone, Carl Mydans's stairwell. Sounds from Wikimedia Commons (CC BY): a mine-telephone bell, wooden stairs, library room tone. Handmade: the cutout animation (figures cut from the negatives and moved; his climb pieced from a seated photograph, flopped), some filled-in background, the captions and cards. Review: did not pass after three rounds. The seated close-ups match the reference photograph, but the stair climb reads as a cut-out sliding behind the rail, and the opening shot sits outside the reference's pale-wall, hard-shadow look.

Passed reviewRobert Bober and Georges Perec · 1980

Récits d’Ellis Island

Traces and testimony. Give a place, its records, and its witnesses distinct voices.

How it was made · 8 foraged sources

Two 25-second movements. Movement I is foraged from Wikimedia Commons: Daniel Jolivet's photographs of the disused halt at Bourré, dated by EXIF (CC BY 2.0), and a 1904 Orléans railway map from the BnF (public domain). Source cards only, no voices. Movement II puts two invented witnesses over the same platform crop. Their accounts are handmade: typeset testimony records and labelled captions. The same foraged bell and keys recordings (CC BY-SA, CC BY) are heard in opposite orders. Méliès's 1896 Vincennes train is labelled as another station. There is no spoken voice and no room recording; silences are declared. A handmade ledger leaves the bell order unresolved.

Nearly passedLouis Malle, adapting Raymond Queneau · 1960

Zazie dans le métro

Visual slapstick from verbal play. Carry a verbal joke into a cut, a gesture, or the street itself.

How it was made · 10 foraged sources

The métro is on strike, so a paper girl chases the tram. A jump cut moves her about two kilometres mid-laugh, beside the tram. Her word "Doukipudonktan", fourteen paper teeth, falls out, stands up as a queue at the tram door, then topples into stairs to its top deck. Foraged: four Library of Congress Photochroms of Paris, c.1890-1900 (public domain), the tram cut from one; Commons paper scans (public domain/CC0). Handmade: jointed puppet, tiles, signs, plaques; the strike is staged. Sounds are synthesized except the wind: "Strong winds before the Rain", SAgbley, CC BY-SA 4.0, modified (trimmed, filtered, mixed). The film is released under CC BY-SA 4.0. Borrowed from Queneau's novel (1959, in copyright), quoted not reproduced: the title, the word 'Doukipudonktan' and the strike premise. Review: the independent critic passed it; the rights reviewer's one objection (this borrowing was unrecorded) is fixed in these credits.

Earlier versionÉtienne Lécroart · 2000

Cercle vicieux

Long-form panel palindrome. Reverse a comic’s panel sequence after its midpoint.

How it was made

A four-page palindrome in black-and-white pen and ink; the camera reads panel by panel and holds each full page. Nine panels are drawn once; eight of them appear twice, giving 17 occurrences in the order A B C D E F G H X H G F E D C B A; the script checks each repeat is byte-identical. The hinge X, the bell that can only be rung from behind the service door, opens page 3 after a page turn. The porter's opening shout reads as an accusation; when the same panel closes the story it reads as protection. Voices are captions with synthesized murmurs, and each repeated panel reuses the same murmur.

99 Ways to Tell a Story · Subjective

First-person reconstruction. Retell the same incident through what one participant can perceive.

How it was made

The same office incident (events E1 to E5 from 99-ways-template) redrawn through the clerk's eyes. The film opens on the clerk's own hands on the desk and the note "Fetch the service key!" The question "What time is it?" comes from a balloon whose tail leaves the frame, with a murmur panned hard left. The wall clock appears only in panel 3, after a head turn drawn with motion lines. The hands then open the cupboard and lift empty in front of the keys, and the service key stays on its hook. The film ends with the objective and subjective pages side by side. The clock's tick is heard before it is seen.
