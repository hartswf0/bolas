A cut-paper mouth reads one fixed poem: as sound, as typed text, then with an image. Foraged from Wikimedia Commons: the voice, 14 single-word Lingua Libre recordings (16 words) by Grendelkhan (CC0) and Vealhurl ("binturong", "popcorn", CC BY-SA 4.0), trimmed and re-ordered with two declared /z/ splices; the soundtrack is CC BY-SA 4.0. The binturong is F. Specht's public-domain engraving from Brehm (1895; scan Biodiversity Heritage Library), cut out and re-hung by its tail by us; in the original it stands. Handmade: the layered paper mouth and teeth, a printed card gong, cord, type, pencil. Room tone and paper sounds are synthesised; nothing rings. Readers are fictional.
 Review status: the independent critic passed it, noting that the tail tied to the knot reads more like a pale paddle than a tail, and that the paper mouth is coarser than the reference. The rights reviewer found the attribution complete and correct, but failed it because the makers moved two acceptance targets themselves (the guess times, and skin area measured as flesh mass); both changes are declared in the plan.
