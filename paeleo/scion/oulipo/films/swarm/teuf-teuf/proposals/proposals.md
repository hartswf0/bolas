# Teuf-Teuf: proposals

Reference: `assets/references/teuf.webp` (Cité Films still from Jabely's 1956 animation). It shows a cream, smoothly washed mural ground between dark rubbed charcoal bands at the top and bottom. Solid black animal silhouettes have grey dry-brush detail inside them. Hand stencils sit in halos of blown pigment, white scratched outlines show ghost animals, and dot clusters float in the upper right. The image is monochrome sepia, softly lit, flat and frontal, with no type.

The defining beat is the same challenge in all three directions. **One load, one push, three mechanisms:** dragged (it sticks and slips, 1.2 m), on loose rollers (3.0 m, and a roller falls out behind), and on wheels and axles (8.0 m). These outcomes are the existing `films/teuf-teuf.py` fixture. The improvement must read with the sound off. One nonhuman object speaks one line.

The common timing for a film of about 60 s: wall wakes 0–10 s, drag 10–20 s, rollers 20–32 s, wheels 32–44 s, counter-check with the axle removed 44–48 s, comparison held 48–60 s. The 30 s Hard proof runs from 10 to 44 s, slightly compressed.

The keyframes are real builds. The scripts are in `src/` (`common.py`, `puppet.py`, `cut.py`, `kf1.py`, `kf2.py`, `kf3.py`, `board.py`). **New downloads: 0 MB.** All the foraged material was already in `films/sources/teuf-teuf/` and is already credited in `films/credits/teuf-teuf.json`.

I searched the Moving Image Archive cache for wheel, cart, roll, push, haul, drag, rope, sledge, stone, crate and load, and looked at the sheets. Only two hits were close. "Belo Horizonte" (1949) shows a 2 s extreme long shot of a hillside, with no visible hauling. The Besse amateur film (1938) turned out to be football, not tug of war. Neither carries a licence field. Commons has Polygoon newsreels ("Café aan de rol", "Transport van huis": buildings on rollers), but the API was rate-limiting (HTTP 429) during this stage, so I did not verify their licences or download them. That is why none of the three directions is archive-led.

## 1 · La paroi (articulated charcoal cutout puppets on the rock)
- **Beat / time:** the rollers, at 27.0 s. Two silhouetted figures move a rough boulder on two logs. The pusher leans 40°, with both hands pinned to the stone. The third log has just rolled out behind and lies free. The carrier, bent at the front, is setting another log down. A white scratched ghost of the boulder marks where the drag stopped (beat 1), and drag grooves are scratched into the ground band. The log left behind speaks: « Et moi ? » dit le rouleau qu'on laisse derrière.
- **Material:** the wall is foraged. It is "Cueva de las manos - detalle.jpg" by Suzko, CC BY-SA 3.0 (Commons), re-toned to the reference's ramp. The rock grain is a high-pass layer, and the real ochre hand stencils are kept as dark hands, so the handprints are real Palaeolithic stencils rather than drawn copies. Everything else is handmade: the figures are cut-card puppets (tapered limb pieces), and the boulder and logs are irregular cut shapes. All of them are filled with dry-brush charcoal and haloed with procedural blown pigment. The charcoal bands are rubbed procedurally.
- **Technique:** #2 Reiniger articulated shadow theatre and #32 Forward kinematics (hip, then torso, then head). #33 Inverse kinematics pins the feet to the ground line and the hands to the stone face (two-bone IK in `puppet.py`). #41 Secondary dynamics adds a head nod and stone sway at each stick or release. #45 Occlusion keeps the logs behind the feet. How it moves frame by frame (25 fps): the boulder's x position comes from the fixture's sim. Each log turns by Δx/(2R) per frame, and its scratched radial mark makes the turn visible. It also translates at half the load speed, so it drifts back relative to the stone until it drops out at the rear, rolls 0.3 s and stops with a "tok". The carrier walks it forward, crouches and sets it down. The IK keeps every contact fixed while the hips advance. The drag beat uses the same rig with a sledge runner: the figure strains, the stone holds, then slips 0.12 m.
- **Risk:** the end-on logs read like wheels in profile. That blurs the rollers-to-wheels distinction, so the logs need bark texture and an irregular outline, plus one oblique close-up (the §8 repair: one contact staged in close-up). The puppet figures are generic rather than as accomplished as Jabely's animals. The re-toned rock is busier and greyer than the reference's smooth wash.

## 2 · Ombres des relevés (engravings cut into shadow-theatre silhouettes, collage animation)
- **Beat / time:** the wheel, at 40.0 s. The el-Bersheh colossus on its sledge now rides two spoked wheels on an axle beam. Three haulers do what a crowd did, and the crowd of 16 stands idle behind them, paler (the back plane). White scratched circles mark where the rollers were left. The wheel speaks: La roue : « Je tourne sur place, et c'est vous qui avancez. »
- **Material:** all of it is foraged from public-domain engravings, and each is a *sourced depiction* of its mechanism, so the chronology rides on its sources:
  - Wilkinson, colossus of Djehutihotep on a sledge (after the el-Bersheh tomb relief). "Colosse-djéhoutihétep2.jpg", public domain (Commons). It supplies the load, the sledge and the hauler rows.
  - Layard, *Nineveh and Babylon* (1882), the Kouyunjik relief of a colossal bull on a sledge over rollers. Commons file from Internet Archive Book Images, no known restrictions, PD by date. It supplies the rope haulers (mirrored), the rollers and the two spoked cart wheels.
  - The mural ground, bands and dot clusters are handmade procedural wash. The axle beam and rope are handmade cut card.
- **Technique:** #29 cutout extraction (`cut.py`: threshold the engraving lines, close, flood-fill the exterior, and the filled interior becomes a solid silhouette). The engraving's own interior lines are kept as light scratches inside the black, which matches the reference's grey brushed detail inside its bulls. #1 Gilliam cutout and #43 Collage graph rewriting: the [substitute] operation literally swaps the plate that supplies the mechanism under the same Wilkinson load (Wilkinson sledge, then Layard rollers, then Layard wheels). #44 Multiplane parallax: the crowd plane sits at 0.55 density and moves at 0.6× during camera drift. #45 Occlusion. How it moves frame by frame: the cutouts are rigid pieces, as in cut-paper animation. Haulers advance with a 2-frame bob and a lean that tracks rope tension (shear ≤ 4°), stepping on held 2s. The load moves along x from the sim. The wheels rotate by Δx/R with their spokes visible (the gaps open onto the wall). The rollers in beat 2 rotate and fall behind as in direction 1. The idle crowd shifts weight in a slow random-phase cycle.
- **Risk:** the engraved figures are rigid, so the bodies cannot show effort the way an IK puppet can. Strain has to come from lean, spacing and timing. Mixing Egyptian and Assyrian sources onto one load is a staged compression, and the film must say so in its source register. Mirroring the Layard haulers is an editorial transform. Merged crowd silhouettes look like a blob at small sizes. The carts in the Layard plate are not "the invention of the wheel", so the film must not imply a date for them.

## 3 · Pochoirs du mouvement (procedural simulation printed as blown-pigment stencils)
- **Beat / time:** the comparison held at the end of the proof, at 52.0 s (push clock 7.0 s). Three bands of the same wall show the same hand stencil at each start and the same boulder. Every 0.5 s the load leaves a *negative stencil*: pigment blown round it, the shape left as bare wall, as round the reference's hands. Older prints are fainter. The spacing is the speed. Dragged, the prints pile into a smear (1.2 m). On rollers, they bunch where the load waits while a log is carried forward (3.0 m). On wheels, they open out evenly and accelerate to 8.0 m. Each lane has a scratched metre ruler, and a mono readout shows the distance. The caption reads: « Même main, même poussée, sept secondes : seule la roue a de l'avenir. »
- **Material:** the simulation is handmade. The three motion laws are in `kf3.py`, with asserts against the fixture's 1.2 / 3.0 / 8.0 m. So are the boulder, the hand (constructed, not traced) and the stencil process. The wall is the same foraged Cueva de las Manos rock (Suzko, CC BY-SA 3.0), softened 50 % toward the wash.
- **Technique:** #8 Sutherland constraint sketchpad (the motion comes from the constraint and force model, not from keyframes). #6 Whitney harmonic motion (the three lanes are one score on a shared clock, and the 0.5 s stencil strobe is its meter). #41 Secondary dynamics (stick-slip, the roller wait). Kinetic typography: the readouts count up live. How it moves frame by frame: all three lanes run simultaneously from the same hand-press frame. The live load moves per frame on its sim, and every 12th frame a spray burst (dots fade in over 3 frames, with a "pff") leaves a stencil. In the 44–48 s counter-check the axle beam is removed: the wheels lane reverts to roller behaviour mid-run and its prints bunch. The other two lanes are replayed exactly, which is the equivalence the theory section asks for.
- **Risk:** it is a diagram more than a performance. Bodies are reduced to the hand, so on its own it gives the "machine's problem through performance" the least. The type (labels, rulers, readouts) moves away from the reference, which has no type. It works best as the closing proof rather than the whole film.

---

## Recommendation
Build **2 · Ombres des relevés** as the film's body, and close it with **3 · Pochoirs du mouvement** as the 48–60 s comparison.

Direction 2 is the nearest to the reference: a smooth cream mural, black silhouettes with brushed interior detail, blown-pigment halos, dot clusters and scratched ghosts. It is also the only direction where the mechanisms themselves are foraged *as historical depictions* (Wilkinson's sledge, Layard's rollers and wheels). That keeps "cite chronology only where sourced" honest: each mechanism comes with its source plate. The [substitute] operation becomes a visible collage act: the plate under the load changes and the load does not. Direction 3's stencil strobe then turns the whole demonstration into one sound-off proof, and its axle-removal replay is the required counterexample.

Keep **1 · La paroi** as a named branch. Its IK puppets are the fallback if the rigid engraved haulers cannot show effort. Its log close-up is the §8 repair for the middle beat.
