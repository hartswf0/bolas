# La Cité Raymond Queneau: round 2 proposals

Reference: `assets/references/citefilm.webp`. It shows pale cream blocks with dark, curved zinc roofs along an avenue, under a flat white overcast sky, seen at eye level across a road and a grass verge. The contrast is low and there is no type. Round 1 is in `r1/`, and you chose its direction 2: a lateral move across a real French cité with two caption editions. Direction 1 below keeps that line. Directions 2 and 3 are animation-led.

The defining beat is the same in all three: **one unchanged picture reads as a welcoming home in edition A and as uneasy surveillance in edition B**, and only what is heard switches it. Each keyframe draws **both** caption tracks so you can compare them: A (warm) above, B (cool) below. In the film the player shows one sidecar track per edition, and the picture master never carries type, because a graphic insertion fails the fixture. The caption-free picture frames are saved next to each keyframe as `kf-N-picture.png`. The scripts are in `src/`. New downloads total about 1.7 MB (four Commons 1280 px thumbnails) in `films/sources/la-cite-raymond-queneau/`.

Common master (about 30 s, identical in A, B and the silent control): establishing (0–8 s), approach (8–16 s), **key shot held (16–26 s)**, exit (26–30 s). The voice-only / music-only crossover test sits on the 16 s cut.

## 1 · Travelling Léon-Blum (foraged photograph, multiplane track). This is the r1 line you chose.
- **Beat / time:** the approach, at 8–16 s. The camera tracks slowly past the parked cars toward the towers, and the swan-neck lamp post crossing the frame is the hinge, at about 13 s. A: « Le soir, l'avenue s'allume pour tout le monde. » B: « Le lampadaire du parking est équipé depuis mars. » The cue: a room-tone swell and a distant radio in A, a faint servo whirr panned onto the post in B. The picture is the same in both.
- **Material:** Commons, "Immeubles Avenue Léon Blum - Corbeil-Essonnes (FR91) - 2023-10-21 - 1.jpg" by Chabe01, CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Immeubles_Avenue_L%C3%A9on_Blum_-_Corbeil-Essonnes_(FR91)_-_2023-10-21_-_1.jpg. The second frame of that series (`-2.jpg`, same author and licence) is downloaded as a clean far plate. This replaces r1's Bellefontaine: the overcast here is real and flat, the avenue is the reference's kind, and the lamp post has its head in frame. The crop leaves out the licence plates.
- **Technique:** #29 GrabCut cuts the cars, fence and kerb into a near plane. The lamp post is cut by a geometric envelope plus a darkness mask. The holes are filled with Telea inpainting. #44 Multiplane parallax: in the film, the sky and towers move at 0.25x, the lamp at 0.6x and the cars at 1.0x of a 40 px lateral track over 12 s, sampled once per frame with no retiming. #45 Occlusion keeps the post in front of the facade. The keyframe is the p = 0.6 state. #27 Declarative compiler and #47 Sound-to-action carry the EDL and the CueClaims.
- **Risk:** this is still a photograph made to move. A faint inpaint ghost shows where the post was (about x 555, y 340), and the move has to stay small. The towers are 1970s brown-and-beige high-rises, not the reference's 1990s cream blocks with zinc roofs. The low camera angle and wide lens are steeper than the reference's flat eye-level view. There are no people.

## 2 · La maquette (handmade paper model, multiplane, the windows as a kinetic score)
- **Beat / time:** the key shot, at 16–26 s. The camera rests on the paper cité and the windows light one by one in a fixed permutation (window i lights at step (37·i mod 96)), one every 0.9 s. This is a visible score that the two soundtracks read differently. A: « Six heures : on rentre, une fenêtre après l'autre. » (each light lands on a door latch, a kettle, a greeting). B: « Six heures : chaque fenêtre allumée est notée. » (each light lands on a typewriter key or a relay click, over a held drone). The keyframe is t = 16.0 s, with 11 of 96 windows lit.
- **Material:** handmade. It is a cut-paper model built for this work: a cream facade sheet, a dark slate sheet for the curved zinc roof, dormers, windows cut through to grey-blue tissue (unlit) or amber paper (lit), balcony strips, bare saplings, a hedge, a grey-card road with paint dashes, and a grass verge. Each sheet has its own fibre grain, a hand-jittered cut edge and a soft overcast drop shadow onto the sheet behind. No foraged bytes are used, so there is no licence question. The palette and composition aim at the reference: two volumes receding to the left, a curved roof end on the right, a verge in the foreground.
- **Technique:** #5 Norstein multiplane and #44 Multiplane parallax: seven planes, with the haze plane at 0.15x and the lamp post at 1.2x during the establishing track (0–16 s). Then the track stops. #6 Whitney harmonic motion: the lighting order is a permutation score, and each window's glow rises over 3 frames, with a 0.5 % paper "breathing" jitter so it does not feel mechanical. #40 Physical tabletop capture is optional, meaning the swarm can print and cut these sheets and shoot them on a rostrum for real. #47 Sound-to-action maps each lit window to its sound event.
- **Risk:** it can read as an illustration rather than a cité, because it is less documentary than the reference's video. Lit windows in daylight need the light to move toward dusk across the shot. The window score is a strong pattern, and B (a machine counting the windows) may win over A unless A's sounds are domestic and slightly irregular.

## 3 · Le réverbère (articulated cutout puppet over a foraged plate)
- **Beat / time:** the key shot, at 16–26 s. A grey-card street lamp, pinned with brass paper fasteners at the pole top and the neck, slowly turns its head from the doorway down to the parked car and holds, and its light pool settles on the car. The keyframe is t = 19 s: the arm at −6°, the head at −42°. A: « Le réverbère se penche vers ceux qui rentrent. » (a creak, a music-box phrase, footsteps). B: « Le réverbère suit chaque voiture qui se gare. » (a servo whirr, a shutter click, then silence). The same nod reads as a bow or as a camera pan.
- **Material:** the plate is from Commons, "Immeuble 9 Chemin Ronde - Noisy-le-Grand (FR93) - 2021-10-02 - 1.jpg" by Chabe01, CC BY-SA 4.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Immeuble_9_Chemin_Ronde_-_Noisy-le-Grand_(FR93)_-_2021-10-02_-_1.jpg. It is a cream residential block under a white overcast sky, and the car's plate is blurred. The puppet is handmade: five cut-card pieces (plinth, pole, collar, swan-neck arm, hood and lens) with their own grain, cut edges and drop shadows.
- **Technique:** #2 Reiniger articulated shadow theatre, #1 Gilliam cutout and #32 Forward kinematics: a two-joint chain (pole top to arm, arm to head) keyframed on eased curves. #41 Secondary dynamics: the head overshoots by 2° and settles after each turn. #45 Occlusion: the lamp stays in front of the plate, and its shadow falls on the facade. The light pool is computed from the lens pose each frame. #47 Sound-to-action ties the turn, the stop and the hold to their cues. The plate itself is still, which is the convention of a collage stage.
- **Risk:** the puppet is an overt fiction, so the "documentary" quality of the Hard proof becomes a fable. That is a gain in clarity and a loss in subtlety. A turning head already suggests watching, so A has to work harder. The Noisy photograph is a wide-angle view of a corner block, not a long avenue.

## Recommendation
Build **2 · La maquette** as the film's body, and keep **1 · Travelling Léon-Blum** as its opening.

Direction 2 is the most truly animated of the three, and it is closest to the reference's own grammar: cream blocks with curved dark roofs, a verge, a road, flat overcast light, eye level. Its window score also turns the proof into a precise, countable instrument. The picture events are exactly timed, so each CueClaim can name a frame, and the viewer can point at the window where the reading changed.

The recommended edit is: the real Corbeil track for 0–8 s ("this place exists"), a match-dissolve into the paper model at 8 s, the window score held for 16–26 s, and the paper lamp post crossing out at 26–30 s. This keeps the archive line you chose while making the key shot an animation. The dissolve belongs to the picture master, so both editions share it.

Keep **3 · Le réverbère** as a named branch, or as the third "silence and room tone" edition's insert. Its turning head is the strongest single sound-switch gesture, but it leans toward B before any sound plays.
