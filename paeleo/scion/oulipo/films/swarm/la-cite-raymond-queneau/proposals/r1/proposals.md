# La Cité Raymond Queneau: three proposed directions

Reference: `assets/references/citefilm.webp`. It is a 1990s video frame: pale cream housing blocks with dark curved zinc roofs, under a flat white overcast sky, seen across an empty road with a grass verge in the foreground. The tones are soft and low in contrast, and there is no type. The defining beat of the Hard proof is **one unchanged shot that reads as a welcoming home in edition A and as uneasy surveillance in edition B**, switched only by what is heard at the key cut. Each keyframe is 1280x720 and shows that shot. The picture is only scaled, plus a half-strength exposure and contrast match to the reference's mean and spread, with no hue grade. **Both** sidecar subtitle tracks are drawn on the keyframe so that you can compare them: A (warm) above, B (cool) below. In the film the player draws only one track per edition, and the picture master's frame hashes never contain type, because inserting a graphic fails the fixture. Captions are the voice, with no TTS. Downloads total about 15 MB and are in `films/sources/la-cite-raymond-queneau/`. The keyframe script is in the session scratchpad.

Planned master (about 30 s, identical in A, B and the silent control): establishing exterior (0–8 s) → approach (8–16) → **key shot, held** (16–26) → exit (26–30). The key cut lands at about 16 s. The voice-only / music-only crossover test from the proof is placed on that cut.

## 1 · La fenêtre (one foraged newsreel, re-cut)
- **Beat / time:** the key shot at about 16–26 s. A resident stands in silhouette at his flat window, with the estate below. A: « D'ici, il voit l'école où vont ses petits-enfants. » B: « D'ici, il note qui entre, et à quelle heure. » The cue that switches the reading is a single sound at 16 s: a child's call and an open major-key accordion phrase in A, a door latch then a held low drone in B.
- **Material:** Commons, *Terrasflats, nieuwe vorm / Terraced apartments in the new form* (Polygoon-Profilti, 1974, Netherlands Institute for Sound and Vision), CC BY-SA 3.0 NL, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Terrasflats,_nieuwe_vorm-Terraced_apartments_in_the_new_form-514043.ogv. The 480p transcode is used, and this frame is at source t=44 s. The same reel supplies the whole master: a pale terraced block behind grass (t≈4 s, the closest match to the reference), the gallery walk, the stairwell, and the window.
- **Technique:** #27 Declarative film compiler. One EDL builds a single picture master, and both editions mux that same video essence, verified by decoded-frame SHA-1 (kf-1 picture `5b069570…`). #47 Sound-to-action. CueClaims tie each audible event to a frame number and to the reading it should cause. The frames are the reel's own camera moves: a slow pan along the block, then a hold on the silhouette. Nothing is warped or retimed.
- **Risk:** the colour is 1974 Eastmancolor and the building is Dutch terraces, not a French cité. That is a period and place drift from the 1998 reference. The silhouette is so readable for surveillance that A might fail. The window shot is backlit and dark, while the reference is high-key. The reel's own commentary must be discarded, and only its picture used.

## 2 · Travelling Bellefontaine (foraged photograph, cut into planes)
- **Beat / time:** the approach at about 8–16 s. This is a slow lateral track past a parking lot toward the Bellefontaine blocks, and the lamp post crossing the frame is the hinge. A: « Le soir, l'avenue s'allume pour tout le monde. » B: « Le lampadaire du parking est équipé depuis mars. » The cue that switches the reading is the soundtrack's treatment of the post as it passes, at about 13 s: a soft room-tone swell in A, a faint servo whirr panned to the post in B. The picture is identical.
- **Material:** Commons, "Immeuble Bellefontaine.jpg" by Alteo31300, CC BY-SA 3.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Immeuble_Bellefontaine.jpg. This is a real Toulouse grand ensemble under an overcast winter sky, with the road in the foreground. It is the closest match in this set to the reference's light and its framing across a road. The licence plate of the parked car is blurred.
- **Technique:** #29 GrabCut. Per-object rectangle GrabCut cuts out the red car, the grey car and the near bollard, and a luminance column mask cuts out the lamp post. The gaps are filled with Telea inpainting. #44 Multiplane parallax. In the film, the far blocks move at 0.25x, the fence and road at 0.6x, and the foreground plane at 1.0x of a 40 px lateral track over about 12 s, which mimics the reference's dolly along the road. #45 Occlusion keeps the post and cars in front. The keyframe is the mid-track state.
- **Risk:** this is a still photograph made to move. Parallax from a single still shows inpaint smear, and a faint ghost column survives beside the post at about x 290, y 250–400. It needs a cleaner plate or a smaller move. The lamp head is cropped out of frame. The light is late-afternoon pink rather than the reference's flat white. There are no people in it, so the "community" reading must come entirely from sound.

## 3 · Vu d'en haut (foraged drone descent)
- **Beat / time:** the key shot at about 16–26 s. The drone descent stops and hovers over one courtyard. A: « Vu d'en haut, tout le quartier tient dans une main. » B: « Vu d'en haut, personne ne sait qui regarde. » The cue that switches the reading at 16 s is when the descent stops: birdsong and a distant school bell in A, sudden silence and then rotor hum brought close in B.
- **Material:** Commons, "Szczecin Niebuszewo osiedle modernistyczne.webm" by Kapitel, CC BY-SA 4.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Szczecin_Niebuszewo_osiedle_modernistyczne.webm. It is a 2023 drone flight over a modernist housing estate. The 480p transcode is used, and this frame is at source t=24 s.
- **Technique:** #27 Declarative compiler. The master is cut from the native flight, and its own motion supplies the descent and the hover. #47 Sound-to-action. The stop of the motion is the cue frame. Optionally, #44 Multiplane parallax is not needed, because the motion is real.
- **Risk:** it is the furthest from the reference. The frame is a sunny summer bird's-eye view with red roofs, where the reference is an overcast view at eye level. The god's-eye view already says "surveillance" before any sound plays, so edition A may not hold. A drone is a contemporary sign that the 1998 film did not have.

## Recommendation
Build **2 · Travelling Bellefontaine** as the spine. It is the only direction that matches the reference's own grammar: a real French cité under an overcast sky, seen at eye level across a road, with a slow lateral move. Its key prop, the lamp post, is truly neutral, so the switch has to be made by sound, which is exactly the claim the proof tests. Before the swarm builds it, find a cleaner plate (a second Commons photograph of the same blocks for the far plane) or reduce the move to hide the inpaint ghost.

Use **1 · La fenêtre** as the held key shot after the approach, or as the third "silence and room tone" branch. Its human figure gives edition A something to hold onto, and its newsreel provenance is clean (CC BY-SA, a named producer). Its darker exposure needs care at the cut. Keep **3** only as a short insert, if at all, because its reading is fixed by the image alone.
