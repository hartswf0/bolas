# les-lieux-dune-fugue: three directions (proposal stage, no film rendered)

Reference: `assets/references/fugue1.webp`. A frontal, flat, static shot of a Paris carriage door, black against warm sandstone, a small "18" plaque, 1978 16 mm colour, no figures. The defining beat of the Hard proof: **the memory of the bench is laid over the doorway, then the bench returns in silence.** For comparison, the existing film `films/les-lieux-dune-fugue.py` (46 s) shows slated places with captions over a synthesized murmur.

Downloads this stage: 3 LoC files, about 0.7 MB in total. Commons API returned HTTP 429 throughout, so the Commons material comes from files already in `films/sources/les-lieux-dune-fugue/`. Build scripts are in the scratchpad (`kf1.py`, `kf2.py`, `kf3.py`).

## 1 · Bench in the Door (`kf-1.png`)
- **Beat / time:** the central beat. Doorway P01, second capture, about 00:31 of 00:56. The station bench P03 shows inside the dark opening like a second exposure while the voice says « Le banc, c'était l'hiver, à peu près. »
- **Material:** *29 rue du Soleil, Paris 20e 5.jpg* by Polymagou, CC BY-SA 4.0 (Commons). *Paris Metro Nationale Bänke.JPG* by Bellomonte, CC0 (Commons). Both are already in the credits file.
- **Techniques:** SCION 29, GrabCut. The bench is a real seeded GrabCut matte: red-slat hue as foreground seeds, then mask-init iterations. SCION 45, Occlusion Compositing. The matte is screen-blended only where the door's dark is, so stone and door leaves stay in front. SCION 44, Multiplane Parallax, for the motion: the door plate is locked off. The bench layer fades in over about 3 s and drifts 1–2 % on its own plane, then fades out. That leaves the empty opening, followed by a hard cut to the bench plate in silence. The grade is a warm, desaturated, frontal match to the reference.
- **Risk:** in the existing film, the two "captures" of P01 are different openings on the same facade: door 29 and the carriage door to its right. They are not the same position. The proof's "one place twice" therefore needs a second photo from the same spot, and Commons is rate-limited right now. The matte edges are soft and the bench reads as a red smear at small sizes. It needs a cleaner matte (SCION 30 SAM 2) or a larger placement. The reference's door is darker and smoother than this graffitied one.

## 2 · The Seam: same door, two times (`kf-2.png`)
- **Beat / time:** "shoot one place twice from the same position at different times", about 00:17. A vertical seam runs down the door's dark edge. Left of it is capture A (source t = 8.2 s, the empty door). Right of it is capture B (t = 13.3 s, a child at the door). The ledger column shows capture_time, remembered_time ("roughly winter", an interval) and screen_time separately.
- **Material:** *Alice in Wonderland* (W. W. Young, 1915), public domain (US, pre-1929). The shot comes from the Moving Image Archive cache, clip `147f497d` (movingimagearchive.com/sources/…). It is a static camera: mean pixel difference in the door region between the two times is 5/255, so the shot is registered as filmed.
- **Techniques:** SCION 53, Civic Footage Performer, and SCION 54, Provenance Graph. Every half-frame keeps its source id and timecode. There is no warp: translate and scale only, following the allways-forage `render.py` rule. Motion: the seam slides across the door from left to right over 4 s, revealing time B over time A. The child appears with no cut and the door does not move. A wipe back then leaves the empty door again.
- **Risk:** the source is 320×240. Shown at 960×720 it is soft. The fix is the full-resolution file from the archive, or LoC/Commons once reachable. It is an enacted, costumed image and not a present-day space, so it must be declared as the alternate method under §4. Alice is a recognizable fiction and may pull the "fictional childhood departure" toward Carroll. The sepia is far from the reference's 1978 colour.

## 3 · Place Record, silent return (`kf-3.png`)
- **Beat / time:** the closing beat, about 00:50–00:56. The bench returns in declared silence, shown as the handmade place record that carries the uncertainty: P03, cue, "roughly winter = interval, open", link to P01 INFERRED, voice none, sound SILENCE. Below it is a hand-ruled route P01 porte → P02 carrefour → P03 banc, with a dashed red line for the inferred memory link.
- **Material:** handmade. The card is a typeset, ruled form built for this work, with a mounted print of the CC0 Metro bench (Bellomonte, Commons). LoC FSA prints were downloaded as alternative bench prints (*Park bench, Lakeland, Florida*, 1939, FSA/OWI, PD, `loc_8a40126v/127v.jpg`), but they are full of sitters, so they were not used.
- **Techniques:** SCION 40, Physical Tabletop Capture: a rostrum card under one lamp. The plan is to print it and photograph it for real in the build. SCION 54, Provenance Graph: the card *is* the ledger. Motion: the camera is held still on the card. During 00:50–00:56 the only change is the print's light rising and the "SILENCE" field being stamped. No sound at all.
- **Risk:** it moves away from the reference's photographic frontality toward a document insert, so it works as the film's end record, not as its look. The bench print contains a poster with an identifiable person, which must be cropped out. If used alone it turns the film into a dossier. It is best as the final 6 s of direction 1.

## Recommendation
**Direction 1 (Bench in the Door) is the spine, with direction 3 as its last six seconds.** Direction 1 is the only one that stages the defining beat (bench memory over the doorway) on present-day, frontal, warm-stone material close to the reference. Direction 3 makes the silent return and the record's uncertainty legible without voice. Before the swarm build, two things must happen. First, the route researcher replaces P01 with one dark carriage door photographed twice from the same position, from Commons when the 429s clear (search "porte cochère" with numbered plaques). Second, the matte is cleaned with SAM 2. Keep direction 2 as a named alternate branch, and use it if no same-position present-day pair can be found.
