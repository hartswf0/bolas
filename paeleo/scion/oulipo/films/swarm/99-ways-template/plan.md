## 1 · Creative thesis

**Thesis.** An ordinary interruption can be made so legible that the photographs themselves become the score. A 1937 railway agent, cut out of his own office with scissors, leaves his desk for a key, answers a stranger's question through the ticket window and stands at the open cupboard with the key in plain view, having forgotten it.

The joke works on two levels:
- **The reader** can see what the clerk cannot: the memo on his desk (P1), the clock (P5) and the brass key on its hook (P8).
- **The cut-paper articulation** is the comedy's mechanics. His head swings on a collar hinge toward the voice, the cupboard door swings on its jamb, and the pencil-arm rises to the chin.

These are real photographs moved like Gilliam cutouts, and the page stays Madden's template: 3/3/2 panels, white gutters, mixed-case balloons, and an objective viewpoint.

**What would make it bland:**
- A slideshow of FSA photos with captions.
- Grey, muddy, photo-album panels: low contrast, no spotted blacks, no white paper showing.
- Balloons in a stock typeface.
- Motion done as pans and dissolves instead of hinged pieces that change pose.
- A cupboard that does not visibly hold the key, so that "forgetting" is only stated, never seen.
- A camera that drifts over the page without performing the reading order.
- Derivatives that are only re-crops of the same page.

**Defining beat: E5, forgetting (film 0:36–0:46, page panels P6→P8).**
1. The ticket window, now empty, says "Thanks!" with a tail out of the panel. The visitor has gone.
2. Cut to the cupboard. The door is open on its jamb, and the brass SERVICE key hangs on its hook at the clerk's elbow.
3. The clerk's head tilts up in three hinged steps toward the shelves. His pencil-arm rises to his chin. The balloon "What the hell was I looking for, anyway?" drops in as paper with one bounce.
4. Hold. The key does not move.

**What a viewer can point to:** the key on its hook inside the open door, the head tilted away from it, the pencil at the chin, and the empty ticket window in the previous panel.

**Comparison baseline:**
- (a) Madden's Template page (`assets/references/madden1.webp`), for page grammar and tonal design.
- (b) The chosen keyframe `proposals/kf-2.png`, for material and the E5 staging. The new E5 panel must be at least as clear and as high-key as kf-2, with the key now visible.
- (c) The existing site film (a clean vector line restyle). The new work must beat it on material reality and on articulated motion.

### Central operation: [derive](Baseline b1, Operator op) → Variant v

**Typed preconditions:**
1. `b.revision == "b1"` and b1 is frozen: the blind readers' retelling matches E0–E5 in order.
2. `op.changed_dimension` is exactly one of {`panel_count/time`, `viewpoint`}. Every other dimension is pinned (material set, lettering, page ground, objective vs subjective tone, outcome).
3. `op` maps every Event to {shown, implied-by-cue(cue spec), omitted}. An event marked *implied* must name a visible or audible cue.
4. `v.outcome == b.outcome`: the key stays on the hook, the door stays closed, the goal is forgotten.
5. The speaker of every retained line equals its speaker in b1.

**Observable consequences:**
- A reader of v can name the same incident (same people, objects, goal and outcome).
- A reader can also name what op changed: "it's shorter, two panels" or "we see it from outside, from the platform".
- v's coverage row in `coverage.json` lists each E as shown, implied (with cue) or omitted. No event is silently re-caused.

**Counterexample (must be rejected):**
- **A "platform POV" variant** where, through the window, the visitor sees the clerk take the key and open the service door. The outcome is changed, so it is a declared alternate story and leaves the style comparison set.
- **A "compressed" variant** that drops the window and draws the "What time is it?" tail to the clerk's own mouth. Speaker identity is broken: the clerk appears to ask himself, and the interruption vanishes.
- Either must fail the coverage check.

**Where replay and alternate views are equivalent, and where they diverge:**
- **Film replay of the page vs the page itself.** Equivalent on event order, speakers, objects and outcome; at rest, the art in each panel is pixel-identical to the page. They diverge in two ways:
  - **Duration:** the film imposes durations (5 s per panel) where the page lets the reader dwell.
  - **Motion:** the film shows the in-panel motion (the head turning, the door swinging, the arm rising), while the page shows only each panel's rest pose. So every page panel must carry its event in a still: P7's door is drawn open, and P8 has its pencil at the chin.
- **Compressed variant (2 panels).** Equivalent on outcome and speakers. It diverges because E1, E2/E3 and E3b are all carried by stacked balloons in one panel, and the reading order inside that panel has to do the work of the timing.
- **Platform POV (visitor).** Equivalent on outcome. It diverges because E0/E1 are out of view. E4/E5 are seen only as a glimpse through the window into the office: the open cupboard far back, the clerk scratching his head. The memo is never seen, so a viewer of this variant alone cannot recover the motive. That is the variant's declared loss.

**Interactive work:** none. The page is read and the film is watched. Invitation, actions, memory and exit are not applicable.

## 4 · Invariants

1. **Event graph b1 is frozen** once the director writes it here and in `ledger.json`. Any change to the beats is revision b2 and invalidates derivatives and their coverage.
2. **Source facts, visible cues and interpretations are kept separate** in `ledger.json`:
   - `fact` is the event as frozen.
   - `cue` is what pixel or sound carries it (for example, a tail exiting the left edge of P6).
   - `interpretation` is what a reader may infer (for example, that the visitor has left). A cue is never relabelled as a fact.
3. **Piece identity is immutable.** Every cutout has an ID (for example `rothstein.clerk.head`) with source item, source-pixel bbox and a transform to page coordinates. The page and the film read the same RGBA pieces at the same revision.
4. **Page/film identity.** Each film panel at rest equals the page crop of that panel (same pieces, poses, balloons and lettering), and every animation resolves into that rest pose.
5. **FK rig invariants:**
   - Bone and pivot offsets stay constant: the head-to-collar pivot distance changes by at most 1.5 px at page scale over any animation.
   - The head never detaches from the collar.
   - The door's jamb edge stays fixed while it swings.
6. **Outcome is invariant across all derivatives.** The key stays on the hook.
7. **Concurrency.** The page-cartoonist owns the canonical beats and `ledger.json`; other units read them at revision b1 and never write them. Each unit writes only its own output directory. A unit that needs a beat change writes a proposal to `amendments.md`.

## 7 · Aesthetic target

These figures were sampled with PIL (luminance percentiles and dominant colours), from `madden1.webp` and `kf-2.png`:

- **Medium.** A photo-comic page: real 1930s–40s B&W documentary photographs, cut as paper pieces with white scissor margins and laid onto a real sheet with ruled panel borders and cut-paper balloons. It is collage made physically legible, not a photo album.
- **Tonal range.** High-key black and white.
  - **Madden:** 57% of pixels are pure white (~252) and 17–19% solid black (~12). The midtones are sparse: the median is 252, and the 25th percentile is 91.
  - **kf-2:** 27% is near-white and 9% black, with a median of 147. It is too grey in its photographs. The target pushes beyond kf-2 toward Madden: at least 35% of page pixels at L>220 and at least 8% at L<40.
  - **How to get there:** choose plates and crops with clean whites (the clerk's white shirt, the white glass, the paper, the racks) and real blacks (his tie, the window frame, the cupboard interior, the deep wood). Then set levels per plate to unify them: black and white points, one curve per source photograph. Never use posterize, threshold, halftone or "comic" filters.
- **Palette.** Neutral greyscale photographs. The page ground is a warm off-white of about RGB (234–242, 232–240, 227–235); the raw 1934 sheet is (213, 197, 163), lifted. The balloon fill is about (248, 246, 240). The ink of the borders, letters and tails is about (18, 17, 16). There is no colour except the sheet's faint cream, and the brass key is grey as photographed.
- **Light.** The deep, even FSA interior light of the Rothstein office: window and flash, hard on the white shirt. Pieces cast a short, soft paper shadow down and right (offset about 6 px at page scale, blur about 4 px, 35% opacity). That is a light raking from the upper left on paper, the same for every piece.
- **Texture.** Real photographic grain from the sources at their native scale (the masters are rescanned, not sharpened), plus the sheet's own fibre. There is no added noise, no fake grain and no vignette.
- **Framing and scale.** Madden's objective, eye-level framing, with the camera never moving between P1 and P3.
  - P1–P3 are medium-wide.
  - P5 and P8 are medium close (waist-up, the head taking about a sixth of the panel height).
  - The window panels are narrow verticals.
  - The pieces keep their photographic scale relationships within a panel: the clerk is not enlarged against his own desk.
- **Typography.**
  - Hand-lettered, mixed-case comics lettering in single-stroke pen, about 70 px caps on the page and roughly 2.2% of page height. It sits slightly irregular on the baseline and has open counters.
  - The balloons are ovals with tapered wedge tails.
  - Labels on the derivative pages are small caps in the same hand.
- **Pacing.** About 5 s per panel. Animation runs on twos with holds at the ends of actions; each balloon drops in over 4 frames with one bounce and is followed by a 1.5 s read hold. The defining beat ends on a 2 s hold.

## 8 · Technique SCIONs (from `swarms.json`; units read them in full)

1. **#1 Terry Gilliam: Comic Cutout Metamorphosis.** This is the governing method. Its primitives are source-image crops, pivot/card joints, matte layers, substitution objects, held frames and impact sound. The comedy is moving the smallest necessary fragment: the head toward the voice, the arm to the chin.
   - **Shots:** S2 and S3 (the sliding, bobbing stepping walk, no legs), S4 (head swing), S7 (door), S8 (arm rise and hold), and the balloon drops as impact objects with paper-tap sound.
   - **Numeric invariant:** every hold lands on its intended frame. Balloon land frames and the end of each action are logged in `anim/timing.json` and checked against the shot list (to within 1 frame).
   - **Perceptual achievement:** paper boundaries look intentional, and each move reads at phone size without explanation. The forgetting reads as a gag, not as a glitch.
2. **#29 GrabCut: Cutout Extraction Swarm.** This makes the pieces. The primitives are box and scribble seeds, unary costs, a pairwise edge term, the graph cut and an alpha cleanup. Each piece is retained as a usable layer: clerk torso, head, pencil-arm, hand, cupboard door, ticket racks, window, lamp, visitor, clock, key, and the inpainted empty-desk plate.
   - **Shots:** all panels.
   - **Numeric invariant:** no background islands (each piece's alpha has exactly one connected component, except declared multi-part pieces), and fingers are retained on the pencil hand (the finger count visible in the mask is the same as in the source crop, checked by the inspector).
   - **Perceptual achievement:** source-edge fidelity at normal size. The white scissor margin reads as a deliberate cut, not as a halo. Failure masks are exported.
3. **#32 Forward Kinematics: Hierarchical Body Logic.** This builds the rig. The primitives are a joint graph (torso → collar → head; torso → shoulder → pencil-arm → hand + pencil; cupboard → jamb → door → key hook → key), local and parent transforms, pivot offsets and view-specific anchors.
   - **Shots:** S1, S2, S4, S5, S7 and S8. The key is attached to the door until P8, so it swings with the door in S7 and stays still in S8.
   - **Numeric invariant:** pivot-distance drift is at most 1.5 px at page scale, with no head/neck detachment, and the key keeps its constant offset from the door hook through the swing.
   - **Perceptual achievement:** the head turns toward the voice and the arm rises with weight (anticipation, overlap and a hold), as a performed action.

Two more SCIONs are noted but not adopted as governing methods: #45 Occlusion (balloon over panel and visitor behind glass) and #44 Multiplane (the soft visitor layer behind the glass). Units may use their simple primitives.
