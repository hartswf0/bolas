# Renzo e Luciana: proposals

Reference: `assets/references/calvino.webp` (Cineteca di Bologna still). It is a black-and-white photograph in mid greys with soft daylight. Marisa Solinas wears a frayed white bucket hat and a striped dress, and Germano Giglioli is bare-shouldered. They sit close together on tube-frame folding chairs on a tiled terrace. He holds a bowl while she drinks from a spoon. Other people crowd the edges of the frame, and the couple's intimacy happens in public. There is no type.

**Defining beat (all directions).** Two co-workers share one private act, a cup or an apple, inside the only minutes their breaks and a hidden corner have in common. A supervisor's doorway is in the same frame. The viewer should be able to point at three things: the clock, the doorway and the gap between the two hands.

**Common clock.** The film runs about 60 s: Version A, the original shift, at 0–28 s, a 2 s cut, then Version B, the late bell, at 30–58 s. One fixture minute is 4 s of film.

| | Renzo's break | Luciana's break | safe corner | meeting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | [12:00, 12:03) | [12:00, 12:04) | [12:01, 12:04) | **[12:01, 12:03)**, t = 4–12 s |
| B | [12:00, 12:03) | **[12:03, 12:07)** (late bell) | [12:01, 12:04) | **empty**: the bell at 12:03 is at t = 42 s |

Sound: the work bell at 12:00 and on her late bell, footsteps for the foreman, a cup on a saucer. Silence (room tone) runs under the meeting, so a sound-off read can be compared with the bell restored.

The keyframes are real builds. The scripts are in `src/` (`common.py`, `cuts.py`, `kf1.py`, `kf2.py`, `kf3.py`, `board.py`) and the GrabCut masks are in `src/cache/`. New downloads total about 1.7 MB: six Library of Congress 1024 px service JPEGs, five of them used, in `films/sources/renzo-e-luciana/loc/`. The Moving Image Archive cache was searched (factory, lunch, canteen, couple, time clock, doorway). It gave factory-floor and shift-change footage, but nothing with both a clear licence and a usable couple at a break. The pre-1929 titles are the only safe PD ones, and they have no break scene. So the foraged direction uses LoC FSA/OWI photographs, which are verifiably public domain.

## 1 · Il paravento (articulated silhouette theatre, handmade)
- **Beat / time:** Version B, 12:03, at **t = 42 s**. The late bell rings over the doorway. Luciana is already up and turned in profile to the door, and her trailing hand stops 18 px short of the saucer Renzo still holds out. The foreman stands in the lit opening, and his light now spills across the floor to their chairs. Each performer has a particular action: he keeps offering the cup, and she obeys the bell with her body while her hand stays behind.
- **Material:** handmade, with no foraged bytes. It is cut black card on a back-lit warm-grey paper screen with fibre grain and lamp falloff. The cut-outs are the reference's tube deck chairs, the striped dress (cut as slits), the frayed bucket-hat brim (cut as a fringe), the terrace tiles (holes cut in the floor strip in perspective rows), a scalloped proscenium, a wall clock at 12:03, and a bell with its clapper mid-swing and crescent "ring" slivers. It is not traced from the reference: the poses and figures are drawn from scratch as smooth Catmull-Rom outlines.
- **Technique and motion:** #2 Reiniger articulated shadow theatre, with each figure an FK chain of pinned pieces (#32): hip, torso, neck, head, shoulder, elbow, wrist, knee and ankle. Luciana's reach is solved with 2-link IK (#33) to a target set by the rule, `GAP = 18 px` short of the saucer. #41 Secondary dynamics adds a 2–3 frame overshoot on the clapper, the hat fringe and the steam curls. #45 Occlusion keeps the foreman on a second sheet inside the lit cut. Frame by frame: the joint angles are keyframed on the fixture clock and sampled every frame, and the clock's minute hand advances 1.5° per film-second (6° per fixture minute at 4 s per minute). In A, her hand reaches the saucer at t = 4 s and they share the cup until t = 12 s. In B, the hand is driven by IK toward the cup while her hip and torso turn to the bell, so the gap opens on screen. The light wedge from the doorway grows as an animated gel layer. #47 Sound-to-action times the bell, the footsteps and the cup.
- **Risk:** the graphic is crisp black on grey, whereas the reference is a soft photograph, so the tonal match is in key and palette, not in medium. Profile silhouettes can look generic unless the hat, stripes and chairs carry the identity. The doorway rule is legible, but the "concealment" part is weaker, because in silhouette everything is equally visible. The foreman's facing direction has to do that work. Ten articulated pieces per figure across 60 s is real rigging work.

## 2 · Collage al marcatempo (foraged FSA/OWI cutouts, articulated collage, occlusion)
- **Beat / time:** Version A, 12:02, at **t = 8 s**, the centre of the safe window. The scene is a real time-clock wall with its card racks, and the clock carries the film's own paper hands at 12:02. Behind the pile of workers' lunch pails, Luciana bites the apple she is leaning in to share, and Renzo leans back and keeps watch. On the left, the foreman stands in a card doorway marked CAPOREPARTO, reading his time card with his back to them. The pails are what hide the couple, and that is the visible cause of the rule.
- **Material (all Library of Congress, FSA/OWI, "No known restrictions on publication", US government work, public domain):**
  - Plate: Russell Lee, *Time clock and tool boxes at the vocational school for aircraft construction workers. San Diego, California*, 1941. https://www.loc.gov/item/2017789670/ (service file `fsa/8c01000/8c01400/8c01492v.jpg`).
  - Renzo: Russell Lee, *Workers eating lunch on curb across the street from the Consolidated Airplane Factory. San Diego*, 1940. https://www.loc.gov/item/2017788376/ (`8c00283v`). The man at far left is cut out.
  - Luciana: Marjory Collins, *Buffalo, New York. Women employees eating lunches brought from home at Ross Heater…*, 1943. https://www.loc.gov/item/2017851428/ (`8d16982v`, OWI-23899-D). The centre woman with the apple is cut out.
  - Foreman: OWI, *Bethlehem-Fairfield shipyards, Baltimore, Maryland. A shipyard worker looking at a time clock…* https://www.loc.gov/item/2017851041/ (`8d28363v`). The item JSON timed out on re-read. The search record gives the rights as "No known restrictions", and the photographer name still has to be filled in for the credits.
  - Handmade: the door frame and open leaf in dark card, the typed CAPOREPARTO sign, the paper clock hands and the back-room paper.
- **Technique and motion:** #29 GrabCut cuts each figure from the photographs (mask hints and hand-scissor clip polygons are in `cuts.py`), and #1 Gilliam / #43 collage gives each one a jittered scissor edge, a pale paper rim and a lifted drop shadow. #32 FK pivots: Luciana's torso is rotated +6° about her waist and Renzo's −4° about his hips. In the film the busts rock on these pins, she takes three bites (the head and apple swap on 2s), and he alternates his head between the clock and her (#38 drawing substitution between two cuts). #45 Occlusion uses the real pail pile, extracted from the plate by a tone threshold with holes filled and re-composited on top, so the couple is really "behind the cassette". The foreman is mirrored so his back is to them in A. In B, the unmirrored cut is substituted at 12:03 (t = 42 s), so he turns toward the pails. The clock's paper hands are redrawn every frame from the fixture clock. The plate stays still, as on a collage stage, with a slow 1.0x/0.6x multiplane drift (#44) between the wall and the cut-outs.
- **Risk:** it is closest to the reference's medium (1940s–60s black-and-white photography), but its people are American war workers, not Italian 1962 workers. Renzo is a low-resolution enlargement, about 2x from a 1024 px file, so the 3,000 px TIFF masters (about 20–30 MB each) would be needed for the film. The cut-outs come from different lights, the grins at camera read as "posing", and the foreman's doorway is an obviously added card element. The pale lunch pail behind Renzo reads as a patch.

## 3 · Cartellini (light-box score: kinetic typography with handmade slotted cards and cut-paper lovers)
- **Beat / time:** the counterfactual slide, **t ≈ 30–34 s**, shown at about 33 s. Three slotted card strips are stacked on a light box under a typeset minute ruler from 12:00 to 12:08: Renzo's break, Luciana's break and the safe corner. Light passes only where all three slots coincide, and that lit slot is the meeting window, the stage on which two small cut-paper profile busts face each other. The frame shows B: Luciana's strip is sliding from [12:00,12:04) toward [12:03,12:07). It has travelled 2 of the 3 minutes, the window has shrunk to [12:02,12:03), and the moving edge is about to close it on them.
- **Material:** handmade. It has a grey board desk, three cream card strips with fibre grain, scissor-jitter edges and cast shadows, a light box frame, typed Italian tags (`RENZO pausa 12:00–12:03`, `LUCIANA … -> 12:03–12:07`, `ANGOLO accessibile 12:01–12:04 · dietro le cassette`), a ruler card, and a title in serif type ("Pausa delle dodici / B · la campanella suona in ritardo"). The profile busts are cut paper, drawn here, with Luciana's fringed hat.
- **Technique and motion:** kinetic typography with #6 Whitney harmonic motion as a score, where the light is computed every frame as the product of the strips' transmissions (each card absorbs 42 %). The coincident slot is therefore the exact intersection [12:01,12:03) in A and empty in B. This is the fixture, measured in pixels rather than asserted. #40 Physical tabletop capture is possible: the strips can be printed, cut and shot on a real light box. #45 Occlusion: the strips' opaque parts mask the lovers outside the window. Frame by frame: A holds the window open at 236 px (2 min) while the busts lean in (the heads tilt on pins, #32). At 30–34 s, Luciana's strip slides 3 min (354 px) on an eased curve, the window narrows to zero, and the closing edge pushes across her bust and occludes it. Renzo is left alone in a dark slot and the bell is heard. #47 Sound-to-action ties the bell to the moment the light reaches zero.
- **Risk:** it is the most legible view of the rule and the furthest from the reference's photographic intimacy. The couple is reduced to tokens, so it risks the "generic couple" failure (§8) unless it is cut against direction 1 or 2. The typography is explanatory, close to the exposition the thesis wants to avoid. Its best use may be as the "schedule and visibility graph" deliverable, or as a 4 s insert at the A/B cut.

## Recommendation
Build **2 · Collage al marcatempo** as the film.

It is the only direction that meets the reference on its own ground: black-and-white mid-tone photography of real people at a break, with the institution in the same frame. The cause of the rule is visible and countable: the clock, the doorway and the pail pile that hides them. Its animation is real cut-out puppetry on pins with occlusion and substitution, not a filter. The late-bell version needs only one change carried forward (the foreman's turn and the clock), which keeps A and B comparable frame by frame.

Before the build, the swarm should fetch the LoC TIFF masters for the Renzo and Luciana sources (within the ~150 MB per-work budget) and fill in the foreman's photographer from the item record.

Keep **1 · Il paravento** as the named alternative branch. It has the strongest single gesture (the IK hand that stays behind while the body obeys the bell), and it could become the B version's coda. Use **3 · Cartellini** as the 4 s A/B interstitial and as the visibility-graph deliverable, not as the film's body.
