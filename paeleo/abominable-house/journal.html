Journal Toward a Paper

Entries 1 to 12 are written today from the record. Entries 13 to 20 were observed today as they happened.

How the entries are kept

The method is taken from the author's notes on Calvino's journals: the page is an observation post, the writer stays out of the picture, one thing follows another, and the margins are firm. Four rules follow from it.

  1. One entry records one thing that was seen: a page, a number, a frame, a message.
  2. The entry says what was there. It does not say what it means.
  3. Each entry has the same parts and stays under a hundred words: what was seen, one line kept for later, and how it is known.
  4. Entries stand in the order of occurrence. No entry is moved to make an argument.

011930sBurbank

The sack

A half-filled flour sack is drawn on studio paper. It has no face, no arms, no legs. Dropped, it spreads. Lifted by its top corners, it lengthens. Its volume stays the same. Animators are asked to make it look tired, then proud, then afraid.

A body with nothing to act with except its weight.

The question came from a manufacturer and the answer went to a newsstand.

The index

A copybook survives a fire. Its pages are gone. Its back cover lists twelve deeds, from Blackmail to Strangling. The order is alphabetical. It is the only order left, and it says nothing about what happened first.

The fire left the alphabet and took the sequence.

The tower leans too late

Six agent studios animate one story: a sack sees a tower of flour lean and holds it. In five of the six first films the sack reacts at 6.7 seconds to a tower that has moved less than one degree. A fresh critic sees a sack startled by nothing. The repair is the same each time: the tower gives way first.

The cause has to be visible before the reaction is.

The penny

The script says the penny drops. Four studios throw it upward so the sack can catch it. One lets it drift at a capped speed. Each method arranges the catch in advance. The line that is easiest to write is the hardest to animate.

Every method wanted to help the penny.

Belindo, the brute

Simulated tenants choose their deeds. When they choose for practical reasons, a violent deed goes to whoever has a motive. Given a role, the wrestler does what wrestlers are said to do.

Not yet seen

These entries cannot be written until someone looks.

  1. A person who has not read the story watches one deed without its chapter card and says what was done.
  2. The printed Parks translation is opened at the narrator's total.
  3. The transcript of the sessions of 30 October to 6 November is read at one critic round: the verdict, the repair, the second look.
  4. Lem's preface is read for the semaphore.
  5. The same twelve deeds are asked of a video model with human figures, and what it answers is written down.
  6. A bank film is watched to its last frame to see what the house looks like when the fire comes.

The paper is whatever these entries turn out to be about once there are enough of them. Twenty is not enough.
