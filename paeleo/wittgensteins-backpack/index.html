Wittgenstein’s Backpack

cool radio, BOXBACK, track 18 of 18. A three-minute electro-funk song about moving day. A paper puppet hauls boxes up a four-storey walkup in Midtown Atlanta, and at every cut the box does a different job: a load, a private interior nobody can check, a rung, a trap, a rig that will not solve, and finally the thing the mover climbs into. The film is a run of language-games, in the sense of Philosophical Investigations §23, which counts play-acting, singing and joking among them.

Watson Hartsoe · 3 min · paper puppet, cut-out collage, Sketchpad rig

The film

The times below start the film at that point. Watch on YouTube. Play the six games.

Five movements, five games

Left: what is on screen. Right: the thought experiment it stages, in paraphrase, with the section to read.

Game 1

The stairs

A cardboard box inside a grocery bag inside a Jansport, carried up a four-storey walkup in Midtown Atlanta. The card reads BOX SCORE: ME 0 / STAIRS 12. It returns at 1:19 with a lumbar belt.

PI §1, §2, §107

The grocer and the builders. In the builders’ language a word is an order to fetch a thing; nobody asks what it names. On the stairs “box” works the same way: it is a load. §107 asks for friction, and the stairs supply it.

Game 2

Everyone has a box

Five puppets march in step. Inside their boxes: a bug, the word BEETLE, a frog, a smaller box, and nothing.

PI §293, §304

The beetle in the box. If nobody can look in anyone else’s box, what is inside drops out of the game, and the box might even be empty. The puppets keep marching whatever they carry. The song lays Latour’s black box, and the package in Se7en, over the same picture.

Game 3

The word-stairs

BOX, KNOX, FOX, SOCKS, CLOCKS, BLOCKS stack up, after Fox in Socks, until they are a staircase. The puppet climbs it and kicks it away under the card THROW AWAY THE LADDER.

Notebooks 29.9.14 · Tractatus 6.54 · Culture and Value

In 1914 Wittgenstein noted a Paris court that modelled a car accident with dolls: a proposition as a world assembled on trial. The Tractatus ends by calling its own sentences rungs to be discarded once climbed. Later he wrote that a place only a ladder reaches was not where he needed to be.

Game 4

Rabbit, duck, fly

A wooden head that is a rabbit, then a duck. Then a fly in a glass bottle and the card SHOW THE FLY THE WAY OUT.

PI part II, xi · PI §309

The duck-rabbit: the drawing does not change, the seeing does, and calling out which animal it is reports that change. §309 gives the aim of philosophy as showing the fly out of the bottle. Here the pun does it.

Game 5

Constraint mode

The curtain drops on a green vector rig labelled SKETCHPAD // CONSTRAINT MODE. The pinned figure reaches past its limb lengths and the solver answers UNSOLVABLE: nothing stretched.

PI §193–194 · Sutherland, Sketchpad (1963)

The machine as a symbol of its own action: in a kinematic drawing the parts are rigid by stipulation and can only move one way. Real cardboard buckles and real backs ache. The lyric widens the containers from box to womb and tomb.

Read alongside

  • Ludwig Wittgenstein, Philosophical Investigations (1953): §1–2 the grocer and the builders; §18 the old city; §23 the list of language-games; §39 and §60 parts and wholes; §43 meaning as use; §107–108 the rough ground; §115 the captive picture; §142 the cheese on the scale; §193–194 the machine as symbol; §217 bedrock; §293 the beetle; §309 the fly-bottle; part II, xi, the duck-rabbit.
  • Wittgenstein, Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus (1921): 4.014 record and score; 5.632 the subject as limit; 6.54 the ladder; 7 silence. Notebooks 1914–1916, entry for 29 September 1914: the Paris court model. Culture and Value: the remark against ladders.
  • Bruno Latour, Science in Action (1987): the black box, a machine or fact that has been closed so that only its inputs and outputs count.
  • Ivan Sutherland, Sketchpad (1963): drawing by constraints, and what a solver does when the constraints cannot all hold.
  • Ursula K. Le Guin, “The Carrier Bag Theory of Fiction” (1986): the first tool as a container, and a story as a bag.
  • Dr. Seuss, Fox in Socks (1965); David Fincher, Se7en (1995); Jastrow’s duck-rabbit; Terry Gilliam’s cut-out foot.
