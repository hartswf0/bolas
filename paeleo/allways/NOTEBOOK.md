# BETWEEN TAKES

## Notes from developing ALLWAYS

These are working propositions from the project's writing process. They can be revised by a film, a source, or a viewer's objection.

### The occasion

A camera points at an event. More happens than the event can contain.

### The unnamed

We can meet a gesture before we know a name. A name remains worth finding.

### The interval

A moment does not have to explain a life to be worth keeping.

### The match

The cut belongs to the editor. The lives on either side do not.

### The blink

An eye that never blinks can still miss what matters. Attention includes the pause, the return, and the decision to look elsewhere.

### The room

The room remains. That does not tell us everything about who left.

### The gap

Leaving an interval open can be the most exact decision in the film.

### The machine

A system can propose more neighbors than we can watch. The next task is choosing which encounters deserve the time of another person.

## A line kept in question

“Everything that happens to no one in particular.”

The line captures the freedom of an event that does not need to serve a protagonist. In a source record, however, someone in particular was there. The work should hold both thoughts without letting the first erase the second.
