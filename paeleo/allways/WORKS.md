# ALLWAYS development catalogue

**ALLWAYS is a cinema practice within PAELEO.** Its archive-based series is **B-ROLL OF CIVILIZATION**, its editorial principle is **NEARMATCH**, and its method library is **SCION**.

These twelve works are development concepts. A written operator, logline, or proposed proof is not a completed film. The source films named in SCION are artistic and operational lineages, not ALLWAYS productions.

> NEARMATCH: resemblance gives us a reason to look; difference gives us a reason to keep looking.

Each first proof is intentionally bounded. Select one primary operator and at most two supporting methods; the primary method governs conflicting constraints. The catalogue does not assert that source material has been acquired, a required adapter exists, or a prototype has passed review.

## NOBODY HOME

**Concept · Rooms after erasure**

Families disappear from their home movies, but their effects remain: chairs rock, doors close, and birthday candles go out in empty rooms.

**First proof.** Build a sixty-second study from one newly recorded room sequence: a person enters, places a cup, moves a chair, and leaves. Show an intact passage, then return to a version with the person removed while the chair movement remains. Hold the empty aftermath with room sound. End with an accessible original/derived comparison. Keep the mask and any synthesized background separate from recorded pixels, so the intervention can be inspected.

**Methods.** Primary: SCION 085 · Jeanne Dielman — Routine as a Sensitive Measuring Instrument. Supporting: SCION 067 · (nostalgia) — Anticipated Image, Vanishing Evidence; SCION 097 · Welcome to Pine Point — Remembered Place Scrapbook.

**Development boundary.** Requires an explicit person-removal and compositing adapter. Removal is an authored intervention; it does not establish that the historical room was empty.

## ALMOST HUMAN

**Concept · Unfinished gestures**

People hesitate, adjust their clothes, check a wrist, and look away. Their small unfinished gestures become a performance no one planned together.

**First proof.** Make a seventy-five-second sequence from six situated gesture excerpts. Begin with one complete collar adjustment, including the pause afterward. Let another person begin a similar movement, then preserve their different outcome. Build a brief rhythm across three further gestures before allowing one participant to stop halfway. Return to the first setting after its action has ended. Use clothing, breath, and room sounds to carry the rhythm without assigning a shared emotional state.

**Methods.** Primary: SCION 057 · Moments — Microevent Attention. Supporting: SCION 084 · Pickpocket — Object Transfer as Dramatic Grammar; SCION 085 · Jeanne Dielman — Routine as a Sensitive Measuring Instrument.

**Development boundary.** The comparison concerns visible actions. It must preserve source circumstances rather than infer identical motives or a universal account of humanity.

## TERRA INCOGNITA

**Concept · Material territories**

A passage through film’s damaged edges, where scratches, light leaks, and blank intervals become terrain and the recorded world keeps surfacing.

**First proof.** Build a sixty-second passage from three recorded film surfaces with different visible defects. Establish a recognizable image before following a scratch or light boundary into the next fragment. Hold one surface long enough to reveal that the apparent landscape is a material carrier. Let a recognizable scene return, then end at a blank interval with its actual recorded noise or an identified original sound composition. Keep observed damage distinct from any generated extension.

**Methods.** Primary: SCION 071 · Standard Gauge — The Carrier as Evidence. Supporting: SCION 073 · A Walk Through H — Narrated Passage Between Maps; SCION 113 · Learning to See — Learned Prior as Visible Transformation.

**Development boundary.** A generated transition is a separate authored layer. The material-study proof does not establish a historical relationship between otherwise unrelated fragments.

## SPLIT SECOND

**Concept · Action and consequence**

Hands release, doors close, and feet leave the ground. Unrelated recordings converge at the instant an action becomes a consequence.

**First proof.** Create a fifty-second sequence around release, using six bounded excerpts with readable beginnings and outcomes. Twice cut away immediately before a hand lets go. On the third return, allow the object’s actual consequence to arrive. Align later actions by their visible phase, then let one longer hesitation resist the pattern. End on an ordinary release after the larger consequences have changed its weight. Preserve contact sounds and disclose the montage’s separate sources.

**Methods.** Primary: SCION 084 · Pickpocket — Object Transfer as Dramatic Grammar. Supporting: SCION 069 · A Movie — Borrowed Continuity, Exposed Construction; SCION 082 · First Position — Practice, Constraint, and Public Exposure.

**Development boundary.** Match action phases, not alleged identical inner decisions or historical simultaneity. The primary operator’s action grammar requires an explicit adaptation for an archival montage.

## CUT TO THE CHASE

**Concept · A shifting pursuit**

Fleeing, chasing, training, stumbling, and stopping form a shifting pursuit across cinema, until the movement itself becomes the story.

**First proof.** Build a sixty-second pursuit from eight excerpts. Establish a runner’s direction, then transfer that momentum across two clearly different settings. Interrupt the rhythm with someone waiting or recovering, retaining their local sound. Resume with an action that changes the apparent relationship between pursuing and being pursued. Finish on a person who stops while the surrounding world continues moving. Compare the cut with and without sound bridges to determine which transitions carry the pursuit.

**Methods.** Primary: SCION 091 · Watchtower of Turkey — Momentum Across an Honest Discontinuity. Supporting: SCION 069 · A Movie — Borrowed Continuity, Exposed Construction; SCION 083 · Berlin: Symphony of a Great City — Civic Rhythm with Unequal Pulses.

**Development boundary.** The earlier ninety-minute proposal remains a development ambition. This first proof must establish changing relationships and rest before a feature duration is justified.

## KINETIC

**Concept · Labor and interruption**

Work repeats across factories, workshops, and homes. Its mechanical rhythms are interrupted by maintenance, waiting, fatigue, and refusal.

**First proof.** Make a ninety-second passage from three documented work processes. Establish a repeated movement in each, then briefly coordinate their rhythms through an original sound phrase. Break that coordination by preserving one complete maintenance or waiting interval at normal speed. Return to the first process after the interruption, revealing something the tighter framing initially excluded. End when one worker or mechanism stops. Keep contextual views available so the shared rhythm does not erase different conditions.

**Methods.** Primary: SCION 083 · Berlin: Symphony of a Great City — Civic Rhythm with Unequal Pulses. Supporting: SCION 079 · The Qatsi Trilogy — Three Regimes of Technological Time; SCION 078 · Samsara — Material Cycles and Changed Returns.

**Development boundary.** Do not compress the Qatsi trilogy into one time-lapse style or infer fatigue and refusal from appearance alone. Include those conditions only when the source supports them.

## ZERO MEMORY

**Concept · An editor forgets**

An editor repeatedly forgets the sequence it has just made, while its archive remains available. The audience begins to remember more of the film than its maker.

**First proof.** Use a fixed collection of twelve short excerpts to build a ninety-second experiment. Give the editor access to the same collection throughout, but reset its declared working history at three visible boundaries. Keep a separate audit log inaccessible to that editing state. Allow repeated selections to remain, then place an intact recurrence beside a changed one in the final passage. Sound should make returning material recognizable without pretending that the underlying model has forgotten its training.

**Methods.** Primary: SCION 106 · Network Effect — Bounded Attention, Unfinishable Archive. Supporting: SCION 067 · (nostalgia) — Anticipated Image, Vanishing Evidence.

**Development boundary.** Requires a new memory-reset policy and external audit log. Persistent archive access, model training, working history, and the spectator’s memory are different things.

## HOMONYM

**Concept · Words change worlds**

A word passes between voices and images, changing sense at each cut until speech seems to edit the world around it.

**First proof.** Construct a sixty-second relay around one word with three visually legible senses. Establish its first use in an intact spoken context, then let an action carry the next meaning before another voice names it. Make the third sense alter the sequence’s tone or stakes. Return to the opening utterance with the new associations still active. Keep original speech intelligible, and test a version without explanatory captions before adding another semantic branch.

**Methods.** Primary: SCION 055 · Words — Polysemic Relay. Supporting: SCION 092 · Hands of Bresson — Gesture Evidence Before a Generalization.

**Development boundary.** A semantic connection needs a defensible interpretation beyond a similarity score. An assembled utterance must be presented as an authored construction, not attributed to a recorded speaker as something they actually said.

## ANYBODY, ANYWHERE

**Concept · Lives in parallel**

Strangers in different places perform related ordinary acts. Paired images reveal both their resemblance and the circumstances that keep their lives distinct.

**First proof.** Build a ninety-second comparison from three pairs of situated recordings. Start with two people preparing a place to sit, allowing a moment of recognizable alignment. Let one person finish while the other waits or changes the task. Restore a wider contextual view on each side before the next pair. Conclude with two actions whose resemblance fails at the outcome. Preserve distinct sound spaces and show recording dates or unknown-date states where relevant.

**Methods.** Primary: SCION 056 · Symmetry — Imperfect Pairing. Supporting: SCION 099 · Out My Window — Situated Panorama of Voices. SCION 080 · Life in a Day is reserved for a shared-date edition.

**Development boundary.** Parallel presentation does not establish simultaneous recording. The initial proof preserves different times and conditions. SCION 080 is reserved for a later shared-date edition with adequate timestamp evidence.

## COLLECTIVE INVENTORIES

**Concept · A composite day**

A day is composed from many people’s ordinary work, with one gesture taking up where another leaves off.

**First proof.** Make a two-minute composite morning from five situated participants or source records. Give each one intact practical action before connecting them through washing, carrying, preparing, or clearing away. Let a handoff between excerpts suggest continuation, then widen the frame to reveal its changed circumstances. Preserve one task that remains unfinished. End by returning to the earliest participant after their action, with a different pace and sound. Identify the assembled day as an editorial construction.

**Methods.** Primary: SCION 065 · Five — Parallel Lives, Unequal Details. Supporting: SCION 084 · Pickpocket — Object Transfer as Dramatic Grammar; SCION 092 · Hands of Bresson — Gesture Evidence Before a Generalization.

**Development boundary.** The documentary primary governs source treatment. A shared action sequence does not make the recordings one documented day or their participants interchangeable.

## MNEMONIL

**Concept · A borrowed life**

A life story assembled from home movies begins to contradict itself: familiar rooms, gestures, and recollections belong to different people.

**First proof.** Create a two-minute fictional composite from four clearly attributed source passages and original narration. Begin with an apparently coherent account of returning to a room. Introduce a second recording that resembles the room but contradicts a concrete detail. Let a later voice or image expose the difference rather than repairing it. Finish by showing how the sequence borrowed its continuity. Keep actual testimony separate from invented biography, and retain the identities of the underlying source records.

**Methods.** Primary: SCION 074 · The Falls — A World Emerging From a Directory. Supporting: SCION 067 · (nostalgia) — Anticipated Image, Vanishing Evidence; SCION 097 · Welcome to Pine Point — Remembered Place Scrapbook.

**Development boundary.** The Library of Congress setting remains an earlier fictional proposition, not a claim of institutional involvement or an established source collection.

## PLANIMETRIC

**Concept · Held frames**

Locked-off images turn rooms and public spaces into stages where routine, background activity, and a small deviation become the event.

**First proof.** Stage or select one ninety-second fixed view of a room with two practical activities at different depths. Establish their ordinary relation before a small obstruction changes one person’s route. Hold the frame while the second activity continues, letting the viewer discover the conflict through timing and sound. End on the changed arrangement without a rescuing close-up. If staged, present it as fiction; if archival, preserve the actual action and camera behavior.

**Methods.** Primary: SCION 086 · Songs from the Second Floor — A Tableau That Accumulates Pressure. Supporting: SCION 085 · Jeanne Dielman — Routine as a Sensitive Measuring Instrument; SCION 089 · Wes Anderson / Robert Yeoman — Geometry That Times an Encounter.

**Development boundary.** Static, centered, and planimetric are separate constraints. The fixed-view proof takes precedence over supporting operators that permit camera moves.

## NOBODY HOME: the source and the intervention

The central proposal is computational erasure. An altered frame does not replace the source recording or establish an empty historical room. Keep the intact source, person mask, transformation decisions, and synthesized background distinguishable. A comparison edition can reveal the intervention while the main film develops its atmosphere. Naturally empty passages before an entrance or after an exit form a separate duration study; they should not silently substitute for the removal premise.

## Studies and formats

### LATENT

**Navigable archive study.** A proposed environment for following modeled relationships between source records. Navigation changes the encounter; coordinates belong to a chosen representation, not an intrinsic geography of memory.

SCION starting points: 095, 102, 103.

### THE VECTOR POET

**Associative editing study.** Search proposes visual, sonic, or semantic neighbors; an editor tests whether their relation develops meaning and preserves context. Similarity scores nominate candidates rather than settle the cut.

SCION starting points: 055, 059, 062, 092.

### THE EYE-TRACE ARCHIVE

**Composition and attention study.** A proposed record of screen-space anchors, motion, and paths of attention through edits. Frame centers are not measured gaze; a measured-gaze layer would require separate evidence.

SCION starting points: 059, 068, 091, 104.

### OPERATIVE CINEMA — Archival Bridges

**Synthetic transition study.** Develops the original proposal to bridge documentary fragments with generated passages. Keep the recorded endpoints and the synthetic interval distinguishable and inspectable; test what the bridge changes instead of treating seamlessness as historical truth.

SCION starting points: 113, 069, 073.

### ANYRECORD

**Intake and catalogue study.** A broad invitation to consider discarded film, audio, still images, and other recordings. Equal consideration does not make every record equally informative or interchangeable.

SCION starting points: 071, 100, 105.

### DOUBLETAKE

**Comparative edition format.** A second viewing, alternate cut, or source/derivative comparison changes the first encounter. Keep the source pool explicit so viewers can inspect what the edit contributes.

SCION starting points: 093, 094.

## Collections and proposed series

- **B-ROLL OF CIVILIZATION** — The central archive-based series: unfinished gestures, routine work, rooms around an event, and material that remains when a scene is over.
- **EQUIVALENTS** — A proposed collection of correspondences in form, action, and affect, with differences left available for attention rather than compressed into sameness.
- **UNWITNESSED** — A proposed collection about backgrounds, recorded absence, and unattended processes. The title does not certify that nobody previously viewed a source.
- **MEANWHILE** — An editorial view of what another place, person, or process is doing beyond the present frame. Parallel presentation need not mean simultaneous recording.
- **COMMONTHREAD** — A proposed series preserving the original epic ambition: gather the ordinary tissue between plots—care, maintenance, waiting, and shared tasks—into sustained films whose connections retain particular lives.
- **EVERDAY** — A proposed recurring or looping form organized around the ordinary day. Its intentional spelling joins daily recurrence and duration; no release schedule is implied.

COMMONTHREAD retains its original ambition to make an epic from the ordinary tissue between plots. It is a proposed series, not merely a search tag. Works can belong to several collections while retaining one canonical record.

Store the artifact and actual review finding alongside the status; publishing a prompt or webpage does not release a film.
