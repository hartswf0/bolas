# PAE-03 · The flour tower, handmade: the brief every swarm receives

PEO is Prompt · Ekphrasis · Operation. PAE-02 held one description of a dog at a threshold still and let each SCION swarm build the scene from the bottom up. PAE-03 does the same with the oldest test in the animation studio: the Disney flour sack (1930s), a body with no face, no limbs and no fingers, whose mood has to read through weight, silhouette, line of action, and four corners that drag behind it. The test's gold standard was a sack that *thinks before it acts*. This brief gives the sack a decision to make.

## The treatment (the North Star)

A half-empty flour sack sits at the edge of a parlor table while a knife slices away at a tower of packed flour with a penny balanced on top, the old parlor game. The sack recognises the tower as flour like its own; it cannot stop the knife, so it does the only thing a sack can do: it holds the tower up, catches the penny when the tower falls, and gathers the scattered flour back into itself until it stands where the tower stood, full, with the penny on its top.

Babel in reverse: the tower is built to make a name, cut down, and scattered; the sack gathers what was scattered into one body. Flour's oldest history is the same motion: grain crushed, sifted, gathered.

**Energy arc.**
- Act 1 (0–9 s), staccato: the knife comes down in hard, fast cuts; each cut is a flinch; the sack is slumped and small.
- Act 2 (9–21 s), sustained: the sack crosses the table slowly, head low, and leans its whole body against the leaning tower; it braces under the next cut.
- Act 3 (21–32 s), surprising and inevitable: the tower falls, the sack leaps and catches the penny, then fills itself from the pile, rising to full height. The knife comes back, finds the penny on a sack, and withdraws.

## The chronicle (the ekphrasis you must operate)

> A table. On a plate, right of centre, a tower of packed flour, a penny balanced on its top. At the far left edge of the table, a flour sack, half empty, slumped low, its corners down.
> A knife comes down and cuts a slice from the tower. Flour sprays. The sack flinches back from the cut. Therefore it watches; the knife comes down again, on the other side, and the sack flinches again, less.
> The tower begins to lean. Flour sifts off its cut face onto the plate. The sack's corners lift. It recognises what the tower is made of. Therefore it comes, slowly, head low, across the table, and stops against the tower's leaning side and leans its whole body into it, spreading wide to hold it.
> The knife comes down a third time. The sack braces and holds. Therefore the knife cuts a fourth time, on the far side, and the tower comes apart. The penny drops. The sack leaps, stretched long, and the penny lands on its top. It comes down hard in a squash, the penny still on it, in a cloud of flour.
> The sack stays still. Then it gathers: the flour on the plate draws into it, and it fills, rising from slumped and half empty to full and tall, standing where the tower stood, the penny on its top.
> The knife comes back. It hovers over the penny. It withdraws.

The viewer must be able to understand the sack and not control it. Cuts push it away; the tower's lean and fall bring it in.

## Invariants (the PAELEO test for this brief)

1. The ten choices happen at their times, ±0.25 s, and read **as choices on screen**: flinching, flinching less, noticing, coming head low, propping, bracing, leaping, catching, gathering, staying.
2. **Volume.** Through every squash and stretch the sack keeps its volume (±6%). Its volume changes only during the gather (24.5–28.5), and then the flour on the plate shrinks by the amount the sack grows: flour is conserved between tower, pile and sack.
3. **The corners drag.** The four corners (two visible ears are enough) trail the body and catch up a few frames later (overlapping action).
4. **Cuts push it away; circumstances bring it in.** The flinches go left, away from the knife; the approach begins only after the tower visibly leans.
5. It ends standing at the tower's place (x ≈ +0.8), full height, the penny on its top, and the knife leaves without cutting it.
6. **No face, no labels doing the acting.** No eyes, no mouth, no state names on screen during the scene.

Everything else is yours: what the sack, the tower, the knife, the table and the room are made of, how anything moves, the camera, the palette, the sound.

## Build from the bottom up, in your swarm's language

You are one SCION swarm. Your lineage's **native formal language** is what you build with, and what you build *the sack itself* with. The lesson of PAE-02, cohort 2: three films failed or narrowly passed because the world was in the swarm's language but the character was a generic shape with a style laid over it. Do not do that. Do not draw one generic sack outline and restyle it. The way the sack is *constructed* and the way it is *moved* must both come from your lineage (pins and substitution; harmonic rates; constraint solving; drawing selection; contour correspondence; simulated cloth and grains; ...). Another swarm is building the same scene at the same time in a completely different way; you will not see its work and it will not see yours.

What every PAE-02 critic looked for first, applied to the sack:
- **Weight.** The bottom pools under gravity; the top carries tension. A squash that does not stretch back reads as a water balloon.
- **Line of action.** The sack's spine curve must be legible in every pose.
- **Thinking before acting.** The notice (6.5) must happen before the approach (9.0), and the approach must read as decided, not drifted.
- **Head low.** During the approach the top of the sack sits visibly below its shoulder line (it leans forward and down).
- **Nothing slides while held.** If the sack moves along the table, its shuffle, hops or drawings must account for the travel.
- **The prop must read as holding.** The sack's body is pressed into the tower's side, and the tower's lean stops because of it.
- **The catch.** The penny must visibly land on the sack's top and stay there through the landing squash.
- **The gather.** Flour visibly leaves the pile and enters the sack; the sack grows; the pile shrinks.
- **Claims must be true.** If your "made of" card says nothing is tweened, nothing may be tweened.
