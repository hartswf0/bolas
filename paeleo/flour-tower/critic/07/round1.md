Flinch (1.0): reads. The knife lands at 1.00. From 1.04 to 1.25 the sack squashes (top 0.47 to 0.44) and slides left to x = -1.48, then rises back. Its ears trail by a few frames. Flinch less (4.0): reads. The slide is 0.06 m against 0.18 m for the first flinch, and the squash is visibly shallower (frames 96–106). Notice (6.5): reads as a gesture. At 6.67–7.0 the ears rise, the body tilts right toward the tower, and the sack holds that pose through 8.9, so the thought comes before the action. The cause is weak, though. The tower barely leans: its angle is 0.001 rad at 7.0 and 0.012 rad at 9.0, under one degree. What carries the "tower is failing" cue is a few sifting flour strokes. Brief invariant 4 asks that the approach begin only after the tower *visibly* leans, and at 9.0 it does not. Come, head low (9.0): reads, and it is the best passage in the film. From 9.1 the contour becomes a wedge whose high point is the rear and whose front is low toward the tower. The travel is a true inchworm. My contact extraction shows the rear ground corner and the front ground corner planting alternately: at 9.67–10.0 the front stays at -0.963 while the rear moves up, then the front advances while the rear stays fixed. Nothing slides. Prop (14.5): reads as holding. From 14.4 the sack's right flank lies flat along the tower's left face, ears raised, and the tower's lean does not change between 14.6 and 17.9. Because the lean was never large (about 5 degrees), "the lean stops because of it" is only faintly visible. The pose is tall and narrow against the tower, not "spread wide". Brace (18.0): weak. Across frames 431–446 the sack's outline barely changes. The top drops 2.4 cm and the body moves 1.6 cm *back* (x 0.397 to 0.381), so it retreats slightly instead of holding or pushing. The spray and the knife carry the moment, not the sack. Leap (21.4): reads. At 21.42–21.5 the body stretches up and leans toward the falling penny, and it is airborne by 21.6. Catch (22.4): reads. The penny settles into the dip between the ears at 22.0–22.3, near the apex, and rides the whole descent. Land (23.0): the squash is hard and the ears snap up a few frames late (22.92–23.04), which is good overlapping action. Gather (24.5): reads. From 24.6 the two humps of the pile visibly shrink while short blue-grey strokes and faint ghost contours travel into the sack. The sack rises steadily to 0.95 by 28.5 and the pile is gone by 28.4. Stay (30.0): reads. The knife enters at 29.5, hovers directly over the penny from 29.9 to 31.1, and withdraws at 31.25. The sack does not move a single point.

Is it a flour sack in every frame? Mostly yes. At rest (0–6 s) the bottom is wide and planted, the sides pinch in, and two ear corners flare up. That reads as a half-empty sack, though it is very symmetric and close to a vase. During the approach the weight sits low and the spine runs clearly from the rear corner down to the front. The full sack (28.5–32) is a tall pillow with small tied corners, which reads as full and heavy. It fails in two places. (a) At frames 555–559 (23.13–23.29) the recovery from the landing squash draws a top much wider than the base, with the ears thrown out horizontally like a mushroom cap or a bowl lip. Weight sits at the top, and there is no line of action. (b) At 21.5–21.8 the airborne stretch is also top-wide and tilted. That is more forgivable in the air, but it reads as a cup more than a stretched sack. Everywhere else the bottom pools.

Lineage. The sack is genuinely built and moved in the lineage's language, not restyled. I verified the card claims. Every frame of the sack is a 64-point closed contour blended from 22 drawn keys, using the landmark indices on the card (bottom 0, corners 22 and 42, top 32). My own segment test finds 0 of 768 sack frames self-crossing, which matches PAE_CHECK. The rescale to hold the area is disclosed on the card; the raw blend drifts between 0.966 and 1.081 before it. The final contour deviates at most 1.55 cm from the uncut tower's own outline. The Foldès consequence lands: the thing that gathered the tower ends up drawn as the tower, with the penny where the tower kept it. The pen line boils slightly, like a hand-redrawn line. The film is honestly "line drawings": the near-paper fills only mask lines behind them.

Craft defects. (1) The penny rises when the tower falls: its y goes from 0.97 to 1.31 between 21.0 and 22.0, up to the wall rail, and the chronicle says "the penny drops". It reads as a flick from the knife, not a fall the sack has to meet. (2) The tower's lean, the circumstance that is supposed to bring the sack in, is essentially invisible before 9 s (under 1 degree) and only about 5 degrees at the prop. (3) The slabs cut off at 1.0 and 4.0 fall as rigid planks and then simply vanish at the table edge around 1.5 and 4.6 s, so the "flour is conserved" story has a visible hole. PAE_CHECK accounts for them as "slicesOffPlate", but the picture shows them disappearing. (4) The fallen pile is two symmetric humps with a valley exactly where the sack will land (22.0 on). That reads as a construction made to match the sack's corner indices, not as a heap a tower would make. (5) The brace does nothing a viewer can see (above). (6) The mushroom recovery at 23.13–23.29 (above). Phone layout at 390x844: the film is in the top half, controls and cards are below, and there is no page scroll or horizontal overflow. It passes.

Verdict: PASS (narrow). Nine of the ten choices read on screen. The approach is very good: corresponding-contour inchworm locomotion with no sliding, and a decided head-low wedge. The metamorphosis into the tower's outline is true and is the film's point. The narrow margin comes from the brace not reading, the barely visible lean that is meant to motivate the approach, and a top-heavy recovery right after the landing.

Repair conditions, ranked.
1. Tower lean (6.5–9.0): by 9.0 the tower must lean at least 4 degrees toward the sack, a tilt visible in a still frame, and that lean must be what the prop then stops (no further change 14.6–17.9). Falsified if the tower angle at 9.0 is below 0.07 rad or the lean is not visible at 1280x720.
2. Brace (18.0–18.6): the sack must visibly compress (top drop of at least 6 cm and/or a widened, lowered base), push its flank into the tower, and not move back. Falsified if sack x at 18.5 is less than at 17.9, or if frames 18.0 and 18.4 are indistinguishable at contact-sheet size.
3. Landing recovery (23.1–23.3): no frame may have the top wider than the base, so the weight stays pooled. Falsified if any frame from 23.0 to 23.5 has a contour width at 0.8 x height greater than its width at the base.
4. Penny (21.0–22.4): the penny must fall from the tower top or be knocked only sideways, never rising above y = 1.0. The sack's leap must meet it on the way down. Falsified if the trace's penny y exceeds 1.0 at any t in 21.0–22.4.
5. Slices (1.0–1.6, 4.0–4.6): the cut slabs must land and stay as flour on the table or plate, or visibly break into the spray. They must not vanish while still drawn as whole slabs. Falsified if a slab outline is present in one frame and absent in the next with no break-up.
