# SCION 14 · critic round 1 · PASS (narrow)

Method. I rendered the page headless at 1280×720 through window.PAE_SEEK and took stills every 0.5 s from 0 to 32 s and every frame in the ten dense windows (329 stills). I built full-frame and cropped contact sheets, looked at single frames at 11.0, 16.0, 21.583 and 23.0 at full size, and checked the 390×844 layout at 15.5 s. I judged the picture before reading PAE_CHECK. I read the source only for the seek hook and to test the card claims. Files are in /home/claude/pae03/critic/14/ (frames/, dense/, sheets/, trace.json, check.json, phone.png).

The ten choices. Flinch (1.00) reads. On the knife's contact frame the sack squashes, then hops left in an airborne drawing with its ears thrown back to the right, lands at about x −1.45 by 1.33 and settles by 1.9. Flinch less (4.00) reads. It uses the same squash and a smaller hop of 5 cm, and the dip is visibly shallower. Notice (6.50) reads as an action but its cause is weak. Both ears go up and the body leans right at 6.50, and a second drawing at 7.25 and a flattened "resolve" drawing at 8.42 make a real think-then-decide beat. However, the tower's lean at 6.5–9.5 is 3 cm and the sifting flour is a few faint dots, so the viewer barely sees what the sack is reacting to. Come head low (9.00–14.0) reads as decided, steady travel. It is an inchworm gait on threes, both base corners pinned in turn, arriving at x 0.40 at 14.0. The front of the sack is lower than its back hump. The cost is that the sack stops reading as a sack here (see below). The silhouette also swings between tall-round and flat-wide every three frames (9.25 → 9.375 → 9.5), which reads as boiling more than shuffling. Prop (14.50) reads as a pose but weakly as an effort. The wedge drawing D24 sets its flat right flank flush against the tower's leaning face, and the tower straightens by about 3.5 cm on the same frame. After that, one drawing is held without change for 84 frames (14.50–18.00), and nothing in the sack shows that it is pushing. Brace (18.00) reads. On the cut frame it switches to a lower, wider drawing (top falls from about 0.66 to 0.52) and does not move back. That drawing is then held dead for 72 frames until 21.0. Leap (21.42) reads, after a good anticipation crouch from 21.00 to 21.42. The stretched drawing is clearly long. Hang time at the apex (21.83–22.40, about 0.6 s at 0.58 m up) feels floaty, but the score asks for it. Catch (22.42) reads clearly. The catch drawing raises both ears either side of the penny, and the penny stays seated through the fall, the wide land squash at 23.00 (ears flung out, flour cloud) and the stretch-back recover at 23.33. Gather (24.50–28.50) reads, slowly. Seven drawings of increasing height and smoothness (fold lines drop out as the cloth tightens) are matched to pile drawings whose flanks visibly sink. The stream of grains is faint, and the sack covers most of the mound, so the pile's shrinking shows only at its edges. Stay (30.00–31.20) reads. The knife comes down to about 14 cm above the penny and wavers between two drawings on fours, the sack perks its corners once at 30.00 (F2) and is otherwise still, the knife smears up at 31.2, and the sack relaxes back to F1 at 31.58. It ends at x 0.80, top 0.93, with the penny on.

Does it read as a flour sack in every frame? No. It does in the rest, flinch, watch, notice, brace, crouch, land, recover and early-gather drawings (0–9.0, 18.0–21.4, 23.0–26.0): two corner ears, weight pooled in a dark wide base, a clear spine. It fails in four places. (1) All eight shuffle drawings, 9.0–14.0 (see 9.375, 11.0, 12.5): the two ears collapse into a single beak at the front low edge, and the body reads as a potato or stone rolling forward, not a sack with its head down. Because the corners lead the motion rather than trail it, the ears do not drag during the approach. (2) The leap drawing D28, 21.42–21.83: a hooked cone with one ear, which reads like a bird or a penguin. (3) Prop D24, 14.5–18.0: the far ear is hidden behind the tower and the near one is a small spike, so it reads as a wedge or a paper bag. (4) F1/F2, 28.5–32: a near-rectangle whose bottom is no wider than its top, the ears reduced to tick marks. That reads as a paper grocery bag, with no weight pooled low and no line of action. As a result, the drawings are not consistently on model with each other: the rest/flinch family, the shuffle family, the leap, and the full family look like three or four different characters that share an ink line and fold marks.

Lineage language. Construction is genuinely the lineage's. The bank has 44 separately authored point outlines, each with its own folds; 40 are used and every frame shows one whole drawing. Grounded drawings do not slide: the pinned-corner placement holds, and the trace confirms 0 mm of held slide. The approach's travel is accounted for by alternating contacts. Exposures are twos, threes on the approach. Airborne drawings are re-placed along an arc but never reshaped. The "nothing tweened" claim is true for shape, with two disclosed qualifications. First, each drawing gets one fixed vertical trim of 0.925–1.268 at load so that its area matches its class, so some authored drawings are shown stretched up to 27%. Second, the brace drawings' flank points are computed to fit the tower's face rather than drawn. Selection, the movement half of the lineage, is mostly nominal. The cue sheet names a pose family for every frame, and the family-mismatch term (6) dominates the pose distance, so across 768 frames the path never leaves the named family. Fourteen of the 22 families have only one drawing. The Viterbi path does real work only inside the shuffle cycle (which is hard-constrained) and the monotone gather. In Willett's Pose2Pose, the performance signal is continuous and the system finds the drawing. Here the score names it, and the 84- and 72-frame dead holds follow directly from that.

Craft defects. Dead holds: D10 44 frames (4.67–6.5), D24 84 (14.5–18.0), D26 72 (18.0–21.0). The pile pops at 21.75: the crumble chunks and three heaps are replaced in one frame by a single smooth mound about twice as wide. The penny's edge-on drawing sits inside the top of the sack outline at 22.33–22.38 and reads as a slot. The penny's rise at 21.46–21.54 passes through the knife's smear. The shuffle silhouette boils (described above). The phone layout holds: film in the top half, letterboxed; scrubber, bank sheet and cards below; no page scroll.

PAE_CHECK, compared. It agrees with what I saw on timing: flinch 1.00, flinch 4.00 (less: dip 9%→8%, push 0.15→0.05 m), notice 6.50, comes 9.25, props 14.50, braces 18.00, leaps 21.42, catches 22.42, lands 23.00, gathers 24.50, stays 30.00. It also agrees on placement (x 0.40 at 14.0, end x 0.80) and on area being constant within 0.001% outside the gather, with sack gain equal to pile loss (0.2926 m²). Volume holds by construction, through the per-drawing trim. The table's HOLDS for "ear lag 6 frames" is a correlation statistic that the picture does not support during the approach, where the ears lead. "Head low 100%" is true by the measure used, but the head is not readable as a head in those frames.

Verdict: PASS (narrow). Every choice lands within ±0.25 s, the catch, land and stay are strong, and the build honestly uses a drawing-substitution language for the body. It passes narrowly because the sack loses its identity through the whole approach, the leap and the end, and because prop and brace are frozen stills chosen by label rather than by selection.

Repair conditions, ranked. (1) From 9.0 to 14.0, every shuffle drawing shows two separate ear corners at the top, trailing behind (to the left of) the top's highest point by at least one exposure after each step, with the body's base visibly wider than its top. The check: a still at 11.0 reads as a sack, not a stone. (2) From 14.5 to 21.0, no single drawing is held longer than 12 frames. The prop and brace families each get at least three authored drawings (a press, an ease and a re-press against the tower) chosen by the path, so that the hold reads as effort. (3) The family-mismatch term is removed or weighted at or below the pose terms, and PAE_CHECK reports how many frames the path picked a drawing outside the cue's family (it must be greater than 0 somewhere and still read). Alternatively, the "Moved by" card says plainly that families are cued and only members are selected. (4) At 21.75, the transition from crumbles to pile goes through at least two intermediate pile drawings on twos, with no single-frame change in pile width over 20%. (5) The full drawings F1/F2 (28.5–32) and leap D28 (21.42–21.83) are redrawn on model: two clear ears, the bottom of F1 at least 8% wider than its waist, and a curved line of action in D28.
