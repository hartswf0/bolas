Does it read as a flour sack. Mostly yes, but as a stiff canvas crock more than a slack sack. At rest (0–6.5, 23.5–32) the silhouette is a trapezoid, wide at the bottom, and the interior grains really do pool: about 70% sit in the lower half in every sampled frame. Two ears are always visible. Failing frames: f27–f34 (1.12–1.42), where it tips over like a tipped can rather than slumping. 9.3–14.4, where the forward lean is a rigid rotation onto the back corner, so it looks like a jar falling over, not a sack folding over its middle. f515–f536 (21.46–22.33), where it becomes an upright capsule with the weight evenly spread (lower-half share 0.48 at 22.0). This may be acceptable for a stretch, but the bottom never trails. The line of action is a straight tilt almost everywhere and never a curved spine, because the strong area constraint and the shear-only pose targets keep the outline convex. When the ears are raised (6.75–9.0, 21.5–22.5, 28.5–32) they stand straight up like horns rather than hanging as cloth corners. The ears do drag and catch up after the flinches and the landing.

Lineage. The sack is genuinely built and moved in the simulation's terms. It is a 48-point Verlet ring with stretch, bend and area constraints, filled with 327 colliding grains. Its travel and its leap come from push-off impulses through points touching the table. Squash, rebound, ear drag and grain slosh are integrated. Flour is conserved and no grain escaped in any frame. The cards are honest about the main scripted elements: the kinematic knife, the tower's scheduled creep, the penny flick, and the gather flow field. Three things go beyond what the cards say. First, the penny's flick velocity is computed from the sack's current centroid, so the world aims the penny at the sack, and the leap is aimed at x 0.82, so the catch is arranged rather than achieved. Second, once caught, the penny is pinned to a cloth edge (state 2), so it cannot fall off. The code comment calls this static friction, but it is a hard attachment. Third, grains entering the mouth are teleported a few centimetres inward. The pose "muscle" pulls toward a keyframed blend of target shapes. That is within the lineage, but it does most of the acting. In the gather, the sack stands inert while a field pumps grains into it; it does not draw them in by any action of its own.

Craft defects. The grains are 2.4 cm discs, so the tower looks like a stack of beads and the pile like pearls or popcorn, not flour. The dust is flat grey discs, and the landing puffs at 23.2–23.6 look like cauliflower. Gravity of 1.8 m/s² makes the collapse and the leap look like the Moon (grains hang in the air over 21.0–22.5). The approach slides: contact points travel continuously between pulses (for example, the contact span moves from x −0.89 to −0.39 over f250–f279 without lifting), so the travel is a skid with pulses, not steps. Measured lift is never above 2 cm before 14.4. Mobile at 390×844 is correct: no page scroll, film in the top band, controls and cards below with internal scroll. But the film is only about 220 px tall inside a black stage, so the ears and penny are hard to read at that size.
