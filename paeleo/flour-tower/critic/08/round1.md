# SCION 08 · critic round 1 · DOES NOT PASS (narrowly)

Method. I rendered 08-sutherland.html headless at 1280×720: stills every 0.5 s across 0–32 s with goals and console both on and both off, every frame in the ten requested windows, contact sheets of each, full-resolution stills at the key times, and one 390×844 layout check. I read the source only to find PAE_SEEK and to check the cards' claims. I judged the picture before opening PAE_CHECK.

Flinch (1.04–1.30): reads. The ring squashes on the knife's stroke, stretches into a long lozenge pointing left, makes a small hop that pays for the travel, and settles at x ≈ −1.54. The ear trails and catches up. Flinch less (4.04–4.25): reads. It is the same gesture at about a third of the size, with almost no travel. Notice (6.75–8.9): reads, narrowly. The corners come up into two raised ears and the seam tips toward the tower. At 6.75 the tower has leaned only about 1° and the visible cue is the grains sifting off its cut face. The lean is plain only by 8.5, so the thinking is shown, but its cause appears late. Come head low (9.0–14.2): weak. It is an inchworm of seven 0.7 s cycles, and each base hop pays for the travel, so nothing slides. The head-low frames read well (9.5, 10.5, 12.0, 14.0: seam leading, low and forward). Each gather phase, though, rears the body into a tall tilted egg standing on a narrow foot (10.75–11.1, top 0.75; 12.9–13.1, top 0.72), well above its own rest height. The silhouette flips between flat slug and upright egg every 0.35 s, so the cross reads as bouncy, not slow and sustained. In several frames (9.25–9.5, 11.2–11.3) the seam sticks out as a beak. Prop (14.5–17.9): reads. The body is pressed flat to the leaning face with the seam along it, and the tower stops at 8.3° while it had been tipping visibly before contact. Brace (18.0–18.3): weak. The tower is kicked from 8.3° to 11.6° and the sack drops only about 6 cm and gives up about 4 cm of ground (x 0.32 → 0.28), which reads closer to "pushed" than "braces and holds". Leap (21.4–22.4): weak. The anticipation crouch at 21.3 reads. The rise, however, happens inside the new pile: at 22.0 the base is 0.18 m up, but the pile around it is taller, so the sack looks as if it stands up out of the heap rather than leaving the ground. Catch (22.25): reads clearly. The penny lands on the flat seam and stays through the landing and every frame after. Gather (24.5–28.5): reads, and is the film's best passage. Grains stream visibly from both piles into the lower body, the piles flatten, and the sack rises from 0.53 to 0.93. Stay (29.2–31.6): reads. The sack is completely still while the knife comes straight down, hovers 0.16 m over the penny, and lifts away at 31.3.

Does it read as a flour sack in every frame? No. Through Act 1, the notice and the gather it does: weight sits low, the two ears are clear, and the seam and dotted spine give a line of action. It fails in four places.
(a) 21.0–21.4: the falling tower blocks are drawn across the sack's outline, so the frame reads as a knot of lines.
(b) 21.6–22.75: the stretched sack is wide at the flat seam and narrow at a rounded bottom. That is top-heavy, an inverted cup or bucket, not a sack with weight pooled low.
(c) 23.04–23.40: the landing crumples the seam into the body. The ring folds into a W (23.08–23.17), and then the seam line and one ear sit inside the outline (23.21–23.40), so the frame reads as a knot of lines.
(d) 26.5–32.0: the full sack is a perfectly symmetrical barrel, widest at mid-height, tapered at the foot, with the ears flared like horns. It reads as an urn or an owl, not a full sack slumping under its own weight, and its line of action is a dead vertical for the last 5.5 s.
Smaller failures: the rearing approach frames listed above (egg on its end, weight high), and the prop (14.5–17.9), where the D-shaped body bulges at mid-height over a narrow foot.

Lineage. The sack is built and moved in Sketchpad's language. It is a 29-point ring with edge-length, seam-collinearity and AREA constraints, two ear points on springs, and floor contact with stick-or-step friction. It is solved iteratively every frame from a warm start. The console shows iteration counts and the area, contact and penny residuals, and the light-pen goals on the seam and base centroids are drawn as dashed lines with crosshairs. With goals and console off, most of the acting still reads, which meets the bar. The code confirms the cards: nothing in the sack is placed directly, and the knife path, tower lean drive and penny launch are disclosed as authored. Two claims are softer than they sound.
1. The prop is not solved as force. The contact weight on the tower is 0 (wT=0). The tower stops because a boolean freezes its lean drive whenever any contact is over 0.5 mm, not because the sack pushes back.
2. During the gather, the seam goal's height is set directly from the fill fraction, so the rise is steered rather than coming purely from the area constraint.
Neither is hidden, but the first is outside the "nothing placed directly" spirit at the one beat where the sack's purpose depends on physics. The penny's launch velocity is solved to land at (0.738, 0.905) at t+1.28 s. As a result the penny flies about 0.6 m upward like a tossed coin, where the chronicle says it drops.

Craft. Debris blocks pass through the sack (21.0–21.4). At the landing, displacing the pile makes two sharp twin flour spikes taller than the squashed sack (23.0–23.6), and the promised cloud of flour is barely visible. A small crumpled loop of ring points appears at the foot during approach hops (10.5–11.0) and at the prop's toe. About a third of the tower's flour is left on the table at the end. Flour is conserved, so this is acceptable, but "the flour on the plate draws into it" is only partly true on screen. Layout: desktop puts film left and panel right; at 390×844 the film fills the top half with controls and cards below and there is no page scroll. Both pass.

Where the table and the picture differ, the table is too generous:
- "Head low 97%" passes because the seam is compared with the back hump, and the hump is exactly what rears up in the egg frames.
- "Leaps" passes on a base lift the pile hides.
- "Braces, drift 0.031" passes a visible backward give.
- No check tests silhouette, so the landing knot and the urn ending pass unseen.

Verdict: DOES NOT PASS (narrowly). The construction is honest. Seven of the ten choices read, and the catch and gather are strong. But the bar requires that every frame read as a sack and never a knot of lines, and the film fails that at the collapse and the landing and gives a non-sack silhouette for its final 5.5 s. Add a weak approach, brace and leap, and that is too much to pass.

Repair conditions, ranked; each can be checked against the render:
1. Landing, 22.9–23.6: the ring stays a simple, non-self-intersecting polygon, and the seam and both ear tips stay on or outside the outline in every frame. Automate the check: no ring segment crosses another, in all 768 frames. The squash reads as a pillow pooled on the plate with the penny on top.
2. Full pose and leap: in every frame outside 21.5–22.5, and at least by 22.6, the sack's width at 25% of its height is at least its width at 60% of its height, so weight sits low. The 26.5–32 pose must sag and bulge at the foot and carry a visible lean or curve in its line of action, not a symmetrical barrel. In 21.6–22.4 the base clears the local pile surface by at least 0.08 m, so the leap reads as airborne.
3. Approach, 9.0–14.2: the top never exceeds 0.58 m and the seam stays below the shoulder in every frame by eye, with no egg-on-end frames like those at 10.75–11.1 and 12.9–13.1. Use no more than 4 slower cycles, each at least 1.2 s, so the cross reads as sustained.
4. Collapse, 21.0–21.4: no block outline crosses the sack ring (the blocks collide with the sack or fall clear of it), and the penny's peak height stays within 0.15 m of its rest height on the tower, so it drops rather than being tossed.
5. Prop and brace, 14.5–18.6: the tower's hold comes from contact force in the solver (nonzero wT), not from a gate that freezes the lean. At 18.0–18.3 the sack visibly compresses, with the top dropping at least 0.10 m, and gives up less than 0.01 m of x before recovering.
