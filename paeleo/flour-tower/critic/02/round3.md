The ten choices. Flinch (1.04–1.33 s) reads. On the hit frame the sack is swapped to a squash piece and tips back hard. It hops up and left at 1.25 and lands at x −1.5, flattened, at 1.33, with the ears streaming behind it. Flinch less (4.04–4.7) reads: the squash is shallower, the tilt is about half, and there is no travel. Notice (6.62) reads. On a held swap the ears go from lying flat to standing up and the body straightens toward the tower. The tower's lean begins at 6.58 and is visible by 7.0, with flecks sifting off the cut face. The sack holds the notice to 8.3, then crouches wide and low at 8.3–8.9 (anticipation), and commits at 9.0. That is thinking before acting. Come head low (9.0–14.0) reads. The neck and ears sit forward of and below the rear hump, and the line of action runs down toward the tower. The travel is made of ten rocking steps, each lifting the base about 3.8 cm on the up-rock exposure. It does not slide, but at phone size the steps look more like rocking than hopping. Prop (14.5) reads. On a held swap the whole sack tips onto its tower-side corner, and its upper lobe presses into the tower's face with a lit hairline between them. The pose is held until 21. The lean recovery of 0.8° is too small to see directly, but the body is clearly pressed into the tower. Brace (18.04) reads, but weakly. The hit is a swap to a deeper squash piece, with no retreat, the ears up, and the tower shuddering against the sack. It is the quietest beat in Act 2. Leap (21.42) reads: an anticipation squash at 21.12–21.38, then a swap to the tallest stretched piece, tilted toward the penny, with the ears trailing. Catch (22.42) is weak. A held swap to the flattest piece puts the sack at x 0.80 with the penny on top and tissue dust behind it, and it relaxes through softer pieces by 24.2. Gather (24.5–28.5) reads. Flecks arc from both heaps into the neck, the heaps visibly shrink and are gone by about 28.3, and the sack rises in held steps from 0.51 to 0.95 m. The flecks are sparse, though, and at contact-sheet size the flow is easy to miss. Stay (29.9–31.8) reads: the sack is dead still, and the knife descends, hovers over the penny and withdraws at 31.2 without cutting.

Flour sack in every frame. The half-empty sack reads as a flour sack from 0 to 21.4 s. It has a pooled, flat bottom, a tied neck with a ruffle, two corner ears, and a line of action through the pins. At 9.0–9.3 the hunched outline is jagged and the tie gets lost in it, but it still reads as a sack bent over. The frames that do not read cleanly are 22.42–22.96 (worst at 22.5 and 22.75). There the tall stretched piece, with the round penny on the ruffle and the two ears spread level like shoulders, reads as a hooded figure with a round head. The ears no longer rise like horns, but it still looks like a head on shoulders. The full sack from 28.5 to 32 now has a pinched neck and a waist above a pooled base, so it reads as a tied, filled sack (pear-shaped, like a money bag) rather than a bell. I name 22.42–22.96 as the failing span.

Lineage. The sack is built and moved in Reiniger's language. It is five rigid cards: a bottom lobe on a base pivot, an upper lobe on a pin, the neck on a pin, and two ears on the neck. The pins overlay shows the pieces on their pins at 7.0, 15.5 and 22.5. Squash, stretch and fullness come from a 49×7 cut library. Each piece is a recomputed outline whose width is trimmed to hold its area; the only transforms are rotations and translations, so "never a scaling" is true. The film is on twos: all 277 changes in sack position, height or ear position fall on even frames. The big changes (flinch, notice, prop, brace, leap, land) are held substitutions on hit frames, as the card says. The ears lag the neck by about 2.8 frames. The gather passes through nine fill pieces, each held at least five exposures. The claims on the cards are true. The one stretch is "it falls under constant gravity onto the neck". That is true in the code, but the gravity is about 0.2 m/s², roughly 1/50 of real gravity, so on screen the penny floats.

Verdict: PASS (narrow). Nine choices read and one, the catch, is weak. The construction and movement are honestly Reiniger, the cards are true, the phone layout now works, and four of round 2's five conditions are met. It stays narrow because the climax is soft. The penny floats instead of dropping, and for half a second after the catch the sack reads as a hooded figure.
