Independent critic B — 072 / KEEP IT — V01

Verdict: bounded VISUAL PASS for two observable sign/action rules, a simple competing intention, and a visible refusal that changes the sign-bearing object. Full native-speed timing, cue-learning and audiovisual acceptance remain INCONCLUSIVE. The third sign's specific 'repeat' meaning is weaker than the first two and should not be reported as independently learned from this inspection.

Method
I first inspected all four ordered one-second actual-film sheets before PERFORMANCE_RULES.json. I then extracted actual movie passages at four frames/second:7–18 (fold, tray approach and first unfold),20–25 (turn), and46–54 (retention and tray flip). I enlarged actual11.5,16,25,34.5,49 and54-second frames. I read the rules only after the first full sheet pass. No builder script or other critic report was read. No audio audition, native real-time playback, physical-paper test or blind cue-comprehension test occurred.

This trajectory supports a put-away intention modestly; it is not a completed deposit or evidence of a character's private motive.

The enlarged25-second picture shows a different orientation from the prior vertical creases. The first and later circular cues provide a second observable sign→turn rule. These changes are actual drawn action, not an interpretation supplied only by a caption.

At34,40 and46 seconds the III sign on the tray is highlighted. The paper now retains many crease lines and irregular upper/lower edges, and small manipulations continue. The graphic III is not an obvious universal repeat symbol and can resemble a pause mark. The earlier responses do not give an emphatic full opening/closing cycle, so its exact 'repeat fold/unfold' meaning is less securely inferable than the bracket and turn rules. I can identify a recurrent demand signal; I cannot claim that its particular verb was independently learned.

Refusal and final state
After the46-second highlight, the puppet retains a narrow folded sheet in one hand rather than broadening/turning it. The other hand reaches toward the marked tray around47–48. The face's pupils shift toward that action. The tray compresses toward a thin horizontal shape, then expands as a pale blank back around49–50. The sign disappears with its surface rather than simply vanishing at an unrelated location. By53–54 both hands again guard the folded sheet and the tray remains blank. That makes the refusal an alteration of the rule-bearing object, not merely a stopped animation.

The prior highlights occur at roughly six-second intervals, and the expected subsequent III highlight at52 seconds is absent. That omission is consistent with the tray losing its sign face. A viewer's felt expectation of that beat cannot be certified by temporal sheets; it needs normal-speed viewing. The film leaves the fold kept, not successfully deposited in the tray. This supports 'keep it' through action without requiring a written moral.

Layer and material coherence
Hands/arms consistently appear over the sheet and touch its visible corners/edges. During rotation the hand coordinates follow the changing object rather than detaching. During the final reach, one hand remains with the sheet while the other interacts with the tray. Crossed arms are simplified graphic overlaps, not a claim of anatomical realism. The table stays behind these actions and the sign occupies the tray surface that is flipped. I found no blocking layer inversion in the inspected samples.

Creases become more numerous and the sheet's edge grows uneven. These are authored consequences in a vector performance, not measured fatigue of real paper. The film labels synthetic fiction, newly composed sound and absence of a real recorded performer/physical-paper simulation. I do not infer actual friction, material mechanics or foley from drawn edge changes.

Largest gap and observable repair
The weakest visual link is III→repeat, especially34–46. If the intended claim requires all three verbs to be readable, strengthen one early III response into a conspicuous widen-then-narrow cycle, preserving the existing retained-fold response later. A hidden-ledger reviewer should be able to describe what was repeated before identifying the later refusal. Alternatively use a less pause-like repeat glyph, but the action contrast matters more than adding a word label. This is an optional strengthening for the present two-rule/intention/refusal criterion, and a required check before claiming three rules were learned.

The opening tray approach is brief and stops short. If blind viewing finds its put-away intention unclear, extend that same rightward approach until the folded sheet nearly meets the tray, then interrupt it with the unfold cue. No new scene or explanation is needed.

Strengths and scope
Protect the two simple readable sign/action relations, the rightward tray approach, the hand that keeps the folded sheet while the other flips the demand surface, and the blank tray after the final expected beat. These form a specific small conflict and consequence rather than merely displaying symbols. The archive is an openly acknowledged reference gesture; the original worker is not made to perform the fiction or feel refusal. Numerical tones/stylized noise and their relationship to sign highlights remain unauditioned. The bounded visual success should not be promoted to complete audiovisual or measured cue-learning acceptance.
