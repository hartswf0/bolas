I did not initially read an unresolved social rearrangement: this is recognizably a comic good-deed/misrecognition resolution.

Responsibility, agency and reaction ownership:
Chaplin retains substantial agency: he handles and allocates his own money before being accused; he defends himself rather than only awaiting rescue. The woman's later check, display and address to the officer are her own visible actions in the shared shot. She changes the situation; she is not only an insert of suffering for Chaplin to redeem. The uniformed figure initially exercises physical control, then visibly changes his response. This is stronger than a montage of unrelated “bully/victim” expressions.
Source inspection matches the seated action, observer crosscut and later three-person encounter to the original scene. The film openly moves an earlier interval after the accusation and labels it. Within retained intervals the observed reactions remain in their source order. The four-second source gap426–430 includes further seated/woman/Chaplin movement before his encounter; omitting it simplifies the transition. I saw no independent evidence of a reaction falsely attached to a different event. I did not audit all output frames or certify source speech translation/authenticity as a separate research task.
The persistent staged-fiction/source label is visible in the inspected samples. It is small, but provenance is also introduced in opening and end cards. It should not be represented as a new documentary observation or contemporary performed experiment.

Largest remaining gap:
The final passage is conventional exoneration and gratitude. Chaplin is increasingly fixed as the generous innocent and the woman as grateful beneficiary. The officer's changed hat gesture complicates his identity, but the closing removal of the obstacle restores a neat comic moral arrangement. That is a partial, not complete answer to the prompt's instruction to avoid permanent hero/victim/bully roles and end on an unresolved continuing relationship. The source's theatrical star persona strengthens this reading. A good accusation-reversal experiment is not identical to the requested worktable mechanism in which the isolated participant chooses a position and initiates shared work with the obstructing person.
