Money transfer: what the images support and do not support:
The long20–43 sequence is primarily money handling, not a clear gift. Its length does not by itself turn preparation into transfer. The brief low movement around47.0–47.5 is consistent with placing money at the woman's near-side clothing/lap, but black costume and overlapping hands obscure the small object and exact direction. It is not a clearly visible palm-to-palm handover. The later discovery/inspection56–59 and her positive response make giving a strong narrative inference. The edit therefore supports 'he apparently helped her, and the accusation is corrected' more strongly than 'we distinctly watched money leave his hand and enter her possession.' Do not describe the latter as verified visual fact.

This preserves source continuity but attention may drift before the action that matters. The woman remains head-down for much of it. The late recognition restores agency, yet the preparatory stretch risks reinstating passive-sufferer versus benevolent-man categories simply through unequal action time. This is a pacing/attention reservation from sampled images, not proof that real-time viewing fails.
