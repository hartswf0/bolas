WHAT THIS WORK DOES
A recorded carton-folding task leads to a dark wet front in paper, then to cutting that produces/separates metal curls, then back to the carton task. The 17–19s card identifies a different-material analogy. The 37–45s comparison pairs earlier and later recorded views with source times and asks what would remain after contact stops. It does not answer a recovery question these recordings did not test.

SCOPE / PROTECTED CONDITIONS
This is an archive adaptation, not the prescribed original50-second tabletop experiment with dry paper, cloth, wet paper, shifted weight and weathered paint. Those new experiments were NOT filmed. The three selected responses are folding, darkening following liquid contact, and curl/chip formation/separation. The carton is already prepared/pre-creased; the wetting shot is staged advertising, with unverified production technique/speed; metal cutting begins before our excerpt. These are different samples and conditions. We do not claim elastic recovery, permanent/irreversible deformation, common properties, or measured memory in matter. No simulated physical response or generated sample appears.

An archive source's commercial claim, including its original 'More than paper' text, is not our measurement or endorsement.
