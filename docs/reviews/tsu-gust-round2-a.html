The gust test · critic round 2 · round 2 blind performance read

Round 2 blind performance read. A separate agent that did not make the work, started Oct 18 00:25 EDT. Brief and report verbatim. File paths in the brief are the working paths on the day.

The brief

You are an independent reviewer of a 7-second stop-motion-style animation test of a small handmade cardboard puppet. You have not been told what the shot is supposed to show, on purpose.

Look at /home/claude/puppet/review2/seq_1.png then /home/claude/puppet/review2/seq_2.png. Together they are every second frame of the shot (12 frames per second), read left to right, top to bottom; each tile is labeled with frame number and time. These are still frames, not real-time playback; say where a judgment would need playback. Full-size frames: /home/claude/puppet/review2/full_f*.jpg.

Answer, terse, in this order:
1. In plain sentences, describe what happens, beat by beat with times: what the character notices, does, and how its attitude changes. Describe what you see; do not guess an intended scenario.
2. What causes the action, as far as the frames show it? Which direction does it come from? How sure are you?
3. Which way does the head turn (toward screen-left or screen-right)? How sure are you?
4. Which beat is least readable, with frame numbers, and why.
5. Rate how clearly the attitude change reads through the body alone: 1 (not at all) to 5 (unmistakable), with one line on why.
Under 300 words.

The report

  1. What happens - 0.0–1.0s (f000–f022): The puppet stands upright, facing camera, arms spread wide. Its scarf flutters toward screen-right. It reads as calm and open. - ~1.0s (f024): White horizontal streaks appear across the frame, like wind or blown snow. The puppet barely reacts. - 1.5–2.0s (f036–f048): The head starts to tilt and turn. - 2.1–2.9s (f052–f070): The puppet braces. Its screen-left leg steps out wide, the torso leans, both arms come level and stiff, and the head is cocked. It reads as straining or bracing. - 3.0–3.9s (f072–f094): It recovers its stance. The head turns well toward screen-left and tilts, showing the side of the head box. The screen-right arm begins to drop. - ~4.0s (f096–f100): The screen-right arm hangs limp. The left of the two background figures disappears, and the streaks thin out. - 4.2–7.0s (f102–f166): A held pose: one arm still pointing screen-left, the other limp, head turned away. It reads as deflated or resigned, but the meaning is ambiguous.

  2. Cause: The streaks and the scarf trailing to screen-right suggest wind coming from screen-left. Moderate confidence: the streaks have no clear direction in stills, and playback is needed to see which way they travel.

  3. Head turn: Toward screen-left, so into the apparent wind. Fairly sure: in f088 and f166 the drawn face has shifted left and the right side of the head box is showing.

  4. Least readable beat: f096–f106, the arm drop and the figure vanishing. Nothing in the puppet ties the two together: its head is turned away from the figures, and the arm falls without any visible trigger. The f052–f066 leg kick is also unclear; it could read as a brace, a stumble or a dance step. Weight and timing need playback to judge.

  5. Rating: 2/5. The body clearly changes, from open to braced to slack with one arm limp, but the poses don't say what the character feels or what it is responding to.
