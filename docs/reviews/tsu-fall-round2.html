The brief

You are an independent film critic reviewing a candidate cut of a short animated film you did not make. Be specific and diagnostic.

Material (stills only; you cannot play video, say where that limits you):
- /home/claude/lab/fall/review/contact_r2.jpg : one frame per second of the 80 s film body, left to right, top to bottom, 8 per row (frame N = second N).
- Extract any extra frames you need from /home/claude/lab/fall/out/fall_body.mp4, e.g. `ffmpeg -v error -ss 21.5 -i /home/claude/lab/fall/out/fall_body.mp4 -frames:v 1 /tmp/claude-0/-home-claude/4fcf2301-cc19-5f90-be19-3bb1b3367fc1/scratchpad/r2_21.jpg`, and view them with Read. The film, "TEMPUS FUGIT: FALL", is a gentle comic short about a cardboard puppet, Boone, in autumn in West Jefferson, N.C., mixing archival found footage with a cardboard diorama. A viewer should read from images alone:
- leaves fall; a gust; Boone raises a hand and time stops (leaves hang; the TV on the porch freezes on a father holding a baby);
- he pokes a hanging leaf and it stays;
- he tapes leaves back onto the maple by hand, then faster and faster (a counter);
- a Virgil card;
- he sits by the frozen TV, then lowers the same hand: time resumes;
- a storm takes every leaf and knocks him into the pile;
- "TEMPEST FUGIT" (the storm runs away too);
- he waves at the father and baby on the TV;
- the film ends on "TEMPUS FUGIT".
The bar: the action and relationships read from images alone; the defining passage (the turn at the frozen TV and the release) earns its effect; staging, composition and cut rhythm are deliberate; the cardboard world and the found film feel like one world.

Return in under 350 words:
1. Verdict: bar met / not met / inconclusive, with the limits of your judgment.
2. Evidence: exact seconds for each remaining problem.
3. The single most consequential remaining gap, with an observable acceptance condition.
4. Protected strengths.

The report

1. Verdict: not met (near miss). Most of the story reads from images alone, and the cardboard and found-film layers mostly sit together as one world. But the defining passage, the turn at the frozen TV and the release, does not earn its effect. Limits: I judged from stills (the 1 fps contact sheet plus about 23 extracted frames). I could not judge motion timing, holds, sound, or whether the leaves actually hang still between frames.

2. Evidence (seconds) - 20.6–21.0: the time-stop gesture works (hand raised, then a white flash). But the flash at 21 washes out the raised hand, so the cause is half hidden by its own effect. - 36.5–37: the overlay reads "STILL FALLING: 4,013" while leaves pour down in the time-stopped world. That contradicts the stop, unless the counter is a joke the images don't set up. The counter also jumps from 10 to 62 within about one second (36–37), so "faster and faster" collapses into a cut instead of a build-up. - 36–38: the tree, TV and sign suddenly change position and scale. That breaks the geography just before the Virgil card. - 46–47: when Boone sits by the TV, his head is tilted up and to the left, away from the father and baby. A leaf is stuck on his face. Nothing shows him looking at, or being moved by, the TV. - 48–50: a white ring floats beside his head. It looks like a UI cursor, not part of the world. - 49.6 → 50.0: the hand goes from up to down in under 0.4 s, with no anticipation. He stands facing the camera, and the flash at 50.5 covers the drop. The release looks like a pose change, not a decision. - 69–70: the wave at the TV lasts about 1 s in a wide-ish frame. The film's emotional payoff is underweighted.

3. Most consequential gap. The turn at the TV: Boone never visibly looks at the father and baby before he lets time go. Acceptance condition: between about 44 and 50 there is a held shot of at least 2 s in which Boone's head is turned toward the TV and the TV image is legible in the same frame. It is followed by the hand lowering, visible across at least 3 consecutive frames at 1/8 s spacing, before any flash. The leaf on his face and the ring are gone.

4. Protected strengths - The "West Jefferson" title (1–3) into "TEMPUS FUGIT: FALL" (5–7) is a clean opening. - The diorama is consistent (HARTSOE sign, THIS SIDE UP box, felt hills). - The TV changes from a grey archival figure (8–18) to the father and baby (19 onward), which plants the freeze well. - The Virgil card (39–41) is legible and well placed. - The storm's colour turn (51–58) and the leaf pile (64–71) read clearly. - The archival sky with "TEMPEST FUGIT" (59–63) and the bare-tree "TEMPUS FUGIT" ending (76–79) make strong bookends.
