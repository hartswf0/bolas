The brief

You are an independent film critic reviewing a candidate cut of a short animated film. You did not make it. Be specific and harsh where earned, but diagnostic.

Material to inspect (images; you cannot play video, so say so where it limits you):
- /home/claude/lab/fall/review/contact_1fps.jpg : one frame per second of the 80 s film body, read left to right, top to bottom (8 per row; frame N = second N).

The film: "TEMPUS FUGIT: FALL", a comic, gentle short for a series about a cardboard puppet named Boone. Setting is autumn in West Jefferson, North Carolina (Blue Ridge). It mixes archival found footage with a cardboard cut-out diorama. Intended story a viewer should be able to read without explanation: Boone is on a porch beside a cardboard TV playing an old home-movie/documentary; leaves fall; a gust; Boone raises a hand and time stops (leaves hang, the film on the TV freezes on a father holding a baby); he tapes the leaves back on the maple one by one, faster and faster (a counter), then a Virgil card ("time flees ... while we wander around, prisoners of our love for detail"); he sits by the frozen TV; he lets go; a storm takes every leaf and knocks him into the pile; "TEMPEST FUGIT" (even the storm runs away); he waves at the baby on the TV; ends on "TEMPUS FUGIT". The bar: a viewer reads the action and relationships from images alone; the defining passage (the turn at the frozen TV and the release) earns its effect; staging, silhouette, composition and cut rhythm are deliberate; the cardboard world and found film feel like one world.

Return, concisely:
1. Verdict: bar met / not met / inconclusive (with what limits your judgment).
2. Evidence: exact seconds for each problem you see (readability, staging, scale, occlusion, text legibility, repetitive or dead stretches, whether the time-stop reads, whether the found film and set cohere).
3. The single most consequential gap, and an observable acceptance condition for fixing it.
4. Up to 4 further fixes ranked by impact, each with a second-range.
5. Protected strengths that a repair must keep.
Keep it under 450 words.

The report

Verdict: not met. One limit on this: I could only look at stills (the 1 fps sheet plus full-res frames at 13, 19, 21, 26, 37, 45, 47, 51, 56, 69 and 73 s). I could not check whether the leaves actually stop moving, how fast the cuts land, or the sound.

Evidence - 0–7 s, dead opening. About 7 s of foggy ridge with no action. The "WEST JEFFERSON / OCTOBER" text is small and dim. - 13–15 s, unclear TV. The TV shows a different clip (an older figure). The father-and-baby image doesn't appear until about 19 s, so the TV's role isn't set up early. - 19–22 s, time-stop gesture. At 21 s Boone raises a hand at the TV, which reads. But he is small, in three-quarter view, with the sign behind him. No still shows leaves hanging in mid-air any differently from the falling leaves before it. From images alone the stop does not read as a stop. - 24–38 s, taping doesn't read. Boone is never shown touching a branch with tape. At 26 s he walks away from the tree. At 37 s he is cut off at the bottom-left edge with the tree between him and the TV. The counter (top-left, small) carries the whole idea. The leaves are five-pointed stars, which read as stars, not maple leaves. - 39–41 s, Virgil card. The Latin is readable, but the English translation (the line that matters) is small and grey. - 42–48 s, turn at the TV. This is the best-staged passage: Boone in close-up, the TV large, the father and baby clear (45 s). At 47 s, though, Boone's sitting pose pushes his torso half out of frame on the left. There is also no visible beat where he lets go. He sits, and then the storm begins. - 51–57 s, storm. It starts as arms-up at 51 s, which reads as celebrating rather than giving up. At 56 s there is a full-frame grey camouflage-like texture that doesn't read as anything. The knockdown into the pile happens in about one second at 54–55 s. - 58–63 s, "TEMPEST FUGIT". Six seconds of found sky footage with no Boone. The pun lands, but the gag has nothing in our world to cut back to. - 64–76 s, the wave. 64–68 s is near-identical frames of Boone lying in the pile. At 69 s the TV shows a different baby (black-and-white, at a window), so he waves at a stranger, not the baby he froze time for. 72–76 s is static.

Most consequential gap: the time-stop and the release, the core of the film, are not visible as events. Acceptance condition: in a single frame after the gesture, the viewer can see (a) leaves hanging still against moving leaves before it, or leaves clearly suspended with motion lines gone, and (b) the TV frame matching the 45 s father-and-baby image. Then, between 45 and 50 s, one held shot where Boone does something visible that undoes the gesture, such as lowering his hand, before the first gust.

Further fixes, by impact 1. 24–38 s: show at least two close shots of a hand pressing a leaf onto a branch, with tape visible, before the montage speeds up. Keep Boone fully in frame. 2. 66–71 s: the wave must go to the same father-and-baby footage, at a larger scale. Cut 64–68 s down to about 2 s. 3. 56–63 s: drop the texture frame at 56 s. Bring "TEMPEST FUGIT" down to about 3 s and intercut Boone in the pile so the storm "running away" plays against him. 4. 0–7 s and 72–76 s: trim each to about 3 s, and enlarge the location text and the Virgil translation.

Strengths a repair must keep - The felt-and-cardboard layered ridges and the found-footage sky backdrop already feel like one world. - The 42–48 s close staging, with Boone and the TV sharing the frame. - The counter jumping from 2 to 19 to 61 against "STILL FALLING: 3,997" is a good comic idea. - The leaf pile at 64+ s as the final state. - The "TEMPEST FUGIT" pun and the bare tree under the closing "TEMPUS FUGIT".
