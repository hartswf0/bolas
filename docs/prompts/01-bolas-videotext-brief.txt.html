the dissertation text the pencil test acts out: From Imagetext to Worldtext

Have bolas showing this Have bolas doing this showing this From Imagetext to Worldtext: Language Games and Operative Description in Generative Media A house can look right and still have no usable entrance. A film can match every noun in a poem and lose its voice. An agent can follow each instruction and help produce an outcome nobody intended. These discrepancies give the dissertation its problem: what must accompany a description for it to support intelligible action, coherent worlds, and accountable interpretation? The three experiments establish progressively harder conditions. First, can a description guide an action? Second, can its consequences hold together across a world? Third, can we understand and revise what happens when those consequences exceed the instruction? Use the Cyberiad stories as provocations for these experiments. Your working systems and documented encounters supply the evidence. EXPERIMENT I · WHEN WORDS OPERATEHow the World Was Saved · Trurl’s Prescription · Electronic Bard Question: How does a description become an instruction, a creative artifact, or a rule someone can act upon? “The house that language built” is the opening encounter. A description becomes geometry, but its interpretation becomes visible in what the geometry permits: entering, standing, looking out, moving between rooms. WAG/WAGYO and LEGO let you compare resemblance with construction. What counts as success depends on the activity in which the description operates. Wittgenstein’s builders provide the minimal scene: words work through a learned arrangement of people, objects, expectations, and actions. “Wittgenstein’s Waymo” can name your contemporary extension: “put that there” becomes a problem of identifying that, locating there, choosing an action, and recognizing its completion. Treat it as an experimental vignette whose assumptions you expose. The wink and the blink then complicate the apparent simplicity of visible tests. Two movements can look alike while doing different things. Likewise, two outputs can resemble each other while occupying different roles: illustration, instruction, parody, testimony, invitation. That is the passage from thick description to thick prompting. Context becomes something you must communicate, preserve, and revise during production. Demonstration: One description, several realizations, one consequential mismatch, and a revision that changes what becomes possible. Contribution: An account of operative description grounded in situated use. EXPERIMENT II · WHEN MODELS BECOME WORLDSSeventh Sally · Dragons of Probability · Three Storytelling Machines Question: What changes when a description must sustain relations across time, space, and multiple encounters? A generated image can leave its unseen surroundings unresolved. An inhabitable world accumulates obligations. A door needs somewhere to lead. A remembered event needs consequences. A route needs to remain traversable from another position. Here worldtext becomes necessary. It names the organization of descriptions, media, rules, and actions through which a world remains available to experience and revision. WYGWYL supplies the sustained case. Mark’s poems move through images, footage, sequences, locations, and the Atlantis city. Each translation introduces decisions: whose perspective organizes the scene, what becomes literal, what remains ambiguous, and which relations persist. The poem field is a crucial intermediate form. A poem organizes possible encounters with footage; an editor’s path through those possibilities becomes a temporal composition. Arcade cinema adds action and feedback. The player helps determine how the archive unfolds. Demonstration: Follow one passage through a poem, alternative cuts, and a spatial realization. Show what each version preserves, introduces, or makes impossible. Contribution: Worldtext as a framework for studying continuity and transformation across representations and actions. EXPERIMENT III · WHEN DESCRIPTION EXCEEDS UNDERSTANDINGDemon of the Second Kind · Altruizine · Trap of Gargantius Question: What happens when individually intelligible descriptions generate collectively unintelligible consequences? More context can overwhelm attention. More plausible footage can make selection harder. More precise instructions can interact in unforeseen ways. A system can satisfy a local condition while undermining the activity that gave the condition its purpose. The operative archive brings this problem into view. Different models make different material retrievable. Descriptions and rankings influence what appears relevant. Successive selections can produce a sequence whose implications exceed any individual match. Found Odyssey offers a concrete encounter: a shared word brings an archival utterance into a new narrative. The match is inspectable; its appropriateness remains a question. The original speaker’s recorded words do not establish agreement with the new role. This experiment asks what an operator needs to notice, question, and revise such consequences. It connects your attention tax to infrastructure, provenance, responsibility, and the limits of prompting. Demonstration: Compare exhaustive retrieval with a bounded, situated selection process. Preserve excluded candidates, competing readings, and the reasons for the final arrangement. Contribution: Criteria for examining when operative descriptions remain legible and revisable as their consequences accumulate. The argument connecting them Experiment What must be established Characteristic failure Words operate An utterance works within a situated activity The output resembles the request but cannot support its purpose Models become worlds Relations persist across encounters Convincing fragments fail to form a coherent environment Description exceeds understanding Consequences remain inspectable and revisable Locally successful operations produce an opaque or harmful whole The story for anyone  The dissertation follows description across three thresholds: when words guide actions, when those actions compose worlds, and when their accumulated consequences exceed what an individual instruction makes intelligible. I investigate these thresholds through building systems, poetic translation, and operative archives. Thick prompting is the method for documenting the contextual knowledge, judgments, and revisions involved. Worldtext names the organization of language, media, rules, and actions that emerges across these practices. The contribution is an account of how descriptions become operative, supported by working artifacts and production histories that expose both their possibilities and their limits.
