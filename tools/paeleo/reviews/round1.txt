PAELEO-00 v1 critic report (round 1)

What I inspected: frames at 2 fps across all 82 s (contact sheets), 8 fps tiles at 4.6–6.1, 21–23, 32.6–33.8, 37.2–38.8 and 52.3–53.5 s, a 24 fps crop at 33.3–33.8 s, and full-res stills. For sound I measured RMS, peak and spectral centroid every 0.25 s, rendered a full spectrogram, zoomed in on six moments and checked a 5 ms envelope. I cannot hear it, so I'm not claiming the voices are intelligible as speech.

1. Verdict: inconclusive. No reference film was supplied to compare against, so "reference stronger" can't be judged. Measured against the bar, it does not pass: the climax refusal has no acting, and the dog visibly pops at 33.5 s.

2. Moments behind the verdict
- 54.1–59.6 s (about frames 1298–1430): after "come in." the dog's pose is the same in every 0.5 s sample. No ear, head, tail or glance toward the doorway changes. Only the camera drifts. The refusal is carried by the narration typed at 58.0–60 s ("the dog stays on the sill. the description let it choose."). The dog lies in profile, parallel to the lit door, so "half in, half out" isn't staged at all.
- 33.50 s (frame about 804): the dog goes from lying curled and facing away (large) to an edge-on sliver in one frame, then stands up much smaller in the same framing. That is a pose and scale snap.
- 37.5–38.0 s: the dog's response to "open the door" (lying down, tag says "lying down") only lands while the next prompt "offer a hand" is already being typed and the operation light is on. That muddies which change caused it.

3. Single largest gap
The thesis beat, "the viewer can understand the dog but not control it", is told by text rather than acted. When asked to come in, the dog doesn't do anything you can read as a choice.

4. Repair condition (falsifiable)
In the 2 s after the "come in." prompt resolves, a re-check at 8 fps must show:
- (a) the dog first reacts visibly: ears or head turn toward the doorway or interior, or the tail moves;
- (b) then it settles back clearly: head lowers onto paws, or it turns its head back out;
- (c) on the final frame of the scene, part of the body (front paws or chest) is past the door-frame line inside the lit opening and part is outside.
All of this has to be legible in a 320 px-wide still with the tags masked.

5. Other problems, ranked
1. 33.50 s (frame about 804): the lying-to-standing snap and scale pop. Every frame-to-frame pose change needs to be continuous, at least 6 frames.
2. 37.5 s: make the reaction to the door read before the next prompt starts typing (an approach or a lie-down inside 34–36 s).
3. 28.0–33.4 s: in the wide shot, the "turned its back" dog is a small curled lump about 70 px wide at 1280. It doesn't read as turned away at phone size; only the close-up at 25–28 s does.
4. 5.0–5.9 s: the IMAGETEXT caption doesn't lift off. The growing picture covers it and it vanishes, then it reappears at 5.9 s as a second copy beside a new tag. That's two copies of the same text over the door frame.
5. 18.0 s and 6.0 s: crossfades overlap text. The title is ghosted over the tags, and "ENVIRONMENT TEXT" is doubled.
6. 64.5–71.0 s and 77.5–81.5 s: the subtitles in the three-scales diagram, the cycle labels and the credits are tiny monospace, unreadable at phone size. All tag text is also around 11 px at 720p.
7. 21.0–22.4 s: "backing away" is a slow drift of roughly 15% of frame width with the head low. It's legible, but the ears and tail don't help.
8. Sound: the voices are short synthetic harmonic glides (21.5, 25.0, 41.5–43, about 54.5 s). The soft voice at 41.5 is noticeably lower and quieter, which is good. "Come in" at 54.5 s is mostly a broadband burst with little harmonic content, so the climax line is the weakest sound cue. Paw-like clustered transients are present at 44–51.5 s.

6. Strengths to keep
- The cut-paper look and the dusk palette.
- The P/E/O indicator bar and tags that update with each prompt.
- The close-up at 25–28 s (dog crouched, head down, facing away). It's the best piece of acting in the film.
- The person sitting down at 31–32 s.
- A continuous approach walk with matching paw sounds (43.5–53 s) and a smooth lie-down onto the sill (53.0–53.4 s).
- A door creak synced to the door opening (33.5–35 s), typing sounds synced to each prompt, a cricket ambience bed, and music only under the close.
- The three-scales nesting animation and a clean PAELEO lockup with a readable tagline (72–77 s).
