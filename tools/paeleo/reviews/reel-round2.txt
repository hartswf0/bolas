REEL CRITIC, round 2: paeleo-01.mp4

What I inspected: the mid-frame of all 54 lenses (at 640 px, plus dog crops scaled to 360 px wide); 5 frames spread across each of lenses 14, 24, 27, 38, 47 and 49; the title at 1.5 s and the end card at 92 s; three plain-dog reference frames from paeleo-00.mp4; and the audio, by measuring loudness (RMS) and looking for sound onsets at every cut time. I did not watch it in motion beyond those samples, and I did not build a pixel mask of the dog, so the 25% test was judged by eye against the paeleo-00 dog.

1. Verdict: does not pass. Most lenses now change the dog clearly, but both parts of the previous critic's repair condition still fail. Four lenses leave the dog essentially plain, and several neighbours within 3 positions share the same kind of overlay.

2. Lenses that still fail:
- 05 Norstein (10.4–12.0 s): the dog is a slightly washed-out plain tan; the multiplane look is only foreground blobs.
- 24 Live2D (40.8–42.4 s): a close-up where the body stays plain tan cut-paper; only the head has a cone and a pink glow. Well under 25% of the dog changes.
- 40 Physical tabletop (66.4–68.0 s): the whole scene is skewed, but the dog itself is plain.
- 44 Multiplane parallax (72.8–74.4 s): plain dog behind foreground bushes. It also repeats 05's idea.
- 53 Atlanta Moving Image (87.2–88.8 s): the sepia grade covers the whole frame; nothing is done to the dog itself.
- Same overlay type within 3 positions (all small top-left text tags): 11/13/15, 31/33, 33/36, 40/41 (adjacent), 45/46 (adjacent). Dark info panels at the top: 19/20/22 and 26/27/28. Labels pinned on the dog's parts: 42/45.
- Near-duplicates: 25/48 are still both state-machine diagrams with the same approach / retreat / lie down / refuse wording and a red "refuse". Lens 14 uses that same wording too. 36/37 are both weight-coloured dogs, but rainbow versus red/blue keeps them visually apart. 07/38 are now distinct. 12 is now distinct from 36/37.
- Lenses 13 and 14 both carry the caption "NORA WILLETT". If that is not intended, it is a captioning error.

3. Broken picture and collisions:
- 14 (24.8–26.4 s): the dog goes grey, then red, then collapses to a thin red sliver around 25.9 s. The dog is not continuous within the lens.
- 38 (63.2 s): the dog is replaced for the whole lens by a white drawing card pasted where the dog stands.
- 49 (80.8–82.4 s): the person is missing for the whole lens; only stray specks remain on the left.
- The collisions flagged before in 14, 27, 47 and 49 are gone in the frames I sampled. 48's "trust 0.12" tag sits against the dog's hind feet.
- The look subtitles in the caption bar are roughly 7 px monospace at 640 wide, so they are unreadable at phone size.

4. Largest remaining gap: overlays are repeated as a way to show each method, where the method should be shown on the dog itself. Repair condition, falsifiable: at the mid-frame scaled to 360 px wide, at least 25% of the dog's pixels differ visibly from the plain tan dog in every lens, including 05, 24, 40, 44 and 53. No two lenses within 3 positions use the same overlay type (top-left tag, dark top panel, part labels, state graph). Lens 14's dog has visible area of at least 50% of its mid-frame area in every frame.

5. Strengths to preserve:
- The sound marks the cuts: a clear onset at 53 of the 54 lens starts. The one miss is the cut into 17 South Park at 29.6 s, and the end card at 90.4 s has no onset. Peak level is −5.2 dBFS (0.55 linear); overall RMS is −23 dB.
- The colour and material language of the dog is strong in 02, 04, 06, 07, 08, 10, 16, 17, 18, 21, 29, 31, 33, 36, 37, 51 and 54.
- The caption bar layout is consistent, and the title and end cards are clean and readable.
- In most lenses the dog stays legible as the same machine.
