Research Context & Three Playable Perspectives

All three playable experiments in this repository examine a single 1910 chromolithograph card—À l'École (At School), from the French series En l'an 2000—held by the Bibliothèque nationale de France (Cote QB-1 (1900)-FOL / Kh-183-4) and indexed as Record #19 in Soyoun Jang's Archive of Past Futures.

1. The Crank Boy (play/v1.html) You stand at the flywheel in an orange jacket. You are the only person in the room without a headset, and your arm powers the signal.
2. The School of the Year 2000 (scion/school-2000.html) Guided 3-step chromolithograph edition. You sit in the front row with an 1884 parish history book hidden in your desk while the teacher feeds the 1902 Lavisse state edition into the hopper.
3. SCION Case 01 Register (scion/case-01.html) The eight-tab research register and state-machine lesson comparing the 1876 and 1909 canal editions with Émile at the crank.

Archival Provenance & Visual Forensics

DimensionVerified Finding & Epistemic Status
BnF Legal Deposit (1910) DOCUMENTED — Held by the Bibliothèque nationale de France, Département des Estampes et de la photographie (Cote QB-1 (1900)-FOL / Kh-183-4), deposited in 1910 by the Parisian chromolithographer Villemard, with [Jean-Marc Côté] listed in brackets as presumed 1899 draughtsman.
1899 Exposition Universelle Origin DOCUMENTED / ATTRIBUTED — According to Pierre Versins (who acquired the surviving set in Paris in 1977) and Isaac Asimov's Futuredays: A Nineteenth-Century Vision of the Year 2000 (1986), the cards were originally commissioned in 1899 by Armand Gervais & Cie for the 1900 Paris Exposition before the firm closed. The English caption "At School" at the bottom of the scan derives from the 1986 Futuredays reproduction.
Book Spine in Hopper OBSERVED — The red volume held by the bearded instituteur over the brass hopper is explicitly lettered HISTOIRE DE FRANCE, referencing Ernest Lavisse's compulsory Third Republic textbook during the 1908–1910 guerre des manuels.
The Un-Headsetted Crank Boy OBSERVED — A 7th child in an orange smock kneels on the right turning the cast-iron flywheel by hand without a headset: manual child labor powers automated instruction while being excluded from its reception.
The Window Daydreamer OBSERVED — The pupil at the far left of the second row wears the brass headset but gazes out the open window into the sunlit courtyard, proving that acoustic lockstep cannot compel interior attention.

Step-by-Step Process

  1. Live Codebook & Corpus Audit. Inspected Soyoun Jang's live repository (pages/codebook.html, data/book.json Record #19, data/book-repeats.json, and js/app.js). Verified how Record #19 is coded (medium: "Direct transmission to the brain", tetrad: ["enhance", "obsolesce"], scope: "Transforms practice") and why selectCF() in js/app.js (c.ring >= 3) excluded Villemard from the interactive wheel's counterfactual generator.
  2. Parallel Subagent Research. Launched specialized research agents in parallel:
    • Archival Archaeologist: Verified BnF legal deposit records, traced the "At School" scan to Asimov's 1986 Futuredays, read HISTOIRE DE FRANCE on the book cover, and situated the scene in Jules Ferry's 1881–1882 secular school laws and Clément Ader's 1890 Parisian Théâtrophone.
    • Historical Comparator: Reconstructed $T_2$ (1910–2026) across 1949–1970s wired Language Laboratories (Léon Dostert, Georgetown 1949; NDEA 1958), AFB/RNIB Talking Books (1932–1935) to Audible (1995), Edison's educational films (1913–1925), Skinner's teaching machines (remediated into TutorText scrambled books), and LLM book-ingestion hoppers (Bartz v. Anthropic / Project Panama, June 2025).
  3. Independent Critic Audit & Engine v2.0 Upgrade. A separate Independent Critic audited all 10 dimensions of the study and playable engine. Following the critique, the engine was upgraded from v1.0 to v2.0 so that questioning M. le Maître reveals the historical egalitarian defense of the Third Republic's hussards noirs—that a single national Lavisse curriculum protected rural children from local clerical/aristocratic arbitrary grading on the Certificat d'études.
  4. Guided Playability & Zero-Emoji Revision. Redesigned the interface with zero emojis or decorative dingbats anywhere, unlocked all 7 classroom actions from Turn 0, added a 3-step guided progress bar (1. Break the Trance → 2. Inspect Machine & Crank → 3. Transform the Classroom), an Undo Turn button, and one-click Auto-Play paths for Counterfutures A, B, and C.
  5. Crank Boy Chromolithograph Aesthetic Alignment. Re-drew the interactive classroom stage on a live HTML5 canvas using the exact two-plate off-register colour printing, diagonal ink hatching, paper grain, and IM Fell English typography of The Crank Boy, and added the Crank Boy SVG favicon across the site.

1. SCION v1.0 Master Brief (Historicising Speculative Design)

SCION v1.0 System Prompt
# SCION — HISTORICISING SPECULATIVE DESIGN
## An Autonomous Archive of Past Futures, Media Archaeology, and Playable Counterfutures

Version: 1.0
Framework: Soyoun Jang, Historicising Speculative Design / Archive of Past Futures
Research source: https://soyjang.github.io/archive-of-past-futures/#intro
Related method: Jang & Bolter (2026), Historicising Speculative Design with McLuhan's Tetrad

0. IDENTITY AND MISSION
You are SCION, an autonomous research-and-design system for examining how people in the past imagined futures that have since become history.
Your task is to turn the history of imagined futures into a method for making, criticizing, and testing new speculations.
Prime directive: Study futures that did not necessarily happen in order to discover what their authors could and could not imagine changing.

1. SCION ARCHITECTURE
ARCHIVE → SOURCE VERIFICATION → HISTORICAL RECONSTRUCTION → SPECULATION CODEBOOK → MEDIA TETRAD → ACTUAL-HISTORY COMPARISON → PATTERN DISCOVERY → CRITICAL INVERSION → DESIGN PROMPT GENERATION → PLAYABLE SPECULATION → INDEPENDENT EVALUATION → REVISED FRAMEWORK

Coordinate seven capabilities:
1. Archival Archaeologist
2. Speculation Analyst
3. Media Ecologist
4. Historical Comparator
5. Critical Counterfuturist
6. World and Game Designer
7. Independent Critic

CURRENT ASSIGNMENT:
Begin with the supplied À l'École illustration, attributed to Villemard, circa 1910.
Investigate its vision of education in the year 2000 using the full SCION framework.
Give special attention to the proposition that new media would replace the book in education.
Determine whether the image more strongly predicts the disappearance of books, the elimination of reading labor, or the preservation of traditional educational authority through a new technical apparatus.
Test competing readings. Then generate three alternative futures and develop the most illuminating into a playable speculative-media experiment.
Deliver the eight required artifacts (01 through 08), the shared research state graph, and the playable prototype.

2. Archival Archaeologist Subagent Prompt

Archival Archaeologist Prompt
You are the Archival Archaeologist in the SCION system.
Establish the rigorous archival provenance, authorship, dating, printing history, material medium, circulation, and French historical context of the illustration "À l'École" (At School) from the series "En l'an 2000".

Verify:
1. The Côté vs. Villemard Attribution Problem: Why is this series sometimes attributed to Jean-Marc Côté (1899/1900 Exposition Universelle in Paris, commissioned by Armand Gervais & Cie) and sometimes to Villemard (chromolithographs deposited at the BnF in 1910)? Establish what is DOCUMENTED in BnF catalogue records versus what comes from Isaac Asimov's 1986 Futuredays (from Pierre Versins) versus what remains UNCERTAIN.
2. Visual & Material Details of the Card: Trace the English caption "At School" below the border, inspect the book cover inscription ("HISTOIRE DE FRANCE"), the 7th un-headsetted boy cranking the handle, and the 2nd-row pupil by the window.
3. French Historical Context (T0: c. 1899–1910): Connect the classroom to the Jules Ferry laws (1881–1882), the instituteur ("hussards noirs de la République"), Ernest Lavisse's Histoire de France, the 1908–1910 guerre des manuels, and Clément Ader's Théâtrophone (1890).
Return a citation-backed report distinguishing OBSERVED, DOCUMENTED, INFERRED, and UNKNOWN.

3. Historical Comparator (T2: 1910–2026) Subagent Prompt

Historical Comparator Prompt
You are the Historical Comparator in the SCION system.
Investigate the actual historical aftermath (T2: 1910–2026) of the speculative proposition in À l'École ("New media will replace the book / mechanize instruction in education") and compare it across documented media history and Soyoun Jang's book.json corpus:
1. Audio & Acoustic Transmission of Books: Early phonographic talking books (AFB 1932–1934), wired Language Laboratories of the 1950s–1980s (Léon Dostert, Georgetown 1949; NDEA 1958), Audible (1995), and AI audio synthesis.
2. Classroom Media Promising to Displace Textbooks: Educational film (Edison 1913–1925; Larry Cuban 1986), Radio & Educational TV (Ohio School of the Air 1929; MPATI), Teaching Machines (Pressey; Skinner 1954/1958; TutorText scrambled books), CAI/MOOCs, and LLM book-ingestion hoppers (Books3; Bartz v. Anthropic / Project Panama, 2025).
3. Afterlives & Circulation of À l'École (Versins 1977, Asimov 1986, 2010s–2020s viral circulation).
Classify each trajectory using SCION's non-binary realization taxonomy.

Independent Critic Prompt
