Research Context: The Sensory Tether & Operative Ekphrasis

This work is part of a collaborative research program led by Watson Hartsoe with Jay David Bolter, Collin Freeman, and Soyoun Jang on operative ekphrasis and thick prompting: translating a historical image and a theoretical framework into a playable world whose rules test the image's unspoken assumptions.

In Soyoun Jang's Archive of Past Futures, Villemard's 1910 card À l'École is coded with Scope = Transforms practice: it imagines a radical mechanical apparatus (a hand-cranked electrical book-grinder wired to brass headsets) while leaving the social hierarchy of the Third Republic schoolroom completely untouched. The archive explicitly codes two quadrants of Marshall McLuhan's Tetrad:

  • Enhances: Direct transmission of book contents into students' heads.
  • Obsolesces: Active reading from paper books.

Version 8 uses the SCION LUDIC 1.0 twelve-step Ludic Loop to make the player discover the remaining two quadrants (Retrieval and Reversal) through the physical constraint of the Sensory Tether:

  • When your brass ear-cups are clamped down ([listen]), you receive the three clauses of the lesson and appear obedient when the Master turns to survey the benches—but a perceptual veil hides the classroom, concealing the moment the Master tears out a dissenting folio (Folio 42) before dropping the bound volume into the hopper.
  • When you lift your right ear-cup ([disconnect]) during the Master's blind spots, you forfeit the live wire clause currently pulsing overhead, but regain sight of the room: you can inspect the wall map and timeline ([inspect]), signal Auguste at the crank to drag the flywheel down to 28 RPM ([modulate-crank]) so the unground folio scrap spits out the bottom grate onto the floorboards, rescue the scrap ([rescue]), and whisper to Jules at the neighboring bench ([compare]) to recover the clause you missed while disconnected.
  • At the end of each of the five lessons (covering French History, Colonial Geography, Civic Labor, the Archive of the Book, and Svante Arrhenius's 1896 Carbon & Climate Futures), the Master strikes his desk for La Récitation, where you choose between [recite] (Orthodoxy), [question] (citing wall evidence), [rewrite] (brandishing the rescued Folio 42), or [refuse] (a full six-pupil helmet strike when Solidarity reaches 60, or solitary reassignment to turn the iron hand-crank yourself).

Theory of Play (SCION LUDIC 1.0)

Play Thesis: In À l'École (En l'An 2000), the player inhabits the split between a wired pupil and the unwired boy turning the crank—discovering that to know what the Master is censoring before it enters the hopper, you must lift your brass ear-cup during the Master's blind spots, sacrifice portions of the live wire stream, coordinate crank speed to spit out unground pages, and piece the missing lesson together with your bench-mates before the Recitation bell rings.
Constraint / RuleWhat it does in playWhat happens if removed
1. The Sensory Tether (Exclusive Modality) While [listen] is active, you capture the wire's clauses and appear obedient, but cannot inspect the room or rescue scraps. While [disconnect] is active, you see the room and can act, but miss the current wire clause. Without this rule, players could read the room and capture the wire simultaneously, eliminating all timing tension and peer dependency.
2. The Master's Periodic Gaze The Master cycles through five visible states: Selecting Book → Tearing Page → Feeding Hopper → Turning Warning (2.0s) → Watching Class (2.6s). Being disconnected while he watches penalizes Orthodoxy and clamps your helmet down. Disconnecting would carry no surveillance risk, turning the classroom into a static point-and-click puzzle.
3. Crank-Speed Materiality At nominal 60 RPM, the grinder pulverizes pages cleanly. Dragging to 28 RPM or surging to 95 RPM causes the blades to cough out the Master's torn Folio 42 onto the floorboards where a disconnected pupil can rescue it. Auguste's hand-crank would be purely decorative animation rather than an epistemological instrument.
4. Evidence-Gated Recitation At the bell, [question] requires a witnessed wall contradiction, and [rewrite] requires the physical rescued Folio 42 scrap. Recitation would degenerate into an arbitrary dialogue tree where players click "rebel" without earning evidence in the world.
5. Brass Helmet Inertia (650ms) Clamping or lifting the heavy brass ear-cups requires 0.65 seconds of physical inertia. A spoil-sport could flicker [listen]/[disconnect] every frame to capture 100% of both channels at zero cost.

Historical Ledger: Documented vs. Inferred vs. Invented

Documented
The 1910 chromolithograph À l'École (attributed to Villemard) in the En l'an 2000 series; the bearded schoolmaster feeding bound books into a hopper; the unwired boy turning the hand-crank; six seated pupils in wired brass headsets with arms folded; the wall charts, map, window, green curtain, and bookshelf; Soyoun Jang's Archive of Past Futures Claim #19 coding (Scope: Transforms practice; McLuhan: Enhances direct transfer, Obsolesces active paper reading).
Inferred
That the overhead rail transmits a synchronous lesson powered by the crank boy's manual labor, and that the crank boy is the only student whose ears are free and whose eyes face the hopper.
Invented
The five lessons across French History (1871 Commune), Colonial Geography, Civic Labor, the Archive of the Book, and Svante Arrhenius's 1896 carbon calculation; the torn Folio 42 censorship mechanic; the Sensory Tether trade-off between [listen] and [disconnect]; the peer whisper recovery with Jules; and the four Recitation Tribunal outcomes.
