The shorts

Correspondence by phonograph and cinema

Operator: sound to action. Timing comes from sound. The man in the chair speaks, in a voice without words, and each syllable moves his head, the receiver and the big horn. Then the screen answers with a real dance filmed in 1914. How much the dancers move, measured frame by frame, sets how loud the music plays. Last, the card itself goes up on the screen of a 1948 picture house, behind the audience's heads.

Observed
The card, En l'an 2000, about 1910. Two plates from the cineosis-lab shot library: a ballroom from Fatty's Magic Pants (1914), set on the screen, and a picture-house audience from Let's Go To The Movies (1948), whose screen the card is projected onto; the heads stay in front.
Cut and moved
The big horn, the receiver, the reclining man's head and the woman on the screen. Her place is filled with the screen's own colour.
Drawn and synthesised
The beam's flicker, the camera, grain and captions; the voice, the projector and the music. The music's loudness follows the dance's measured motion.
