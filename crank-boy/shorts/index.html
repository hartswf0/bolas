How the whole fits

Each card is worked three ways. A film: a short with one operator from the SCION animation swarms and a plate of real archive film. A game: the same card with a second operator, one you play. A place in the Town: the card in a row with the other six under one drawn power line, its handle-holder recastable into the others' places. The doodles drawn over the cards are real Quick, Draw! records, the ones Secondhand Motion chose for its cartoons.

Toward CINEOSIS

CINEOSIS, in the Bolas lab, has the crash-test family perform the 45 signs of Deleuze's cinema books inside unaltered archive shots. The shorts run the same path the other way round: the archive comes into the card, through a surface the card already has. Each short is proposed below as a candidate passage for one sign. The proposals are readings, not results: none has yet had the blind test the CINEOSIS handmade passages had, where a fresh critic describes the passage before reading what it was meant to show.

What we learned

  1. The card's own drawing is the best cast. Cut pieces moved a little read as the card coming alive; redrawn or regenerated pieces would read as something else.
  2. The archive belongs on a surface the card already has. A mirror, a screen, a floor, a sky or a fire takes a plate without a seam; a plate pasted over a figure reads as a television.
  3. Do not key thin things. A biplane's wings and a crowd's heads broke apart when keyed. A soft window, or the plate's own screen, worked.
  4. Numbers and eyes measure different things. The scrubber passed its contact test perfectly while a blind critic could not see the brushes move. Every short needs both.
  5. Keep the performance apart from the performer. Because the crank is a function of height only, any handle-holder can turn it, and the Town could be built from the shorts without remaking them.
  6. Low resolution limits the camera. Three cards are under 600 pixels wide; past about 1.5 times zoom they go soft. Better scans would help more than better code.
