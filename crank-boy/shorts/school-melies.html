À l'école, a trick film

Proof scene for the first call sheet: the school as a trick-film tableau of 1907. The camera never moves, as in Méliès's films. The card stands on a stage with curtains and footlights. Every change is a stop-substitution, a cut on an exact frame with no dissolve. The film runs as fast as the boy turns the crank, because a hand-cranked projector did too. Three substitutions happen. The back wall becomes the falling stars of The Eclipse (1907), re-printed in the card's eight inks. The teacher's book comes out of the machine as a moon. Then the moon takes a pupil's head, and turns back into a book.

Ledger

Observed
The card À l'école, about 1910. The plate: the falling stars of The Eclipse (Georges Méliès, 1907), from the cineosis-lab shot library (clip ed87f1da, 7 to 18 s), re-printed in the card's eight inks through a dot screen. The puffs of smoke are The Balloonatic (1923), re-printed the same way.
Cut and moved
The boy's upper body, the pupil's head and the teacher's book. The wall and window are keyed out by the card's own colours (assets/shorts/parts/school/wall.png), not painted.
Drawn and synthesised
The proscenium, curtains and footlights; the moon, drawn from two Quick, Draw! records (a moon and a face, CC BY 4.0); the gate weave, flicker and scratches; the piano and the projector. Nothing is generated by an image model.

The sky is re-printed in the card's four darkest inks and its white, so the stars stay the card's own paper. What this proof tests: whether an archive frame from the card's own decade, re-printed in the card's inks, can replace a surface of the card on a single frame without the cut showing as a seam. Not yet blind-reviewed.
