Bullet time, for real

One photograph can fake an orbit. What does a real one take?

How it was made

  1. Film Boone on the butter churn (a turntable stool) holding one pose. Nine phone clips.
  2. Solve the turntable angle per frame from tracks on the disc; skip hand frames.
  3. Run structure-from-motion on the cut-out frames to get the orbit.
  4. Ask Marble 2 for the rooftop along that orbit, a view every 5 degrees.
  5. For each frame of the lean, take the view and the photo nearest the wanted angle and stand him in it.

What this case teaches

  1. The same 10.6 s of lean, rebuilt six times, shows exactly what each change does.
  2. A generated world is only as steady as its conditioning: each batch of views is conditioned on the posed roof and the most recent views.

How it differs. The first case where Boone's body is real photographs at every angle.

World Labs announcement of Atlas, an "omni world model for spatial intelligence" (dated September 1, 2026), pasted into the session at Oct 18 11:13.

It is a third-party announcement, so it is summarised here rather than republished. What the rooftop runs took from it:

- Atlas makes and poses whole places, not single pictures: images2PosedRGBD returns an image with metric depth and a camera, and generation can be conditioned on posed views.
- So the rooftop could be asked for view by view along Boone's solved turntable orbit (every 5 degrees, 0 to 205), each batch conditioned on the posed roof and the most recent views.
- Every view comes with depth, which the composite uses to stand Boone at the orbit centre at the right scale.

The product appears on this site under its model name, Marble 2.
