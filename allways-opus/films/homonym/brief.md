# HOMONYM

**Concept · Words change worlds**

A word passes between voices and images, changing sense at each cut until speech seems to edit the world around it.

**First proof.** Construct a sixty-second relay around one word with three visually legible senses. Establish its first use in an intact spoken context, then let an action carry the next meaning before another voice names it. Make the third sense alter the sequence’s tone or stakes. Return to the opening utterance with the new associations still active. Keep original speech intelligible, and test a version without explanatory captions before adding another semantic branch.

**Methods.** Primary: SCION 055 · Words — Polysemic Relay. Supporting: SCION 092 · Hands of Bresson — Gesture Evidence Before a Generalization.

**Development boundary.** A semantic connection needs a defensible interpretation beyond a similarity score. An assembled utterance must be presented as an authored construction, not attributed to a recorded speaker as something they actually said.

## Primary operator SCION 55: Words — Polysemic Relay

Theorem: Make viewers discover a word's changing senses through successive visible actions, then use the discovered ambiguity to change the sequence's emotional direction. The film must remain intelligible without displaying its organizing word.

Acceptance criteria:
- The critic can identify the organizing verbal relation after viewing, while describing at least two different senses.
- Transitions do more than repeat a similarly shaped object.
- The last return changes the significance of the first shot.
- Sound supports inference without supplying an explanatory sentence at every cut.
- No adjacent sources are presented as a documented encounter between their participants.
