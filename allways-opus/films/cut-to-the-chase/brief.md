# CUT TO THE CHASE

**Concept · A shifting pursuit**

Fleeing, chasing, training, stumbling, and stopping form a shifting pursuit across cinema, until the movement itself becomes the story.

**First proof.** Build a sixty-second pursuit from eight excerpts. Establish a runner’s direction, then transfer that momentum across two clearly different settings. Interrupt the rhythm with someone waiting or recovering, retaining their local sound. Resume with an action that changes the apparent relationship between pursuing and being pursued. Finish on a person who stops while the surrounding world continues moving. Compare the cut with and without sound bridges to determine which transitions carry the pursuit.

**Methods.** Primary: SCION 091 · Watchtower of Turkey — Momentum Across an Honest Discontinuity. Supporting: SCION 069 · A Movie — Borrowed Continuity, Exposed Construction; SCION 083 · Berlin: Symphony of a Great City — Civic Rhythm with Unequal Pulses.

**Development boundary.** The earlier ninety-minute proposal remains a development ambition. This first proof must establish changing relationships and rest before a feature duration is justified.

## Primary operator SCION 91: Watchtower of Turkey — Momentum Across an Honest Discontinuity

Theorem: Carry the viewer’s bodily sense of direction across discontinuous places while periodically restoring enough context to recognize the leap. A transition should transfer momentum and attention, not counterfeit one continuous documentary journey.

Acceptance criteria:
- Transitions carry readable direction or attention at normal speed.
- At least one leap remains perceptible as a change of place.
- The contextual rest reveals an activity rather than merely slowing the score.
- Speed changes do not produce broken bodily motion or hidden continuity claims.
- The ending’s stillness gains force from the preceding momentum.
