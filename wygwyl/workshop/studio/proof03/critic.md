# Blind A/B critic: proof 03

A fresh critic looked first at the paintings, then saw frame strips of two methods without labels.
- **X:** proof01, with one painting per view bent by a mesh rig: the poet's walk, the poet's turn, and the three-actor collision scene.
- **Y:** proof03, the exposure sheet of whole paintings: the poet's beats and the couple's spin.

## Verdict: Y is stronger. Y scored 5.5/10, X scored 3.5/10.
- **Y.**
  - Every frame is on-model: faces, hands, the gown and the pinstripes all hold.
  - The spin "reads clearly as a 360° turn… the best single passage in either method".
  - No foreign pieces, no floating, no size pops, and a seamless loop.
- **X.**
  - The woman's gown becomes a "garbled drawing" for about 3 s: a box at mid-thigh, a rod from the chest, and her feet smeared away.
  - The man in the pinstripe suit carries a hook-shaped piece that is in no painting, on every frame.
  - The walk is a crouched shuffle.
  - The turn is a one-frame swap.
  - The fall in the collision scene is "real acting", but it's a rigid plank pivoting at the ankles.

## Y's largest gap
1. The walk: one frozen stride slides across the floor.
2. Every beat change is a one-frame pop, with no in-betweens.

## Repair condition, falsifiable (quoted from the critic)
Y passes if all of these hold:
- in a re-render of the walk, which boot is in front alternates at least every 0.5 s;
- each planted boot stays within ±3 px while it is in contact;
- every beat change has at least 2 in-between frames that are intermediate between the two poses;
- no in-between frame shows a duplicated or missing limb;
- drawing integrity stays at today's level.

## Critic remarks not upheld
- **Back views called "invented".** They are the dancers' own paintings (dance-back, dance-threequarter-back), not inventions.
- **Hollow grommets called "style drift".** The poet's track-suit paintings are painted with ring grommets, so this isn't drift.
