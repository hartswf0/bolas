What the checks don't see

The gown. The vintage woman's 3/4 rig is cut from a dance painting with her skirt flung out. Bending her legs drags the skirt mesh, so it crumples in the walk and after her turn. Deformation can't fix this. She needs walking and standing 3/4 drawings swapped in, the way the turn already swaps the front drawing for the 3/4 one.

Walk posture. Hips ride at 95% of leg length so both feet can reach the floor at their drawn depths. The poet still reads slightly crouched.

Staging. In casting A the poet falls backward across the vintage woman, who is turning to watch. Nothing checks whether actors overlap when they aren't colliding.

Falls that start mid-walk. When the hit lands on the near foot, the far foot swings down and takes the weight. A planted foot can slip up to about 4.5 cm while that hand-off happens. The solo fall clips don't have this.

The cast, measured

Front-view bone lengths in cm, after each painting is scaled to the actor's height. These measurements are what the clips get retargeted onto. Walk strides are in leg lengths, so a longer leg gives a longer stride at the same tempo.
