Why we kept choosing odd pieces

Every method we built filled a gap in the drawings with the nearest wrong thing. The rig bent a painting of the poet approaching a motorbike into a walk. The parts lab glued cards from different sheets together. The pose selector reached for a drawing with a table baked into it. The swarms agree: when a pose isn't in the drawing, swap to a drawing that has it. If no drawing has it, commission one.

The poet acts in five of his own paintings

Walk in, point, recoil, turn to us, open arms. The critic's largest gap is here: the walk is one frozen stride sliding across the floor, because only one clean walk drawing exists, and each beat change is a one-frame pop.

The critic's repair condition

This came from a fresh critic who looked at the paintings first, then judged both methods blind. The exposure sheet passes when:

  • the leading boot alternates at least every 0.5 s in the walk;
  • planted boots stay within ±3 px;
  • every beat change has at least 2 in-between frames with no broken limbs;
  • drawing integrity stays where it is now.

The real fix for the walk is to commission a 3/4 walk cycle (four drawings). The stopgap to test is bending each drawing by at most 15°, only near its own pose, toward the next one, for those 2-frame in-betweens. The same blind critic would judge it.

The simple method

Vocabulary. Each actor's clean paintings: one costume, a full figure, no scenery in the cutout. Each is tagged by view and posture.

Sheet. A list of beats. Each beat names a painting, how long it holds, how far the body travels, and whether it lands with a settle.

Placement. One size per actor, the lowest painted pixel on the floor, and the body centre carried across swaps so nothing jumps.

Turns are rings of views. A missing view holds the previous drawing, so any cast member plays the same beats.

Gaps are commissions. A beat no clean drawing covers is reported as a drawing to make, never filled with the nearest wrong one.
