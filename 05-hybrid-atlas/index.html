How each passage is made

  • Plate: a segment with no cuts, at 24 fps.
  • Camera: a pinhole camera fitted to the plate.
  • Camera moves: tracked per frame:
  • raft: template matching and ECC alignment
  • row: phase correlation
  • desert and corridor: floor-feature homographies
  • Animation: the crash-lab rig, authored in a world laid out to the plate and checked by the lab checker.
  • Dummy layers: rendered per actor as transparent layers from the plate camera's direction, then mapped into the plate.
  • Shadows: projected along the plate's light onto walls, desk, cargo and ground.
  • Occlusion mattes:
  • the rattan chair, keyed on a clean plate
  • polygons for the table, lamp and cargo heap, carried by the track
  • an ISNet matte for the ranger
  • Look:
  • grade matched to the plate (mono or colour)
  • shading toward the light
  • passed through the plate's native 320×240 resolution, so the softness matches
  • grain
  • Sound: the plate's own track, or ambience where the plate is looped, with foley placed from foot contacts.

What the critics confirmed works

  • Tracking holds dummies to moving plates within about 2 px (39).
  • Wall shadows agree with the plate's own shadows (38).
  • The rattan-chair occlusion holds (38).
  • The plate dims with the lamp in linear light (28).
  • Sound lands on events (38, 28).

Pipeline fixes needed before the other 39 signs

  1. One scale-and-contact solve per plate, with an automatic gate. - Use the horizon or ground plane and a person in shot. - Reject the render if Bola's foot line and head height disagree with her depth or with the 85%-of-a-man rule. - Fix one Bola look and grade it per plate.
  2. Proper occlusion and contact. - Roto or keyed mattes instead of rectangles. - Flat planted feet with contact shadows. - Cast-shadow direction and hardness taken from the plate's key light.
  3. A legibility gate on the action before rendering. - The beat must actually be animated, at a scale where it reads. - Cause must come before effect. - Any real prop gets its own clean element. - Footsteps come from the contacts the animation actually produces.

Limits

  • The crowd plate for 5 Dividual was tried and dropped. Neither human-segmentation model could separate a dense crowd at 320×240, so it is not in the pilot.
  • Looped plates (38, 42) cannot carry their own sound past 3.4 s, so they use ambience.
  • Dummies have no motion blur.
  • Camera tracks are 2D homographies. Parallax off the ground plane is not modelled.
Method notes (working)

CINEOSIS hybrid pilot — method notes (working)

Pipeline per passage 1. Plate: the sign's own example clip from cineosis-lab, cut-free segment, 24 fps, upscaled to 720 high. Static plates shorter than the action are ping-ponged. 2. Camera: a pinhole fitted to the plate (focal, height, pitch, roll) from table/lamp/row/horizon points; moving plates add a tracked 2D transform per frame (raft: template + ECC; row: phase correlation; desert and corridor: LK floor features → homography chain). 3. Dummies: the crash-lab rig authored in a world laid out to the plate, checked by the lab checker (balance, planted feet, reach, clash, floor), rendered per actor as a transparent orthographic layer from the direction the plate camera sees it, mapped by the pinhole's local affine at the actor's anchor (plus the tracked transform). 4. Shadows: the dummy silhouette projected along the plate's light onto receiving planes (wall, desk, cargo, ground), mapped by the plane's homography, blurred and multiplied; contact occlusion under feet and hands. 5. Occlusion: per-shot mattes — key of the rattan chair on a clean plate, hand polygons (table, lamp, cargo heap) carried by the track, ISNet salient matte on the ranger; depth-gated against the dummy's distance. 6. Look: grade (mono or colour, saturation, white balance, contrast), terminator shading from the silhouette toward the light, resampled through the plate's native resolution (320×240) so it shares the plate's softness, per-frame grain. 7. Interaction with the plate: lifted real glass (cut out, carried, its place inpainted); lamp turned down (plate relit in linear light, globe included); raft motion carried; plate camera moves followed. 8. Sound: the plate's own track (looped plates get ambience), foley placed from the rig's foot contacts and authored events.

CINEOSIS hybrid atlas: what scaling taught us

This round built 84 passages across all 45 signs:

  • 78 automatic passages, two new plates for every sign apart from the six pilot signs.
  • 6 pilot passages, carried over from the pilot.

The film is about 12 minutes long. A fresh critic, who had not seen the work being made, reviewed it:

  • a survey of the whole film
  • start, middle and end strips for all 78 automatic passages
  • consecutive-frame crops of 6 passages

The automatic pipeline (per plate, no hand placement)

  1. Plate choice. Take the two best-scoring examples per sign, then trim each to its longest cut-free segment (up to 8.5 s).
  2. Analysis. For each plate, measure: - the picture area inside any black bars - a camera track (feature homographies chained from the middle frame) - object mattes (ISNet, every 15th frame, carried between samples by the track) - person detection - colour and exposure statistics
  3. Mode. Each plate is staged in one of three modes: - G (ground): Bola walks in from the nearer edge to a spot on the ground, sized for that depth, or at 85% of a person's height when a person is found. - S (surface): she stands small on a photographed tabletop or object. - P (proscenium): for close-ups, titles and abstract images, she stands in front of the image like a figure before a cinema screen and casts her shadow onto it.
  4. Action. Each sign has its own gesture sequence, for example: a point for Solid perception, cowering for Originary world, a dance for Plastic figure, cupping an ear for Sonsign, leaning in to read for Lectosigns.
  5. Integration. - Objects whose base is nearer the camera than her feet occlude her. - Her shadow falls on the ground (G, S) or on the image plane (P). - The grade follows the plate: mono with a tint, or colour matched by saturation and white balance. - She is softened through the plate's native resolution and given grain. - Footsteps come from her foot contacts, mixed over the plate's own sound.

What fails, and how often

  1. Hidden cuts and dissolves inside plates: 37%. They are fatal in G and S, mostly harmless in P. Soft archival footage defeats ordinary scene detection.
  2. Bola off frame or cut by the edge: about 13%. Causes: short trims, slow entries, a track that carries her out.
  3. Wrong support or scale: about 12%, all in G. She stands at person height on a desk or console, or in crowds with no visible floor.
  4. Occlusion matte errors: about 10%. She ghosts through people, is erased by the very surface she stands on, or nearer people pass behind her.
  5. The track applied as rotation or shear on zoom and roll plates. Rare, but each case is the worst artifact in the atlas.
  6. Plate mismatch: about 13%. Dark plates give her no visible contact, she is lit for day on night plates, and her yellow is too loud on tinted plates.

Next fixes, ranked by passages lifted

  1. Shot segmentation tuned for soft footage, including dissolves. Then either trim to one shot or re-stage per shot. About 13 G/S passages lifted and about 14 P passages cleaned.
  2. Keep her in frame: at least 70% of the passage fully visible, entry finished within 1.5 s, and re-anchoring when the track drifts. About 10 lifted.
  3. Support and scale validation: feet only on floor-class pixels, scale checked against people, tabletops routed to S. About 9 lifted.
  4. Occlusion that is temporally stable, comes only from bases nearer the camera than her feet, and never from her own support surface. About 8 lifted.
  5. Plate matching: grain, blur, exposure and tint matched to the plate, a footlight for P on dark plates, and the track clamped to translation plus uniform scale. About 10 lifted.

Drop or reroute:

  • dense crowds with no visible floor
  • zoom or roll plates in G
  • plates under about 100 frames after trimming
  • already-animated plates
  • interiors with furniture, which should go to S

What carries over:

  • P is the mode that survives cuts, so any plate that cannot be cleaned goes there.
  • Open ground with a mid-distance person is where G works.
