Afterimage

the eye keeps the glow

afterimage held by the retinablinksI held · II release · III carry

Stare at a cold green-white light and look away, and the eye shows you pink. That pink is not in the world. The eye makes it, out of what it was made to look at.

This study treats the screen as a retina. It adapts to the archive's light and, where the light has gone, keeps the complementary image. The projector light is tinted a clinical green-white, so everything the eye is forced to watch returns as pink. Nobody paints it on.

It joins three ideas on this site. A Clockwork Pink held an eye open with a clamp. Paradiso kept every cut frame in a tin. Here the eye is the tin: it keeps what was taken away, and the glow it carries afterwards is its own.

Three movements

  1. I · Held0:00–0:18
    An eye from a 1950s hospital film, a camera lens, a light bulb and a lighthouse. The cuts come faster each time, after the Ludovico sequence. The eye is never allowed to blink, so each image burns pink into the next.
  2. II · Release0:18–0:30
    The cutting stops on the eye. A hand lifts a miner's lantern, in its own warm light, untinted. Then the first blink, with red light through the lid, and the retina rests.
  3. III · Carry0:30–0:45
    Darkness. The eye's own pink afterimage is the only light left. A flower opens inside it, and when the eye comes back it carries the flower's ghost. The sound in this movement is the glow itself: its loudness follows how much afterimage the eye still holds.

What was measured

The retina writes a log of how much afterimage it holds on every frame. A check reads that log. These checks do not say whether the study works. That is the critic's job.

  • passNo blink is allowed during Held.
  • passThe burn builds. The afterimage in the last four seconds of Held is __BURN__.
  • passEvery blink lets the retina rest: its adaptation drops across each blink.
  • passThe glow is carried. In the dark, with no new light on screen, the afterimage never falls below __DARK__.
  • passSome glow is still there at the end, before the fade.

Sources

Every shot is public-domain archive film from the Internet Archive, cut into frames by secondhand-motion. The footage is retimed, cropped, tinted and composited, never drawn on. The sound is synthesized.
