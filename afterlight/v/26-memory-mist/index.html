Build log · how the lab made this recollection

01Finding the people

Every frame of the memory was segmented so the lab knew who was in it. Three models did the work: a multiclass selfie model (face, hair, body, clothes), a general person model that still finds figures in the dark, and an interactive segmenter that can be pointed at one thing, here the hand.

  • People and the toy stay sharp, are pushed forward in depth, and get a soft cut-out of the real figure under the mist. Everything else is dimmed, desaturated and pushed back.
  • The toy is derived, not detected: the segmenter finds the fist and the toy as one object, so the lab subtracts the fist body and what sticks out (the head and the legs) is tagged HORSE.
  • Limits: small distant figures are missed; very dark frames needed local contrast and a restricted search area to avoid false figures.

Files: make_masks.py, masks.npz

02Cutting to the score

The track has a clear skeleton: a main pulse about every 2.83 seconds, the bass dropping out at 0:36.7, returning on the hardest hit at 0:53.66, and the drone cutting at 1:33.2 into a held tone. Every cut is on that grid.

  • 0–36 s faces first, the boy at the fire, the others, the face the fire lights on the strong hit at 19.8 s.
  • 36.7–53.7 s the void: the burial of the horse in ash, slowed, with the boy's face between its halves.
  • 53.66 s the hand over the flames, on the downbeat.
  • 53.7–90 s assembly, cut on the bars.
  • 90–100 s the hand and the horse before the drone cuts, then a near-freeze under the held tone.

03Sound

The film's own audio goes through the same edit as the picture: each beat's sound is stretched to its slot with pitch kept, slowed beats are washed in a short reverb so anything spoken dissolves where the picture does, and the whole sits about 7 LU under the track. The mix peaks at −1.5 dB.

Not verified by ear: no one involved in building or reviewing this could listen, so whether any speech is intelligible in the mix is unknown.

File: build_audio.py

04Steadying the picture

An earlier cut flickered. The cause was a 12 Hz pop: the picture is built from source frames at 12 per second and each new frame entered at full weight before decaying. The fix samples time continuously, ties the point noise to its source frame, fixes exposure per beat and eases the note pulses. Energy at 12 Hz on the same stretches fell from 3.8, 7.2 and 2.5 to 0.5, 0.4 and 0.04.

File: flicker_check.py

05The console frame

The picture is a point volume in the manner of AFL-07: every frame is a sheet of points that keeps its moment, the newest faces the camera and the past recedes into mist. The glass around it builds each beat: a scan sweep, an outline drawn around each person, then the room dissolving behind. The hand shot is reframed with a zoom and pan and cut out from full-size source frames so the toy has detail.

Files: render_focus.py (final), render_ui.py, render_edl.py, render.py (earlier versions)

06Independent review

Four fresh critics, each given only the video and a bar, reviewed successive versions. None could listen.

1Too many beats with no person; interface cluttered; tracker and labels unreadable at phone size. Flicker passed.
2Subject and flicker passed. The loudest hit landed on a fire texture, not a clear subject; stage words meaningless.
3The toy did not read as a horse; a repetitive loop at the end; scan sweeps were noise.
4Much improved; flicker passes; sound and picture broadly line up. Still: the toy is unreadable without its label, about 30 s of dark low-information picture, and the HORSE tag is small on a phone.

The bar is not fully met. The toy is mostly hidden inside the fist in the source footage, so no render can show a clear horse profile from it.

07What this cannot do
  • The horse is outlined and tagged, but is not recognisable as a horse without its label.
  • Roughly 30 seconds, mainly the dark void and the running figures, stay low in information.
  • Phone legibility was checked only by downscaling, not on a device.
  • The picture is derived from a copyrighted film and the sound is a supplied track plus the film's own audio. Rights have not been cleared.

P2The critic's brief (verbatim)

Each critic was a fresh agent given this brief and nothing else: none of the builder's reasoning, measurements or earlier verdicts. This is the final version (earlier ones differed only in not asking about the toy horse); the file paths are replaced by placeholders.

You are an independent critic. You have no history with this piece. Judge only what you can see and hear by inspecting the actual file.

FILE: [candidate video]  (100 s, 1280x720, with sound). Put your working files in [a scratch folder]. Do not read any other file in the parent folders, and do not read code or notes.

CONTEXT: A director asked for a short piece that renders a memory as a mist, cut to music. Their feedback on an earlier version was: "too hard to watch and understand; pull more focus on humans and forms; the other stuff reads as clutter." They will watch it mostly on a PHONE.

HOW TO WATCH (use ffmpeg and the Read tool to view images):
1. Normal-speed first: pull frames every 1.5 s across the whole piece at 640 px wide and view them in order as contact sheets (e.g. 3x4 per sheet). Say what you think the piece is about from the images alone.
2. Phone test: re-view a dozen frames scaled to 360 px wide. Can the on-screen words still be read? Can you tell what is a person, and what the held object is?
3. Around cuts and the strongest moments, and through the final 12 seconds, pull frames every 0.5 s.
4. Flicker/strobe: pull 24 consecutive frames (ffmpeg -ss T -t 1 -vf fps=24) from two steady passages and judge whether brightness or texture pulses.
5. Sound: use ffmpeg ebur128 or astats to report loudness over time, and a spectrogram image (showspectrumpic) to say what you can see about structure. You cannot listen; do not claim you heard anything. Say what the picture does at the strongest audio events (list the times).

RETURN, in under 450 words:
- Verdict on each as PASS / REPAIR / INCONCLUSIVE: (a) a first-time viewer can tell who and what the piece is about; (b) humans and forms are clearly the focus and the rest is not clutter; (c) on-screen words are legible at phone size and the interface helps rather than adds noise; (d) no flicker or strobe; (e) picture and sound events line up (as far as you can check without listening); (f) the held object near the end reads as a toy horse (say what you can actually make out of it without the label).
- Evidence for each: exact timestamps.
- The 3 most consequential remaining weaknesses, each with timestamps and the observable change that would close it.
- The strongest moments (timestamps).
- Anything you could not check.
Be specific and blunt. Do not soften a verdict to be polite.

The method behind the review loop: a builder never awards its own pass, critics run the work before reading the design, repairs target causes, and every claim is classified as measured, structural, judged or unknown.
