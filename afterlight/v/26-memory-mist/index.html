Afterlight / AFL-26

Experiment I · What Light Leaves · AFL-26 · 1:40 · with sound

Memory mist

“Anything real should be a mist.”Dr. Ana Stelline, Blade Runner 2049 featurette · a memory is recalled feeling, not accumulated detail

The idea

If a real memory is feeling and not detail, a memory should be rendered by what it keeps and what it lets go. The scene of K's wooden horse (2:07–3:06 of the featurette) is cut into fifteen beats. Each is rebuilt, as in AFL-07, as a sheet of points that keeps the moment it was filmed: the newest frame faces the camera, earlier frames recede behind it into mist, and anything that moved leaves a body of light through time. Each beat has a mist value, which is how much detail it is allowed to keep. Corridors and pursuers go to 0.65–0.7 and dissolve. The hand over the flames and the burial of the horse in ash go to 0.12–0.2 and stay dense, because they are the feeling.

Cut to the music

The score is Modular Tonal Lego (Treblo), the file supplied for this piece. It has a clear skeleton, and the cut follows it. A main pulse falls about every 2.83 s, so every cut lands on a bar. The bass drops out at 36.7 s and comes back on a hard downbeat at 53.66 s. The drone cuts at 93.2 s and leaves a single held tone.

  • 0–36 s · faces first. The piece opens on the boy against the fire, then a child running, the others, and the face the fire lights, which lands on the strong hit at 19.8 s.
  • 36.7–53.7 s · the void. With the bass gone, the burial of the horse in ash plays slowed across the hollow, with the boy's face between its two halves. The crouched child shows as a pale silhouette in the dark.
  • 53.66 s · the hand. The bass returns on the downbeat and the frame cuts to the hand over the flames. The fire is turned down so the fist and the toy are what you see, and the horse is tagged.
  • 53.7–90 s · assembly. The others, the face, the boy turning, the others again, cut on the bars and on the loud plucks.
  • 90–100 s · the hand and the held tone. The frame comes in on the hand and horse before the drone cuts at 93.2 s, then slows to a near-freeze under the held tone and fades.

Every pluck is also a pulse in the picture: for a moment the mist thins, the detail sharpens and the camera pushes in, and then the mist takes it back. Each note rebuilds the memory a little and loses it again, which is the idea of a memory as recalled feeling.

People first

An earlier version rendered everything with equal weight and was hard to read. This one separates the people and the held toy from the room. Each frame is segmented (MediaPipe selfie-multiclass for face, hair, body and clothes, DeepLab for people in the dark, and an interactive segmenter for the hand), and the masks decide what the memory keeps. People and the toy stay sharp, pushed forward in depth, with a soft cut-out of the real figure under the mist. Everything else is dimmed, desaturated and pushed back. The mask files are in the source folder.

The held toy is found by segmenting the whole fist and subtracting the fist body: what sticks out of it, the head and the legs, is the horse. The segmenter cannot separate toy from hand by itself, so this is derived, not detected, and the HORSE tag marks the largest piece of it.

Seen through the instrument

The memory is seen through a recall console, but a quiet one. Each beat is built on screen: a scan line sweeps down, the outline of each person is drawn around them, then the room dissolves behind. A plain title names every beat. There is no other text, and the outlines stay inside the glass. On every pluck of the music the outlines glow and the mist lifts a little.

Independent review

Three fresh critics, each given only the video and a bar, reviewed successive versions. The first found too many empty beats and a cluttered interface. The second passed the subject and flicker and found the loudest hit landing on a fire texture. Both findings were repaired: every beat now has a face, a figure or the hand, the stage words were removed, and the hand and horse are lit and tagged so they read. The last version's verdict is in the pull request.

The sound

The mix is the supplied score under the featurette's own audio (its score, fire and anything spoken over them). The film's sound goes through the same cut list as the picture: each beat's audio is stretched to its slot, pitch kept, and the slowed beats are washed in a short reverb so the voice dissolves into mist where the picture does. The film's sound sits about 7 LU under the music. Where a beat runs near real speed, whatever the featurette carries there (score, fire, any voice-over) plays close to as recorded; I have not been able to confirm by ear that speech is intelligible anywhere in the mix.

The flicker

An earlier scored cut flickered. The cause was a 12 Hz pop: the picture is built from source frames at 12 per second, and each new frame entered at full weight before decaying. The fix samples time continuously, ties the point noise to its source frame, fixes exposure per beat, and eases the note pulses in and out. Energy at 12 Hz fell from 3.8, 7.2 and 2.5 to 0.5, 0.4 and 0.04 on the same stretches (flicker_check.py).

The horse

The wooden horse is the payoff, so the last ten seconds are given to it. From 90.3 s the frame is reframed on the hand: a zoom and pan fill it with the fist and the toy, cut out from the full-size source frames, with the fire turned down behind. The toy's head and legs are outlined and tagged HORSE. On the drone cut at 93.2 s the picture slows to a near-freeze on it and fades with the track. It is still a small carved toy in a child's hand seen through mist, and the tag is what names it; the segmenter cannot split it from the fist, so the outline is derived by subtracting the fist body. In the burial (36.7–50.8 s) the horse is not visible in the dark footage; the crouched child is.

Segments and precursors

Frame beats (F) are the telling: Ana and K on the couch. Memory beats (M) are the rendered memory. Each has the numbers to distil a memory precursor from: share of fire-warm pixels, motion energy, and a three-colour palette.
