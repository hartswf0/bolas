Build log · how the lab made this recollection

01The story, in order, with all the footage

The previous cut rearranged the memory to the music and repeated its faces. This one tells it straight. The telling (Ana and K, from the featurette), then every shot of the memory in the order the film shows it, then the return to K and Ana, then the horse held once more. The memory's footage is all there; the interview is trimmed to the telling and the return. Shots are only sped or slowed a little to land on the music's bars, except the hand shot, which is slowed to a near-freeze on purpose.

The music's three events fall on story events: the bass drops at 0:36.7 as the child runs for the fire and the burial begins; it returns at 0:53.66 as the others find him; the drone cuts at 1:33 as the telling ends and the horse is shown.

02The horse, drawn two ways

In the footage the toy is mostly hidden inside the child's fist: only a small head and two legs show. No render can show a clear horse from that, so the lab draws one, and says so. Both ways use the same drawing: a wooden toy horse built from polygons and animated by hand in code (it nods, its legs swing, and at the end it lifts out of the fist into a clear profile). It is not the film's toy. They differ in the language it is drawn in.

The same two languages carry the scenes and events (the place, the child running, the door of light, the burial) in each cut. The faces stay in mist in both. Switch between them under the screen.

Files: horse_styles.py, m07_masks.py, m07.npz

03What is observed and what is generated

Observed: every picture is an unaltered frame of the featurette, 1:49 to about 3:26 of the supplied video. Where noted it is slowed (frame-blended), time-remapped to the bars, or cropped (the hand shot is a zoomed window). The intake shots are only graded and letterboxed.

Generated: the mist (a point volume), the cut-paper tones and shadows, the engraving and its hatching, the paper cards and pin, the outlines, the console and its title, and the drawn horse and its motion. All of it is code. The masks that find people, the fist and the toy come from three segmentation models (MediaPipe selfie-multiclass, DeepLab v3, and an interactive segmenter), used only to analyse frames. No image or video generation model was used.

Not observed: the horse in the two drawn passages. The toy's visible head and legs are real; the rest is drawn.

04Finding the people

Every frame of the memory was segmented so the lab knew who was in it: a multiclass selfie model (face, hair, body, clothes), a general person model that still finds figures in the dark, and an interactive segmenter pointed at the hand. The faces stay sharp in mist; the hand shot also has a full-resolution mask of the fist and of what sticks out of it (derived by subtracting the fist body, because the segmenter finds fist and toy as one object).

Limits: small distant figures are missed; very dark frames needed a restricted search area to avoid false figures.

Files: make_masks.py, masks.npz

05Sound, in full

The film's own audio goes through the same edit as the picture: in the telling and the return, the voices lead and the music steps back 8 dB; in the memory, the music leads and the film's sound sits about 7 LU under it. Shots that run near real speed are left untouched; others are stretched with pitch kept, and slowed ones are washed in a short reverb.

Not verified by ear: no one involved in building or reviewing this could listen, so how intelligible the voices are in the mix is unknown.

File: build_audio2.py

06Steadying the picture

An earlier cut flickered. The cause was a 12 Hz pop: the picture is built from source frames at 12 per second and each new frame entered at full weight before decaying. The fix samples time continuously, ties the point noise to its source frame, fixes exposure per beat and eases the note pulses. Energy at 12 Hz on the same stretches fell from 3.8, 7.2 and 2.5 to 0.5, 0.4 and 0.04.

File: flicker_check.py

07Independent review

Fresh critics, each given only the video and a bar, reviewed successive versions. None could listen.

The dark scene beats and the buried scene did not read, the toy in the fist looked like a stool or a dog, the bottom tag was jargon and tiny on a phone, and the four looks (live, paper or ink, mist, drawn horse) felt like a patchwork. Flicker passed in both.
the horse passage, blind comparison (two critics, labels swapped)The two critics chose opposite ways overall. They agreed on the facts. Silhouette: bold and designed, but the horse and the fist were one black mass for the first six seconds. Card: the horse separated from the hand throughout, but its striped wood fill shimmered like a processing artefact and the hand edges were ragged. The on-screen caption named the style, so the test was not fully blind.
after repair: silhouette cutFlicker passes. The held horse (25-33 s and 93-100 s) reads as a horse. The faces and the silent burial are strong. Still: the story is carried by captions, 9-18 s reads as abstract, the buried horse is tiny and upside-down, the two people never appear together again, and the four looks join with hard cuts.
after repair: card cutFlicker passes. The paper horse reads as a horse in the hand and at the end; the faces at 54-69 s are the clearest images. Still: the burial and 13-19 s and 31-36 s are near-black, the buried horse is upside-down and reads as a table without its label, there are too many caption changes, and the piece ends on an object, not on the two people together.

Neither way won. The silhouette is the cleaner image and the card the clearer object, so the page lets you switch between them and keeps your place.

08What this cannot do
  • The horse in the footage is a small head and two legs. The lab draws a horse so it can be seen, and says so; it is not the film's toy.
  • Phone legibility was checked by downscaling, not on a device.
  • The sound has not been heard by anyone building or reviewing this.
  • The picture is derived from a copyrighted film and the sound is a supplied track plus the film's own audio. Rights have not been cleared.

P2The critic's brief (verbatim)

Each critic was a fresh agent given this brief and nothing else. This is the version used for the earlier cuts; file paths are replaced by placeholders. The later reviews used the same brief with the story described in a line.

You are an independent critic. You have no history with this piece. Judge only what you can see and hear by inspecting the actual file.

FILE: [candidate video]  (100 s, 1280x720, with sound). Put your working files in [a scratch folder]. Do not read any other file in the parent folders, and do not read code or notes.

CONTEXT: A director asked for a short piece that renders a memory as a mist, cut to music. Their feedback on an earlier version was: "too hard to watch and understand; pull more focus on humans and forms; the other stuff reads as clutter." They will watch it mostly on a PHONE.

HOW TO WATCH (use ffmpeg and the Read tool to view images):
1. Normal-speed first: pull frames every 1.5 s across the whole piece at 640 px wide and view them in order as contact sheets (e.g. 3x4 per sheet). Say what you think the piece is about from the images alone.
2. Phone test: re-view a dozen frames scaled to 360 px wide. Can the on-screen words still be read? Can you tell what is a person, and what the held object is?
3. Around cuts and the strongest moments, and through the final 12 seconds, pull frames every 0.5 s.
4. Flicker/strobe: pull 24 consecutive frames (ffmpeg -ss T -t 1 -vf fps=24) from two steady passages and judge whether brightness or texture pulses.
5. Sound: use ffmpeg ebur128 or astats to report loudness over time, and a spectrogram image (showspectrumpic) to say what you can see about structure. You cannot listen; do not claim you heard anything. Say what the picture does at the strongest audio events (list the times).

RETURN, in under 450 words:
- Verdict on each as PASS / REPAIR / INCONCLUSIVE: (a) a first-time viewer can tell who and what the piece is about; (b) humans and forms are clearly the focus and the rest is not clutter; (c) on-screen words are legible at phone size and the interface helps rather than adds noise; (d) no flicker or strobe; (e) picture and sound events line up (as far as you can check without listening); (f) the held object near the end reads as a toy horse (say what you can actually make out of it without the label).
- Evidence for each: exact timestamps.
- The 3 most consequential remaining weaknesses, each with timestamps and the observable change that would close it.
- The strongest moments (timestamps).
- Anything you could not check.
Be specific and blunt. Do not soften a verdict to be polite.
P3System instruction: SCION agent-first architecture

The method behind the review loop: a builder never awards its own pass, critics run the work before reading the design, repairs target causes, and every claim is classified as measured, structural, judged or unknown.

Read the full instruction. The other SCION prompts are in docs/prompts.
