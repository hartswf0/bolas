Memory mist

“Anything real should be a mist.”Dr. Ana Stelline, Blade Runner 2049 featurette · a memory is recalled feeling, not accumulated detail

The idea

If a real memory is feeling and not detail, a memory should be rendered by what it keeps and what it lets go. The scene of K's wooden horse (2:07–3:06 of the featurette) is cut into fifteen beats. Each is rebuilt, as in AFL-07, as a sheet of points that keeps the moment it was filmed: the newest frame faces the camera, earlier frames recede behind it into mist, and anything that moved leaves a body of light through time. Each beat has a mist value, which is how much detail it is allowed to keep. Corridors and pursuers go to 0.65–0.7 and dissolve. The hand over the flames and the burial of the horse in ash go to 0.12–0.2 and stay dense, because they are the feeling.

The horse, twice

  • M07 · 2:27.3–2:28.9: a hand over the furnace flames holds the wooden horse. The shortest beat is the hottest one. Exposure is fixed so the fire cannot swallow the hand, 34 layers of past stack behind it, and the camera orbits ±22° so the hand and what it holds read as a volume.
  • M10 · 2:36–2:48: the child buries it in the ash. The longest beat, with the least light. The frame is lifted until the ash reads as dust. The thrown ash is the moving mass, so its trail of light is the memory of the gesture.

Between them the child runs, the fire answers, and other children look on: place, chase, threat, feeling, others. The wooden horse is not outlined as an object. It is held by time and exposure on those two beats, and the next step is to track it with SAM 2 on the M07 and M10 frames and give it its own point layer.
