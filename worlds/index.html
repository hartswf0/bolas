The wall above is the reference board. Every tile below it is a still taken from the running page; touch one to go in.

52 worlds.
One orchestra.

Each world is a different answer to the same question: what does it look like when every jellyfish is one sound? Some are places, some are ways of seeing, and twelve are paints and prints.

PAINTPaint and print

Paint. On concrete: a stencil, a three-colour poster and a spray can. On film: running dye, and ink still wet. On paper: watercolour and black ink wash. On canvas: thick paint. And four prints: woodblock, cyanotype, risograph, glass.

STENCILthe street: two cut sheets and a can, on a rolled cream groundTwo sheets only: black for whatever is lit, red for whatever is brightest.CUT POSTERthe street: three flat colours, red on top, pasted to a wallThree flat colours. Nothing is shaded; things are one colour or the next.INK ON FILMpaint: dye on clear film, in bands that run along the strip and step frame by frameWhatever is lit stains the film. The stain runs along the strip in bands, and each band holds a few frames and then steps.SPRAY WALLthe street: the orchestra sprayed on concrete in its own coloursEverything lit is sprayed, with mist round it, and the heavy marks run.WET INKpaint: ink still wet and out of focus, pulled along the filmNothing has an edge. Every animal is a bloom of its own colour, and blooms that meet make a third colour.WATERCOLOURpaint: a pencil drawing, then washes on cold-pressed paperPencil first, then water. A wash is strongest at its edge, where the pigment is left when it dries.INK WASHpaint: black ink in three dilutions on fibrous paper, and one red sealOne colour, three dilutions. Loud is black, quiet is grey, and where the brush runs dry the paper shows.THICK PAINTpaint: loaded brush dabs laid along the way things moveEvery mark is one dab of thick paint, laid in the direction things are moving.WOODBLOCKprint: indigo, slate and rust blocks on cream, with the grain of the woodFour blocks: indigo for the dark, slate for the half-lit, bare paper for the lit, rust for the brightest. Every flat carries the grain.CYANOTYPEprint: Prussian blue paper, white where the thing layWhatever lies on the paper stays white. The longer it stays, the whiter.RISOGRAPHprint: pink, yellow and blue drums and black, out of register, in a grain of dotsThree drums and black, each a little out of register. Warm things print pink and yellow, cool things blue.GLASSprint: everything is glass; only edges and thickness showEverything is glass. You see edges, and thickness, and whatever is behind.

NEXTHow these evolve

Each world is one rule with one test. A world changes when its test fails, not when a new filter is available. What was tried and withdrawn is part of the record.

REFThe brief
